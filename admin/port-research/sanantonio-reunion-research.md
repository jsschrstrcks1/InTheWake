# PART 1 — SAN ANTONIO, CHILE

## 1. The berth and what this port actually is

**What the port is.** San Antonio is Chile's largest port by container throughput and the country's principal general-cargo gateway. Per ECLAC/CEPAL's port ranking, San Antonio moved 1,407,869 TEU in 2023 against Valparaíso's 788,970 TEU — San Antonio is first in Chile, Valparaíso a distant second ([PortalPortuario, CEPAL ranking](https://portalportuario.cl/san-antonio-se-ubica-como-el-puerto-chileno-de-mayor-movimiento-de-contenedores-segun-cepal/); [Empresa Océano](https://www.empresaoceano.cl/puertos/san-antonio/ranking-cepal-puerto-san-antonio-se-mantiene-como-el-primer-puerto-de)). This is not a cruise port that also does cargo. It is a heavy industrial cargo port that takes cruise ships.

**Governance.** The landlord authority is **Empresa Portuaria San Antonio (EPSA / Puerto San Antonio)**, a state port company ([puertosanantonio.com](https://www.puertosanantonio.com/); [AIVP member listing](https://www.aivp.org/es/la-aivp/nuestros-miembros/directorio/empresa-portuaria-san-antonio/)). Terminals are concessioned: **San Antonio Terminal Internacional (STI)** and **Puerto Central (PCE)**, now branded **DP World San Antonio** ([DP World San Antonio](https://www.dpworld.com/en/ports-terminals/chile/san-antonio)).

**Where cruise ships berth.** Cruise ships go **alongside — no tendering** — inside the commercial port complex, at the **Puerto Central / DP World (PCE)** berths ([CruiseSheet port page](https://cruisesheet.com/port/san-antonio)). Puerto San Antonio's own history records that cruise calls began in **April 2017**, when *Norwegian Sun* berthed at Puerto Central after her Valparaíso call could not be performed; since then more than 430,000 passengers and crew have come ashore here ([Puerto San Antonio — Cruceros](https://www.puertosanantonio.com/cruceros)).

Note which berths cruise ships **cannot** use: **Sitio 9 / Terminal Policarpo Toro** is limited to vessels of 190 m LOA, 32.22 m beam, 30,000 t displacement; **Sitio 8 / Terminal Norte (Puerto Panul)** is a dry-bulk grain berth (~12.2 m draft) ([Puerto de San Antonio, Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_de_San_Antonio)). Volendam at 237 m is well over the Policarpo Toro limit — she will be on the DP World / STI quays.

**Does Volendam fit? Yes, comfortably.** STI's three docks give 930 m of combined quay at up to **14 m maximum draft** ([CruiseMapper San Antonio](https://www.cruisemapper.com/ports/san-antonio-port-9465)); DP World's multipurpose facility has a **700 m continuous berth** with deep-water access ([DP World San Antonio terminal overview](https://www.dpworld.com/es/san-antonio/passenger-terminal/terminal-overview)). Volendam needs 237 m of quay and 8.1 m of water. There is no dimensional constraint here at all.

**The passenger terminal.** Holland America's own port page gives the facility as **"Terminal De Pasajeros, Alan Macowan 240, San Antonio"** ([HAL San Antonio (Santiago) port details](https://www.hollandamerica.com/en/us/plan-a-cruise/get-ready-for-your-cruise/cruise-ports-and-hotels/san-antionio-santiago-chile)). Chilean sources describe the terminal as roughly **3,600 m² located ~250 m from the berth**, with customs and SAG (agriculture/livestock inspection) control facilities ([search-surfaced description of the DP World passenger terminal](https://www.dpworld.com/es/san-antonio/passenger-terminal)). HAL also states plainly: **no public parking at the pier, no luggage storage at the pier** (same HAL page).

**Can you walk out? Effectively, no.** The terminal sits inside an active container yard, and pedestrians are not permitted to cross the container port ([CruiseSheet](https://cruisesheet.com/port/san-antonio); [Cruise Stories, San Antonio port report, 7 Mar 2025](https://mycruisestories.com/2025/03/07/san-antonio-chile-cruise-port/)). Everyone leaves by vehicle — ship shuttle, ship excursion coach, or taxi from the terminal forecourt. The terminal is around **3 km (2 mi) from San Antonio's main square** ([CruiseSheet](https://cruisesheet.com/port/san-antonio)).

**Shuttles.** Cruise lines typically run a shuttle from the terminal to a drop-off point in town, and taxis and pre-booked coaches wait at the terminal ([CruiseSheet](https://cruisesheet.com/port/san-antonio)). Independent reports also describe minibus shuttling to the San Antonio bus terminal, from which intercity buses run ([Cruise Stories](https://mycruisestories.com/2025/03/07/san-antonio-chile-cruise-port/)). **Treat the specific shuttle arrangement as ship- and call-specific and confirm on board** — reports disagree about whether the bus terminal is a 10-minute walk or requires a shuttle, and both cannot be true given the port-walk prohibition. Marked UNVERIFIED below.

## 2. Why ships call here rather than Valparaíso

The two ports are 90 km apart on the same stretch of central Chilean coast and both serve Santiago. Historically Valparaíso was *the* passenger port — it is the one with the UNESCO historic quarter and the cruise-ship romance. The shift is recent and has two drivers:

1. **Cargo gravity moved south.** San Antonio overtook Valparaíso as Chile's main freight port in the 21st century ([San Antonio, Chile — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio,_Chile)), and the CEPAL numbers above show the gap is now close to 2:1 in TEU. More quay, more depth, more investment.
2. **Berth availability and labour reliability at Valparaíso.** The commonly stated reason cruise lines migrated is berthing conflicts and threatened labour disruption at Valparaíso ([search-surfaced summary citing San Antonio port guides](https://www.whatsinport.com/San-Antonio.htm)). The origin story of cruise calls at San Antonio supports this: the first call, *Norwegian Sun* in April 2017, happened precisely **because her scheduled Valparaíso call could not be performed** ([Puerto San Antonio](https://www.puertosanantonio.com/cruceros)). What began as a diversion became the default.

**Is San Antonio purely a Santiago gateway?** Overwhelmingly it is marketed that way — Holland America itself brands the port "San Antonio (Santiago), Chile." But that framing is a mistake for this particular port, and section 6 is the reason: **Isla Negra is 31 km away and Santiago is 110 km away.** The single most rewarding thing within reach of this berth is not in Santiago. San Antonio town itself has a modest but real offer — the 600 m **Paseo Bellamar** promenade, sea lions on the rocks by the **Grúa 82** national monument at the fishing cove, the **Humedal del Río Maipo** wetland (~140 ha, 132 bird species, an Important Bird Area since 2010), **Playa de Llolleo**, and the **MUSA** natural-science museum overlooking the river mouth ([Portal Turismo San Antonio](https://turismo.sanantonio.cl/visite/atractivos/item/230-boca-rio-maipo); [Litoral de los Poetas / Chile es Tuyo](https://chileestuyo.cl/destino/litoral-de-los-poetas/); [Playa de Llolleo, Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_de_Llolleo)). None of that is a reason to skip Isla Negra, but it means the town is not the void that cruise guides imply.

## 4. Santiago on a port day

**The numbers.** ~110 km / 68 mi each way; **1.5–2 hours each way** depending on traffic ([iSouthAmerica](https://www.isouthamerica.com/how-to-get-to-san-antonio-cruise-port/)). Holland America's own airport transfer figure — 106 km, **120 minutes** — is the more conservative and probably the more realistic planning number, because it reflects the reality of Ruta 78 into the capital ([HAL port page](https://www.hollandamerica.com/en/us/plan-a-cruise/get-ready-for-your-cruise/cruise-ports-and-hotels/san-antionio-santiago-chile)).

**The honest read.** Four hours of driving out of a port day. On a typical 08:00–18:00 call with an all-aboard at 17:30, that leaves roughly **five to six hours in Santiago** — enough for a compressed highlights loop, not enough for a considered day. The risk is not the outbound leg; it is the return. Ruta 78 and the Santiago approaches are congestion-prone, and a single incident turns a 2-hour return into 3. **On an independent basis this is a genuine miss-the-ship risk.** The mitigations, in order of safety: (a) a ship's excursion — the ship waits; (b) a reputable private transfer company with a written return-by time; (c) don't go. Independent taxi or bus, with no accountability for the return, is the version that strands people.

**What is actually worth seeing, ranked for a compressed day:**

- **Plaza de Armas** — the colonial core, cathedral, the Correo Central. Free, always open, the natural start.
- **Mercado Central** — the 19th-century wrought-iron fish market. Best mid-morning to early afternoon. Loud, touristy at the centre, better at the edges.
- **Barrio Lastarria** — the walkable, café-and-bookshop quarter below Cerro Santa Lucía. The single most pleasant hour in central Santiago.
- **Cerro San Cristóbal** — the city's hill park, reached by **funicular** from the Pío Nono entrance in Bellavista (Tue–Sun 10:00–18:45 last descent; Mondays 13:00–18:45; **closed the first Monday of each month for maintenance**) or by **teleférico/cable car** from Estación Oasis, Av. El Cerro 750, Providencia (summer hours Tue–Sun 10:00–19:45) ([Funicular Santiago](https://funicularonline.cl/); [Teleférico Santiago](https://telefericosantiago.cl/); [chile.travel](https://chile.travel/diario-de-viajes/teleferico-y-funicular-en-santiago-muevete-en-el-cerro-san-cristobal-por-los-aires/)). Note the Monday closures — a January–March world-cruise call could easily land on one.
- **La Chascona** — Neruda's Santiago house, Fernando Márquez de la Plata 0192, Providencia; Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00 (to 19:00 in January–February); general admission **CLP 11,000**, reduced **CLP 4,500** (students under 17, accredited students, over-60s); no reservation, first-come first-served; audioguides in Spanish, English, Portuguese, French, German, Italian ([Fundación Pablo Neruda — Informaciones](https://fundacionneruda.org/informaciones/)).
- **Museo de la Memoria y los Derechos Humanos** — Matucana 501; **Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00, last entry 17:30, free admission** ([Museo de la Memoria — Visítanos](https://mmdh.cl/visitanos/); [Registro de Museos de Chile](https://www.registromuseoschile.cl/663/w3-article-50857.html)). This is the most substantial and the most demanding of the six. It is also the one that makes the trip to Santiago mean something rather than tick something. If you go to Santiago, give it 90 minutes minimum. **Note it is closed Mondays** — as are La Chascona and most Chilean museums.

**A hard scheduling point for a Jan–Mar call:** three of the six (La Chascona, Museo de la Memoria, and Neruda's other houses) are **closed Mondays**. Check the call's day of week before committing to a Santiago plan built around them.

## 5. Valparaíso and Viña del Mar

**Distance and time from San Antonio:** Valparaíso ~90 km / 56 mi, **~1 h 15 min** ([Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Valpara%C3%ADso/San-Antonio-Chile)). Viña del Mar is ~10 minutes further north along the coast. Round trip is ~2.5 hours of driving — meaningfully less exposed than Santiago and, for most people, a better-value day.

**The UNESCO inscription — exactly what is listed.** The **Historic Quarter of the Seaport City of Valparaíso** was inscribed on the World Heritage List in **2003** under **criterion (iii)** alone: Valparaíso as "an exceptional testimony to the early phase of globalisation in the late 19th century, when it became the leading merchant port on the sea routes of the Pacific coast of South America" ([UNESCO WHC #959](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/959/)). The inscribed property is **23.2 ha** with a **44.5 ha buffer zone** (67.7 ha total) ([UNESCO WHC #959](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/959/)). **This matters practically: the listed area is small.** It is the amphitheatre core — the port-side flats plus the lower slopes of Cerro Alegre and Cerro Concepción, with their funiculars, alleys and mansion architecture. It is *not* the whole city, and most of what visitors photograph as "Valparaíso" — the far hills, much of the street art — is outside the inscribed zone or in the buffer.

**The ascensores/funiculars — the number has changed, and most published guides are wrong.**
Nineteen ascensores were built between 1883 and 1916 ([Ascensores de Valparaíso, Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascensores_de_Valpara%C3%ADso)). As of a verification dated **22 May 2026**, **seven are operating and open to the general public: Barón, Concepción, Cordillera, El Peral, Reina Victoria, Espíritu Santo, and San Agustín** ([Apuntes y Viajes, 25 May 2026](https://apuntesyviajes.com/2026/05/25/ascensores-operativos-de-valparaiso-en-2026-cuales-estan-funcionando-y-como-recorrerlos/)). The specialist tracking site [ascensoresvalparaiso.org/estado/operativo](https://ascensoresvalparaiso.org/estado/operativo) lists broadly the same set, adds **Hospital Van Buren** (restricted institutional use, not a tourist ride), and flags **Espíritu Santo** as in *marcha blanca* (trial running) — so **"seven" is the optimistic count and "six for a visitor" is the safe one.**

Fares as of May 2026: **CLP 200 general; CLP 300 on Concepción; CLP 600 with a bicycle on Barón/Cordillera; CLP 1,000 for foreign tourists.** Hours: weekdays 07:00–21:30, weekends and holidays 08:00–21:30 ([Apuntes y Viajes, 25 May 2026](https://apuntesyviajes.com/2026/05/25/ascensores-operativos-de-valparaiso-en-2026-cuales-estan-funcionando-y-como-recorrerlos/)).

**The one that catches people out: Ascensor Artillería is CLOSED and has been since April 2021.** It failed on a key pin and has not run since. A 2025 restoration tender was declared void (*desierta*) — the only bid, CLP 12 bn, far exceeded the CLP 7 bn budget ([Epicentro Chile, 18 May 2025](https://www.epicentrochile.com/2025/05/18/ascensor-artilleria-sigue-cerrado-licitacion-fue-desierta/)). The MOP (Ministry of Public Works) opened a **new tender on 22 December 2025**, with more than CLP 6 bn allocated and, for the first time, the architectural restoration separated from the electromechanical works ([La Región Hoy, 25 Dec 2025](https://laregionhoy.cl/2025/12/25/ministerio-de-obras-publicas-abre-nueva-licitacion-para-restaurar-el-ascensor-artilleria/); [Meganoticias, 24 Dec 2025](https://www.meganoticias.cl/nacional/510201-ascensor-artilleria-valparaiso-vuelve-24-12-2025.html)). Award was scheduled for **30 July 2026 — today** — with the elevator potentially operational **in 2027** (same sources). **For a January–March 2028 call, Artillería may well be running again — but it is not certain, and it is definitely not running now.** **Ascensor Polanco** — the vertical-shaft one, architecturally the most unusual — is also out of service and under restoration/design through 2026 ([Apuntes y Viajes](https://apuntesyviajes.com/2026/05/25/ascensores-operativos-de-valparaiso-en-2026-cuales-estan-funcionando-y-como-recorrerlos/)).

**Street art.** Concentrated on Cerro Alegre and Cerro Concepción and along Calle Templeman, Pasaje Gálvez and the stairways between them. This is the most photographed thing in Chile and it is genuinely good, but it is also the part of the visit with the highest petty-crime exposure — see §8.

**La Sebastiana** — Neruda's Valparaíso house, Ricardo de Ferrari 692, Cerro Bellavista. Same regime as the other two houses: **Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00 (to 19:00 in Jan–Feb), CLP 11,000 general / CLP 4,500 reduced, no reservation needed, first-come first-served, audioguides in six languages** ([Fundación Pablo Neruda](https://fundacionneruda.org/informaciones/)). The view from the top floor is the best interior view in the city.

**Viña del Mar.** The beaches — Playa Acapulco, Playa El Sol, Reñaca further north — are broad, developed and cold (see §9: the Humboldt Current means the water is 14–17 °C in summer; people sunbathe, few swim for long).

**The Moai.** The **Museo de Arqueología e Historia Francisco Fonck**, 4 Norte 784 at 1 Oriente, Viña del Mar, has an **authentic moai from Rapa Nui, the only original one on the Chilean mainland**, standing outside the façade — brought over in **1951** ([Museo Fonck — Moai](https://www.museofonck.cl/new_site/index.php/moai.html); [Museo Fonck, Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Fonck)). The museum, founded 1937, holds one of the most complete Rapa Nui collections on the continent. Hours: **Mon 10:00–14:00 and 15:00–18:00; Tue–Sat 10:00–18:00; Sun and holidays 10:00–14:00**; free on the first Sunday of each month for Chileans and residents with ID ([Museo Fonck](https://www.museofonck.cl/new_site/)). The moai stands on the street side of the fence — you can see and photograph it without paying, which is what most cruise visitors do.

## 6. Isla Negra — probably the best answer for this port

**This is the section most guides get wrong by omission.** They treat San Antonio as a Santiago gateway and never mention that Pablo Neruda's most personal house is **31 km / ~30–35 minutes** from the berth ([CalcularRuta San Antonio → Isla Negra](https://www.calcularruta.com/de-san-antonio-valparaiso-a-isla-negra-beach.html); corroborated at 15 mi / ~33 min by [Distancias Entre](https://www.distanciasentre.com/cl/san-antonio-a-isla-negra-el-quisco-ruta/RutaHistoria/3471250.aspx)). Compare: Santiago is 110 km and 2 hours.

**Casa Museo Isla Negra**, Poeta Neruda s/n, Isla Negra, commune of El Quisco, Valparaíso Region. Tel +56 9 8538 0053 ([Fundación Pablo Neruda — Isla Negra](https://fundacionneruda.org/museos-casa-museo-isla-negra/)).

- **Hours:** Tuesday–Sunday **10:00–18:00**; extended to **19:00 in January and February** ([Fundación Pablo Neruda — Informaciones](https://fundacionneruda.org/informaciones/)). **Closed Mondays.**
- **Admission:** general **CLP 11,000**; reduced **CLP 4,500** for students under 17, accredited students, and over-60s ([same source](https://fundacionneruda.org/informaciones/)).
- **Booking:** **no reservation required for individuals** — entry is first-come, first-served. Group and school bookings go through the foundation. Audioguides in Spanish, English, Portuguese, French, German and Italian. Pets not permitted ([same source](https://fundacionneruda.org/informaciones/)).
- **The catch:** the houses have a **limited daily capacity** and, being first-come-first-served, **Isla Negra in particular can have long waits** ([Fundación Pablo Neruda](https://fundacionneruda.org/informaciones/)). Go early. A ship arriving at 08:00 and a car leaving the terminal at 08:30 puts you at the door near opening, which is exactly right.

**Why it is the smart choice.** Neruda built the house facing the Pacific, filled it with ships' figureheads, bottles, shells, narwhal tusks and masthead carvings, and he and Matilde Urrutia are buried in the garden overlooking the sea ([Casa de Isla Negra, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_de_Isla_Negra)). It is the house he loved most and the one that explains him. Against Santiago it wins on every axis that matters on a port day: 30 minutes instead of 120 each way, no motorway-congestion return risk, and it is a *singular* thing rather than a hurried sampler of a capital.

**A realistic full day from the berth:** Isla Negra at opening (2 hrs including travel and queue), back up the coast for lunch at a *caleta*, and either the San Antonio waterfront/Humedal or a Casablanca winery on the way back (§7) — all inside a 60 km radius of the ship. That is a better day than five hours in Santiago, and it carries a fraction of the risk.

## 7. Wine — Casablanca Valley and San Antonio Valley

**San Antonio Valley / Leyda.** The **Valle de San Antonio** is a wine subregion whose vineyards sit on rolling coastal hills roughly **4–12 km from the Pacific**; the **Leyda** DO within it covers the communes of San Antonio and Santo Domingo and was formalised as a designation of origin in **2002** (under the framework of Agriculture Decree No. 464 of 14 December 1994) ([Valle de Leyda, Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_de_Leyda); [Enoturismo Chile](https://www.enoturismochile.cl/valles/valle-de-san-antonio-leyda/)). Some Lo Abarca plantings are **4 km in a straight line from the sea** ([Enoturismo Chile](https://www.enoturismochile.cl/valles/valle-de-san-antonio-leyda/)). The style is cool-climate coastal: **Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay above all, plus Syrah and Pinot Noir** ([Enoturismo Chile](https://www.enoturismochile.cl/valles/valle-de-san-antonio-leyda/); [Chile es Tuyo](https://chileestuyo.cl/destino/valle-de-san-antonio-leyda/)).

**This is the valley named after the port you are standing in.** Viña Leyda is **40 km from Casablanca and 95 km from Santiago** ([Enoturismo Chile](https://www.enoturismochile.cl/valles/valle-de-san-antonio-leyda/)) — i.e. it is essentially on the port's doorstep, well inside a 30–40 minute drive.

**Casablanca Valley** lies immediately north of San Antonio Valley, on Ruta 68 between Santiago and Valparaíso, roughly **100 km west of Santiago** ([WineWiki San Antonio Valley](https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/san-antonio-valley/)). From San Antonio it is a detour of roughly 40–60 km depending on which winery — comfortably reachable, and it sits naturally on the route if you are also going to Valparaíso.

**Does a tasting work on a port day? Yes, easily — and better than at most South American ports.** San Antonio Valley/Leyda can be combined with Isla Negra in a half day. Casablanca combines naturally with a Valparaíso day. Both are far more feasible than fitting wine into a Santiago run. **Book ahead** — Chilean wineries generally require reserved tasting appointments rather than walk-ins, and I did not verify individual winery visiting hours (see UNVERIFIED).

## 8. Practicalities

**Currency.** The **Chilean peso (CLP)** is legal tender throughout Chile ([Turismo City guide](https://www.turismocity.com.pe/promociones_aereas/p-como_pagar_en_santiago_de_chile_y_cuanto_dinero_llevar_2r2-8913.html)). USD is not generally accepted outside hotels and some tour operators.

**Cards and ATMs.** Cards are widely accepted in cities. ATMs are branded **Redbanc** and are ubiquitous and secure; note that **Chilean banks generally levy their own commission on foreign-card withdrawals on top of whatever your home bank charges** ([Turismo City](https://www.turismocity.com.pe/promociones_aereas/p-como_pagar_en_santiago_de_chile_y_cuanto_dinero_llevar_2r2-8913.html)). Withdraw larger amounts less often.

**Tipping.** Chilean law requires restaurants, bars, cafés and similar table-service establishments to **suggest a 10% tip on the bill**, distributed in full to staff. It is **voluntary** and the customer may expressly refuse it; the employer must show the amount on the bill. The 10% suggestion applies only where staff serve tables — **not** at fast food or delivery ([BioBioChile, 28 Aug 2024](https://www.biobiochile.cl/noticias/servicios/toma-nota/2024/08/28/como-funciona-el-cobro-de-propinas-en-chile-o-quienes-pueden-recibirla-es-legal-pedir-el-10.shtml); [Nubox — Ley de propinas](https://blog.nubox.com/contadores/ley-de-propinas-en-chile)). Where a tip is paid by card, the employer has a maximum of **seven business days** to pass it on ([BioBioChile](https://www.biobiochile.cl/noticias/servicios/toma-nota/2024/08/28/como-funciona-el-cobro-de-propinas-en-chile-o-quienes-pueden-recibirla-es-legal-pedir-el-10.shtml)). Practical norm: pay the 10%; it is expected, and refusing it reads as a complaint about the service.

**Language.** Spanish. English is not widely spoken outside hotels, tourism and the professional classes. Chile scores **517, ranked 54th of 123, "moderate proficiency"** in the EF English Proficiency Index 2025 ([EF EPI Chile](https://www.ef.com/wwen/epi/regions/latin-america/chile/); [EF EPI 2025](https://www.ef.com/wwen/epi/)). Chilean Spanish is also notoriously fast and idiom-heavy even for fluent speakers. Carry the ship's name, berth and all-aboard time written down.

**Taxi and rideshare.** Uber is available and HAL's own port page says so explicitly ([HAL port page](https://www.hollandamerica.com/en/us/plan-a-cruise/get-ready-for-your-cruise/cruise-ports-and-hotels/san-antionio-santiago-chile)). The legal position has changed: **Law 21.553 — the "Ley EAT" / "Ley Uber"** — was enacted in **April 2023** to regulate transport-application companies (Uber, DiDi, Cabify, InDrive). Its implementing regulation was approved on **7 April 2025** and in force from **7 May 2025**; companies must register in the Subsecretaría de Transportes' national electronic registry, and drivers must hold professional licences and meet vehicle requirements ([Ley 21.553, BCN Chile](https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=1191380); [Docer — Ley EAT](https://docer.cl/blog/que-es-la-ley-eat-chile-empresas-aplicacion-transporte/); [Oficina Virtual, May 2026](https://www.oficinavirtual.cl/blog/2026/5/26/ley-uber-chile-requisitos-conductores-vehiculos)). Practically: rideshare works, is legal, and is now regulated. Metered taxis are fine; agree the route, and in Valparaíso prefer a taxi called by a restaurant or hotel over a street hail.

**Safety — specific, not vague.** The UK FCDO's Chile advice states plainly:
- *"Muggings happen frequently, especially in Santiago and Valparaiso, and increasingly with weapons such as guns and knives."*
- Distraction thefts — one person distracts, another takes the bag — are called out as occurring **"especially in central Santiago and Valparaiso."**
- Pickpocketing and theft are *"very common throughout Chile."*
- Drink spiking is reported particularly in the **Suecia and Bellavista** areas of Santiago.
([FCDO Chile — Safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/chile/safety-and-security))

**Valparaíso's reputation is earned but geographically specific.** The tourist core — **Cerro Alegre and Cerro Concepción** — is the safest part. Risk rises as you descend to the lower city and rises sharply in the **port area and surrounding streets, especially after dark** ([My Chile Travel Guide — Is Valparaíso safe?, 2026](https://mychiletravelguide.com/is-valparaiso-safe/)). For a cruise day the practical rules are: stay on the two named hills and the connecting funiculars; do not carry a visible camera bag down into the Plaza Sotomayor / port streets; do not wander off the painted stairways looking for "more murals"; do not take valuables. A daytime visit to the inscribed quarter is normal and safe if you stay in it.

**Agricultural / customs restrictions — this is strictly enforced and it applies to cruise passengers.**
Chile's **SAG (Servicio Agrícola y Ganadero)** requires **everyone over 18 entering Chile to complete a sworn declaration (Declaración Jurada)** stating whether they carry products of plant, animal, biological or chemical origin. It can be completed digitally up to a day before travel at **ingresoachile.cl**, or on paper, and must be presented to the SAG inspector at the point of entry ([SAG — Declaración Jurada de ingreso a Chile](https://www.sag.gob.cl/ambitos-de-accion/declaracion-jurada-sag-de-ingreso-chile); [SAG — trámite](https://www.sag.gob.cl/tramites/declaracion-jurada-sag-sobre-ingreso-de-productos-de-origen-vegetal-o-animal-chile)).
**Prohibited:** products of animal and plant origin — fruit, seeds, plants, flowers, honey, and **handicrafts made from natural materials such as wood** ([SAG](https://www.sag.gob.cl/ambitos-de-accion/ingreso-chile)).
**Penalties are real, not theoretical:** SAG has publicised fining a single passenger **30 UTM** for illegally introducing products ([SAG press release](https://www.sag.gob.cl/noticias/sag-multo-pasajero-con-30-utm-por-ingreso-ilegal-de-productos)). A false declaration is itself sanctionable ([SAG](https://www.sag.gob.cl/ambitos-de-accion/declaracion-jurada-sag-de-ingreso-chile)).
**Practical implications aboard:** do not take fruit off the ship, and do not take fruit or plant material back aboard from ashore — the ship will have its own restrictions on the return leg for the same reasons, and the San Antonio passenger terminal has SAG facilities precisely for this. Wooden souvenirs and craft items are a genuine risk category people do not expect.

**The coast-versus-Santiago difference, which is the thing that catches people out.** In January the coastal mean is **15.9 °C** and Santiago's is **21.9 °C** — a **6 °C gap in the daily mean**. The gap in daytime highs is far larger: Santo Domingo's January mean maximum is **20.6 °C**. Santiago in January routinely runs high-20s to low-30s. **A passenger who dresses for the ship's deck at San Antonio and then drives to Santiago will be dressed for the wrong climate by a wide margin, and vice versa returning in the evening.** Dress in layers and carry a windproof shell; the coast in the afternoon has a persistent onshore sea breeze that makes 20 °C feel like 15 °C.

**The marine layer / camanchaca.** *Camanchaca* is the coastal fog generated under the Pacific anticyclone; it typically forms overnight and **dissipates during the course of the morning** ([Clima de Chile, Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clima_de_Chile)). Maritime influence produces morning cloud along the Valparaíso coast that generally clears before midday ([Megatiempo regional forecast](https://www.megatiempo.cl/pronostico/12513-pronostico-region-valparaiso-vina-del-mar-fin-de-semana-san-felipe-los-andes-san-antonio-1ab.html)). It is **much more persistent April–October and least troublesome December–February**, though summer mornings can still start grey ([Clima de Chile](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clima_de_Chile)).

**What to expect on a January–March world-cruise call.** The best window of the year. Dry — coastal rainfall in Jan–Feb is essentially nil and March marginal. Coastal daytime highs around **19–21 °C**, overnight lows **10–11 °C**. Likely grey and cool at 08:00 as the ship berths, clearing to bright sun by 11:00. Santiago the same day will be **27–32 °C and cloudless**. Sea temperature is cold year-round (Humboldt) — the beaches at Viña look Mediterranean and feel Atlantic. Sun protection matters: the UV index in central Chile in summer is high and the cool sea breeze disguises it.

# PART 2 — LA RÉUNION (LE PORT / POINTE DES GALETS)

## 1. The berth

**The port.** Réunion's single commercial port is the **Grand Port Maritime de La Réunion**, also called **Port Réunion** or the **port de la Pointe des Galets**, in the commune of **Le Port** on the island's north-west coast. It is the only port in France combining five functions in one: maritime passenger terminal, commercial port, naval base, marina and fishing port ([Grand port maritime de La Réunion, Wikipedia FR](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_port_maritime_de_La_R%C3%A9union); [reunion.port.fr](https://reunion.port.fr/)).

**Two sites, 3 km apart.**
- **Port Ouest** ("ancien port"), inaugurated **September 1886** — the old basin, now largely marina, fishing and naval.
- **Port Est** ("nouveau port"), inaugurated **1986** — the modern deep-water container and passenger port.
([Wikipedia FR](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_port_maritime_de_La_R%C3%A9union); [Présentation générale de Port Réunion, PDF](https://reunion.port.fr/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/1-presentation-generale-de-port-reunion.pdf))

**Cruise ships berth alongside at Port Est.** The **gare maritime** (maritime terminal) is at Port Est and handles departures and arrivals of "touristic" traffic — cruise ships and inter-island links. It opens only for cruise arrivals/departures and inter-island shuttles, and offers free WiFi ([Les Ports de l'île de La Réunion](https://www.ile-delareunion.com/fr/pratique/gare-maritime.html); [guide-reunion.fr — Transport maritime](https://guide-reunion.fr/guide-pratique/transports/bateau/)). **No tendering.**

**Does Volendam fit? Yes, with margin.** Port Est's access channel and turning circle were deepened to take draft from **13 m to 16 m**, and a **648 m quay** was built capable of handling two 80,000 t vessels simultaneously ([VINCI Construction Grands Projets — Grand port maritime de La Réunion](https://www.vinci-construction-projets.com/fr/realisations/grand-port-maritime-la-reunion/)). Vessels **over 290 m LOA** have berthed at Port Est ([Présentation générale de Port Réunion](https://reunion.port.fr/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/1-presentation-generale-de-port-reunion.pdf)). Volendam at 237 m / 8.1 m draft is well inside those limits. *(One secondary source cites a ~12 m draft limitation; even that is comfortably above Volendam's requirement — see CORRECTIONS.)*

**Cruise volumes.** Réunion expected **37 cruise ships** by the end of the **2026 season** (season ending May 2026), with roughly **65,000 cruise passengers per season** on average, predominantly German, British, American and South African ([France Info La 1ère — Arcadia call](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/reunion/port/le-bateau-de-croisiere-arcadia-et-ses-2-400-passagers-posent-l-ancre-a-la-reunion-1668723.html)). This is a modest cruise port, not a mass-market one — infrastructure is adequate rather than lavish, and the island is not tuned to absorb thousands of walk-off passengers.

**Shuttles.** Île de La Réunion Tourisme organises shuttle services for cruise passengers to **Le Port town, Saint-Gilles and Saint-Denis** ([France Info La 1ère](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/reunion/port/le-bateau-de-croisiere-arcadia-et-ses-2-400-passagers-posent-l-ancre-a-la-reunion-1668723.html)). Confirm on board — provision varies by call.

## 2. The volcano — Piton de la Fournaise

**Current status as of 30 July 2026: quiet, at "Vigilance" alert, with the enclos partially reopened.**

The **Observatoire Volcanologique du Piton de la Fournaise (OVPF-IPGP)** daily bulletin for **29 July 2026** (issued 30 July 2026) records:
- **Alert level: Vigilance** (the lowest level).
- 4 rockfalls in the summit cone and enclos; 1 summit volcano-tectonic earthquake (M 0.44); 3 deep volcano-tectonic earthquakes (largest M 0.93); no regional earthquakes.
- Summit GNSS stations recording **no particular deformation signals**; far-field stations likewise.
- Soil CO₂ flux still elevated following the February–April 2026 eruption; summit fumarole SO₂ and H₂S below 0.1 ppmv — background values typical of a repose phase.
([OVPF-IPGP daily bulletin](https://www.ipgp.fr/volcanoweb/reunion/Bulletin_quotidien/bulletin.html), read 30 July 2026)

The **monthly bulletin published 2 July 2026** reported slight pressurisation of the magmatic system through June following the eruption that ended **12 April 2026** — moderate seismicity, weak inflation, but **"no element currently allows announcing an imminent eruption"** ([Zinfos974, July 2026](https://www.zinfos974.com/piton-de-la-fournaise-la-recharge-du-volcan-marque-une-pause-aucune-eruption-imminente-en-vue/)).

**Recent eruption record.**
- **18–20 January 2026** — a short eruption on the northern flank. A seismic crisis began 18 January at 16:34 local ([IPGP news](https://www.ipgp.fr/en/news-and-agenda/news/%F0%9F%8C%8B-piton-de-la-fournaise-an-eruption-underway-on-the-northern-flank-of-the-volcano/)).
- **13 February – 12 April 2026** — the year's second and much larger eruption. It stopped, restarted, stopped and restarted again, running **58 days**, well above average. Between **29 and 36 million m³ of lava** reached the surface and **8.5 hectares of new land** were built out into the sea as a lava platform ([Imaz Press — volcano assessment](https://imazpress.com/volcan/volcan-bilan); [Linfo.re — 8.5 ha platform](https://www.linfo.re/la-reunion/societe/piton-de-la-fournaise-la-plateforme-est-d-environ-8-5-hectares)).
- Previous named eruption on the OVPF record: **Piton Guétali, 2 July – 10 August 2023**, ~39 days ([IPGP OVPF](http://www.ipgp.fr/fr/ovpf/actualites-ovpf)).

**This volcano erupts often.** Two eruptions in the first four months of 2026 alone. Anyone planning a 2028 call should assume the *possibility* of an eruption and, more importantly, of eruption-related access restrictions.

**Access — and yes, the road and trails do close.**
The ORSEC "specific organisation" for Piton de la Fournaise has escalating phases; access to the **enclos Fouqué** is governed by **prefectural order** and is closed outright when the alert rises. The prefect returned the island to the **vigilance phase on 7 May 2026 at 15:00**, and **prefectural order n° 2026-627 of 7 May 2026** reopened the enclos partially ([Préfecture de La Réunion — Piton de la Fournaise : réouverture partielle de l'enclos](https://www.reunion.gouv.fr/Actualites/Communiques-de-presse/Piton-de-la-Fournaise-reouverture-partielle-de-l-enclos)).

Under that order, in the **upper enclos** only two routes are authorised:
- **Pas de Bellecombe → Formica Léo → Sentier Rivals → Cratère Caubet**
- **Pas de Bellecombe → Formica Léo → the Dolomieu crater observation site** (northern access)

The **Sentier Kapor remains prohibited** — part of it is buried under the 2026 lava flow. In the **lower enclos**, the 2007 lava tunnels, the 2004 lava tunnels (reopened 6 May 2026) and the northern limit of the 2026 flow via the *chemin de pêcheur du Grand Cap* are open. The prefecture stresses that **"vigilance remains necessary"** and that a new intrusion returns the area to a higher alert with immediate closure. ([Préfecture de La Réunion](https://www.reunion.gouv.fr/Actualites/Communiques-de-presse/Piton-de-la-Fournaise-reouverture-partielle-de-l-enclos))

There is a second, non-volcanic closure risk: in a separate incident, a **rockfall on the Pas de Bellecombe staircase** led to a prefectural order banning access to the enclos and the evacuation of eight hikers, with three days of works needed to secure the descent path ([Linfo.re](https://www.linfo.re/la-reunion/societe/eboulis-sur-l-escalier-du-pas-de-bellecombe-acces-a-l-enclos-interdit-par-arrete-prefectoral-8-randonneurs-evacues); [France Info La 1ère](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/reunion/eboulis-au-volcan-trois-jours-de-travaux-necessaires-pour-securiser-le-sentier-de-descente-dans-l-enclos-1611633.html)). **Check the prefecture's site and the ONF trail status before committing.**

**Is it feasible on a port day? Marginally, and only as an organised excursion.**
- Drive time from the west coast: the route runs via **Plaine des Cafres** and **Bourg-Murat**, then the Route du Volcan. From Saint-Pierre it is **~40 min to Bourg-Murat and ~1 h 30 min to Pas de Bellecombe** ([Offices de tourisme du Sud — La Route du Volcan](https://www.sudreuniontourisme.fr/tresors-du-sud/la-route-du-volcan.html)). Le Port is considerably further round — **budget ~2 h 30 min each way**.
- That is **five hours of driving** for a viewpoint. On a 09:00–18:00 call you would have roughly two to three hours on site.
- **What you actually see from Pas de Bellecombe** (the belvedere at the rim of the enclos): the vast caldera floor, the **Formica Léo** scoria cone directly below, the Dolomieu summit cone beyond, and — in clear weather — an extraordinary black-and-ochre lunar landscape. You do **not** see lava unless there is an active eruption. Descending into the enclos on the authorised trails takes hours and is not a port-day activity.
- **Cloud is the real enemy.** The Plaine des Cafres and the volcano road cloud over reliably from late morning; the belvedere is frequently in fog by 11:00. An early start is not optional.
- **Verdict:** worth it only if the excursion leaves at first light, the forecast is clear, and you accept that you may drive five hours to look at cloud. See the ranking in §9.

## 3. The cirques and UNESCO

**What exactly is inscribed.** **"Pitons, cirques and remparts of Reunion Island"** was inscribed on the World Heritage List on **1 August 2010** under natural criteria **(vii)** — superlative natural phenomena and exceptional natural beauty — and **(x)** — the most significant natural habitats for in-situ conservation of biological diversity. The property covers **105,838 hectares**, more than **40% of the island's land area**, and corresponds broadly to the core zone of the **Parc national de La Réunion** ([UNESCO WHC #1317](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1317/); [Association des Biens Français du Patrimoine Mondial](http://www.assofrance-patrimoinemondial.org/en/properties-inscribed/naturals-landscapes/pitons-cirques-and-remparts-of-reunion-island/)).

UNESCO's justification: the combination of volcanic activity, tectonic landslide events and erosion from heavy rainfall has produced "a spectacular rugged landscape of striking beauty," dominated by **Piton des Neiges** (dormant) and **Piton de la Fournaise** (extremely active), with great diversity and numbers of endemic plants and the most significant remaining natural habitats for terrestrial biodiversity conservation in the **Mascarene Islands** ([UNESCO WHC #1317](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1317/)).

**Piton des Neiges: 3,070 m** — the highest point in the Indian Ocean ([Altitude Rando — Piton des Neiges](https://www.altituderando.com/Piton-des-Neiges-3070m); [Decathlon Outdoor](https://www.decathlon-outdoor.com/fr-fr/explore/france/le-piton-des-neiges-depuis-salazie-66fbcf7bbb6e0)). It is a dormant volcano, and the three cirques are its collapsed and eroded flanks.

**The three cirques.**
- **Cirque de Cilaos** — south-west. Reached only by the **RN5, the "route aux 400 virages"** — 35 km of hairpins, rock-cut tunnels and ravines from Saint-Louis up to over 1,200 m. Allow **1 h to 1 h 30 min from Saint-Louis alone**, more with photo stops. Landslides and works occasionally close sections, especially in the wet season ([guide-reunion.fr — Route de Cilaos](https://guide-reunion.fr/route-de-cilaos/); [Route de Cilaos, Wikipedia FR](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_de_Cilaos)). Thermal springs, lentils and wine (§6).
- **Cirque de Salazie** — north-east, the wettest and greenest. Reached by the **RD48 from Saint-André**, ~18 km up to the cirque, then on to **Hell-Bourg** ([Cascades de France — Voile de la Mariée](https://cascadesdefrance.fr/listing/cascade-du-voile-de-la-mariee-salazie-ile-de-la-reunion/)). This is the road commonly called the **"route des cascades"** for the waterfalls tumbling into the Rivière du Mât gorge along it.
- **Cirque de Mafate** — **accessible only on foot or by helicopter. Verified: true.** There is **no road into Mafate** ([cirquedemafate.re — Accès](https://www.cirquedemafate.re/fr/acces-cirque-mafate); [reunion-mafate.com](https://www.reunion-mafate.com/sejour/acces)). It is inhabited — several hundred people live in scattered *îlets*, of which **La Nouvelle** is the largest and effectively the "capital"; gîtes and tables d'hôtes operate at La Nouvelle, Marla, Roche Plate, Les Orangers, Aurère and Grand-Place. More than **140 km of marked trails** cross it, with the classic western approaches from **Le Maïdo**, **Sans-Souci** and **Dos d'Âne** ([cirquedemafate.re](https://www.cirquedemafate.re/fr/acces-cirque-mafate); [Tropicalement Vôtre](https://www.tropicalement-votre.com/destinations/cirque-mafate-reunion.php)). Supplies and post are helicoptered in. **Mafate is not visitable on a port day except from the air.**

**Trou de Fer.** In the Salazie massif — a gorge of nearly **300 m depth** containing **four waterfalls**, formed in the geological depression of the Piton des Neiges massif ([Decathlon Outdoor — Salazie hikes](https://www.decathlon-outdoor.com/fr-fr/inspire/france/randonnee-cirque-de-salazie-la-reunion)). It is **effectively only seen properly from a helicopter** — the land approach via the Bélouve forest reaches a belvedere but the full amphitheatre is an aerial sight, which is why every helicopter operator sells it as the centrepiece (§4).

**Voile de la Mariée.** In the Cirque de Salazie, visible from the road roughly **1 km beyond Salazie village on the way to Hell-Bourg** — a fan of fine aerial cascades dropping from about **1,200 m to the cirque floor at about 800 m**, named for its resemblance to a bridal veil. There is a dedicated roadside viewpoint with limited parking ([guide-reunion.fr](https://guide-reunion.fr/tourisme-loisirs/interet/cascades/le-voile-de-la-mariee/); [Cascades de France](https://cascadesdefrance.fr/listing/cascade-du-voile-de-la-mariee-salazie-ile-de-la-reunion/); [Vivre La Réunion](https://vivrelareunion.com/explorer/voile-de-la-mariee/)).
**Safety note that guidebooks omit: the trail to the base of the falls is prohibited by a municipal order dating from October 2014**, and the commune of Salazie posts continual rockfall warnings at the trail entrance ([Cascades de France](https://cascadesdefrance.fr/listing/cascade-du-voile-de-la-mariee-salazie-ile-de-la-reunion/)). View it from the road.

2. **€300–350 for 45 minutes is a lot of money.** For a couple that is €600–700. Judge it against what else the day could buy.

Against that: there is no other way to see Mafate and Trou de Fer, the flight compresses the entire UNESCO property into 45 minutes, and it removes all the road-time risk that ruins land excursions here. **On a single port day at Le Port, this is the strongest use of the time.**

## 5. Saint-Denis and the coast — and the shark risk

**Saint-Denis.** ~15–22 km, ~15–25 minutes from the berth, largely on motorway ([Les Ports de l'île de La Réunion](https://www.ile-delareunion.com/fr/pratique/gare-maritime.html); [l-itineraire.com](https://www.l-itineraire.com/de_le-port_a_saint-denis)). It is a French *préfecture* town with Creole colonial architecture — the *cases créoles* of rue de Paris, the Jardin de l'État, the Grande Mosquée Noor-e-Islam, the seafront Barachois with its cannons. It is pleasant, walkable and thoroughly French-provincial-with-palms. **It is not the reason you came.** For a first and only visit, the interior beats the capital comfortably.

**Saint-Gilles-les-Bains and L'Hermitage.** ~20 km, ~18 minutes by car ([ViaMichelin](https://www.viamichelin.com/routes/results/le_port-97420-reunion-reunion-france-to-saint_gilles_les_bains-97460-reunion-reunion-france)). This is the island's beach resort strip: the **L'Hermitage lagoon** behind the fringing coral reef, casuarina shade, restaurants, and the departure point for Hélilagon and Corail flights ([Vivre La Réunion — Saint-Gilles-les-Bains](https://vivrelareunion.com/explorer/saint-gilles-les-bains/)).

### The shark risk — the current legal position, stated precisely

**This is a real, legally-enforced restriction, not a historical footnote. Do not soften it.**

**The framework.** A **prefectural order of 8 February 2017** established temporary regulations for swimming and certain nautical activities. It restricts, **within the 300-metre band off the coast of the department**, the activities most exposed to shark risk — **swimming, and wave-powered nautical activities (surf, bodyboard, bodysurf, longboard, paddleboard)** — **except in the lagoon and in designated managed and supervised areas established by municipal order** ([Préfecture de La Réunion — Rappel de la réglementation](https://www.reunion.gouv.fr/Actions-de-l-Etat/Securite-protection-des-personnes-et-des-biens/Protection-civile/Prevention-et-reduction-du-risque-requin/Rappel-de-la-reglementation-applicable-pour-la-baignade-et-les-activites-naut)).

**Where you MAY swim and surf** — the prefecture's own list:
1. **In the lagoon and on the reef flats** at **Saint-Leu, L'Hermitage, La Saline-les-Bains, Saint-Pierre and Saint-Gilles**;
2. **In areas fitted with anti-shark nets** (*filets de baignade*);
3. **In operational experimental zones (ZONEX)** covered by *Vigi Requins* surveillance and Water Patrol — including **Boucan Canot, Roches Noires beach and L'Étang-Salé**.
([Préfecture de La Réunion — Risque requin : où peut-on se baigner et surfer ?](https://www.reunion.gouv.fr/Actualites/Communiques-de-presse/Risque-requin-ou-peut-on-se-baigner-et-surfer-a-La-reunion))

**Outside those zones, swimming and nautical activities are PROHIBITED**, and the prefecture states the risk is increased there ([same source](https://www.reunion.gouv.fr/Actualites/Communiques-de-presse/Risque-requin-ou-peut-on-se-baigner-et-surfer-a-La-reunion)).

**What changed recently.** In **March 2025** the prefectural order was amended to **re-authorise swimming with fins, mask and snorkel (PMT — *palmes, masque, tuba*) within the 300-metre band**, with the official recommendation **not to do it alone**. The administration's reasoning: a mask lets you see underwater and therefore see danger coming — and diving was never prohibited. Ordinary swimming within 300 m remains banned outside protected reefs and netted areas ([France Info La 1ère, 27 March 2025](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/reunion/risque-requin-la-baignade-palmes-masque-tuba-de-nouveau-autorisee-dans-les-300-metres-l-etat-fait-evoluer-la-reglementation-a-la-reunion-1573393.html)).

**What the lagoon protection actually is.** It is **the coral reef itself**, not a man-made barrier. The fringing reef along the west coast at L'Hermitage/La Saline/Saint-Gilles creates a shallow lagoon that bull sharks and tiger sharks do not enter. **The protection ends where the reef ends.** Passing beyond the reef line — which is a short swim — takes you out of the protected zone and out of the legally permitted area simultaneously. There are additionally **netted enclosures** at some beaches, which are engineered barriers, and **ZONEX** zones, which are not barriers at all but *surveilled* areas with spotters and water patrols.

**Enforcement is active.** The prefect has reinforced prevention and control operations on the coast and at sea, and **maritime-brigade gendarmes are conducting checks and issuing fines** for swimming and nautical activity outside authorised zones ([France Info La 1ère](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/reunion/risque-requins-le-prefet-de-la-reunion-renforce-les-operations-de-prevention-et-de-controle-sur-le-littoral-et-en-mer-1658858.html); [Imaz Press](https://imazpress.com/toute-l-actu/risque-requin-la-gendarmerie-serre-la-vis-controles-et-verbalisations-sur-les-plages)).

**Sharks are present and current, not historical.** On **4 January 2026** the Centre Sécurité Requin reported a 2 m bull shark behind the reef at **Roches Noires**. In the same period **30 sharks — tiger and bull — were captured** in the bay of Saint-Paul, Saint-Gilles (notably Boucan Canot and Roches Noires), Trois-Bassins, Saint-Leu and Saint-Pierre. **Since 4 November 2025, 16 confirmed shark sightings** at Saint-Paul, Le Port, Saint-Leu and L'Étang-Salé ([Préfecture / Imaz Press coverage](https://imazpress.com/toute-l-actu/risque-requin-la-gendarmerie-serre-la-vis-controles-et-verbalisations-sur-les-plages); [Guide Réunion — risque requin élevé](https://guide-reunion.fr/breves/detail/risque-requin-reglementation-en-vigueur/)).

**The flag system** applies at supervised beaches: **red = swimming forbidden; yellow-orange = dangerous but supervised; green = safe and supervised** ([Préfecture de La Réunion](https://www.reunion.gouv.fr/Actions-de-l-Etat/Securite-protection-des-personnes-et-des-biens/Protection-civile/Prevention-et-reduction-du-risque-requin/Rappel-de-la-reglementation-applicable-pour-la-baignade-et-les-activites-naut)). The prefecture is explicit that **public measures cannot eliminate shark risk entirely** and that **absence of signage does not imply permission** — where signage is absent, regulated activities are prohibited ([same source](https://www.reunion.gouv.fr/Actions-de-l-Etat/Securite-protection-des-personnes-et-des-biens/Protection-civile/Prevention-et-reduction-du-risque-requin/Rappel-de-la-reglementation-applicable-pour-la-baignade-et-les-activites-naut)).

**The bottom line for a cruise passenger:** swimming in the **L'Hermitage / La Saline / Saint-Gilles lagoon inside the reef** is legal and is what everyone does. **Swimming anywhere on the coast that is not the lagoon, a netted beach, or a flagged ZONEX is illegal and dangerous.** Do not follow a local example, do not swim out over the reef line, and do not treat a pretty empty beach as an invitation — an empty beach on Réunion usually means a banned one.

## 6. Culture, food, history

**No indigenous population.** Réunion was uninhabited when Europeans arrived. Occupied by France in the early 17th century and first named **Île Bourbon**, it was colonised late ([Musée de Villèle — Esclavage et engagisme](https://www.musee-villele.re/fr/esclavage-et-engagisme)). Permanent settlement dates from the **1660s**; the population is entirely the product of settlement, slavery and indenture. There is no pre-colonial layer and no indigenous claim — a fact that shapes everything about Creole identity here.

**Slavery and abolition.** Slavery was abolished at Réunion by proclamation of **Sarda Garriga on 20 December 1848**, freeing **~65,000 slaves — some 60% of the island's total population** ([Herodote.net — 20 décembre 1848](https://www.herodote.net/20_decembre_1848-evenement-18481220.php); [Musée de Villèle](https://www.musee-villele.re/fr/esclavage-et-engagisme)). **20 December — *Fèt Kaf* — remains the island's principal public holiday.**

**Engagisme.** Indenture: a renewable **five-year contract** offered to workers from outside the colony, which became essential to the plantation economy immediately after abolition. **On the very day of abolition, 20 December 1848, 500 Indian labourers disembarked.** Between 1848 and 1860 some **47,285 indentured workers** were introduced; Indian indentured numbers rose from ~11,000 in 1849 to ~37,000 in 1859. The system **doubled the island's population in under 35 years** ([Engagisme à La Réunion, Wikipedia FR](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engagisme_%C3%A0_La_R%C3%A9union); [Portail esclavage Réunion — indentured labour](https://www.portail-esclavage-reunion.fr/en/documentaires/abolition-slavery/after-abolition/indentured-labour-in-reunion-island/)). The consequence is visible everywhere: Tamil temples beside Catholic churches beside mosques, and a population with Malagasy, East African, Indian (both Tamil *Malbar* and Gujarati Muslim *Zarab*), Chinese and European ancestry.

**Maloya — verified UNESCO listing.** **Maloya was inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2009** (inscription 1 October 2009) ([UNESCO ICH — Maloya, #00249](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/maloya-00249)). UNESCO's description: a form of music, song and dance native to Réunion, "of mixed racial origins since its outset," created by **Malagasy and African slaves on the sugar plantations** and later adopted by the whole population. Originally dedicated to ancestral worship as ritual, it became "a song of lament against slavery," and for the past thirty years has represented the island's identity. Traditionally a dialogue between soloist and choir over percussion, it now mixes with rock, reggae and jazz ([UNESCO ICH #00249](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/maloya-00249)). *(Note: maloya was effectively suppressed and semi-clandestine into the 1970s — its UNESCO listing is a political as well as a cultural fact.)*

**Food.**
- **Cari** (curry) — the base dish: meat or fish with onion, garlic, turmeric, ginger and tomato, served with rice, *grains* (beans or lentils) and *rougail*.
- **Rougail** — the condiment/side: crushed tomato, onion, chilli, sometimes with salted fish (*rougail morue*) or sausage (*rougail saucisse*, arguably the national dish).
- **Samoussas** — the ubiquitous street snack, an Indian-Ocean inheritance from the *engagisme* population.
- **Vanille Bourbon** — Réunion vanilla, named for Île Bourbon; the hand-pollination technique that made commercial vanilla possible was discovered here by **Edmond Albius**, an enslaved boy, in 1841.
- **Rhum** — *rhum arrangé* (macerated with fruit, vanilla and spices) is the standard digestif and the standard souvenir.
- **Lentilles de Cilaos** — small brown lentils grown on the steep volcanic slopes of the Cirque de Cilaos, whose flavour is attributed to the soil and mountain climate; the classic partner to rougail and cari ([Papilles et Pupilles](https://www.papillesetpupilles.fr/2019/12/lentilles-de-cilaos.html/); [Blog Hertz Réunion](https://blog.hertzreunion.com/lentilles-cilaos-reunion/)).
- **Vin de Cilaos** — produced since the mid-19th century, originally from the **Isabelle** grape alone; since 1998 Malbec, Chenin and Pinot Noir have been planted, and since **2004** the designation **"vin de pays de Cilaos"** applies to wines produced between **600 and 1,300 m altitude — making it France's highest vineyard** ([Allons La Réunion — Cilaos](https://allonslareunion.com/restaurants-reunion/cirque-de-cilaos.html)). It is a curiosity more than a great wine, and that is fine.

## 7. Practicalities

**Currency: the euro.** Réunion is a French *département et région d'outre-mer* (DROM) and uses the euro. No currency change from mainland France.

**EU status — and the Schengen trap.** Réunion is an **EU outermost region** but **is NOT part of the Schengen area** ([Expat.com — formalités d'entrée à La Réunion](https://www.expat.com/fr/guide/afrique/la-reunion/13874-formalites-d-entree-a-la-reunion.html); [Service-Public.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F10610?lang=en)). Consequences:
- **EU/EEA/Andorra/Monaco/Switzerland citizens:** no visa; a national identity card suffices ([Expat.com](https://www.expat.com/fr/guide/afrique/la-reunion/13874-formalites-d-entree-a-la-reunion.html)).
- **Everyone else who needs a visa for France needs a visa specifically endorsed for Réunion.** A Schengen visa is **not sufficient** unless the visa sticker names the DOM — it must read **"Valable pour la Réunion"** ([Expat.com](https://www.expat.com/fr/guide/afrique/la-reunion/13874-formalites-d-entree-a-la-reunion.html); [Air Austral — documents de voyage](https://www.air-austral.com/preparer-mon-vol/documents-de-voyage/ile-de-la-reunion-ressortissants-etrangers.html)). **This is the single most common paperwork failure at this port.** Passengers of visa-required nationalities who obtained a Schengen visa for a European segment of the world cruise may find it does not admit them here.
- Passports should have **at least two blank pages** and be **issued within the last 10 years** ([Expat.com](https://www.expat.com/fr/guide/afrique/la-reunion/13874-formalites-d-entree-a-la-reunion.html)).
- Visa-exempt nationalities for short stays in France (US, UK, Canada, Australia, NZ, Japan and others) are generally also visa-exempt for Réunion — but **verify against your own nationality; do not assume from the Schengen list.**

**Language.** French, and **Réunion Creole** in daily life. English is not widely spoken outside tourism-facing businesses. France scores **539, ranked 38th of 123, in the EF English Proficiency Index 2025** — an 11-place jump from 2024, but still "moderate" ([EF EPI 2025](https://www.ef.com/wwen/epi/); [PR Newswire — France EF 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/niveau-danglais--la-france-fait-un-bond-de-11-places-au-classement-mondial-ef-2025-et-depasse-enfin-lespagne-et-litalie-302617462.html)). Assume less English than that ranking suggests once you leave Saint-Denis and Saint-Gilles.

**Cards and cash.** Full French banking. Cards near-universally accepted, contactless standard, ATMs (*distributeurs*) everywhere in towns. **Carry some cash for mountain villages, gîtes and roadside stalls in Cilaos, Salazie and Hell-Bourg.**

**Connectivity.** Réunion is French territory and part of the French mobile market. **EU roaming rules ("roam like at home") apply to EU/EEA SIMs.** UK SIMs post-Brexit and non-EU SIMs will roam at international rates — check before landing. Operators: Orange Réunion, SFR Réunion, Free Réunion, Zeop. Coverage is good on the coast, patchy-to-absent inside the cirques and on the volcano road. The Port Est maritime terminal has **free WiFi** ([Les Ports de l'île de La Réunion](https://www.ile-delareunion.com/fr/pratique/gare-maritime.html)).

**Driving and car hire.** Left-hand-drive, drive on the right, French road law, French signage. Car hire is straightforward and there are many local operators; an EU/UK/US licence is accepted for short visits. **The RN1 coastal motorway and the Nouvelle Route du Littoral** — a 12 km viaduct partly over the sea linking Saint-Denis to La Possession — make the northern coastal run fast ([Nouvelle route du Littoral, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouvelle_route_du_Littoral)). Everything else is slow.

**The mountain-road reality — quantified, because this is where port days die.**
- **RN5 to Cilaos:** 35 km, ~400 bends, from sea level to over 1,200 m, with single-lane rock tunnels. **1 h–1 h 30 min from Saint-Louis alone**, and Saint-Louis is already ~50 km from Le Port. Landslides and roadworks close sections, particularly in the wet season ([guide-reunion.fr](https://guide-reunion.fr/route-de-cilaos/)).
- **Route du Volcan:** Saint-Pierre → Bourg-Murat 26 km / ~40 min; Bourg-Murat → Pas de Bellecombe a further ~50 min ([Offices de tourisme du Sud](https://www.sudreuniontourisme.fr/tresors-du-sud/la-route-du-volcan.html)). From Le Port budget **~2 h 30 min each way**.
- **RD48 to Salazie/Hell-Bourg:** ~18 km from Saint-André to the cirque, then further to Hell-Bourg ([Cascades de France](https://cascadesdefrance.fr/listing/cascade-du-voile-de-la-mariee-salazie-ile-de-la-reunion/)).

**Rule of thumb: on Réunion's mountain roads, divide the map distance by about 25–30 km/h, not 60.** Coach drivers are slower still. A "50 km" excursion into the interior is a two-hour drive. Every published port-day plan that treats Cilaos or the volcano as a casual add-on is wrong.

**Tipping.** French norms — **service is included by law** (*service compris*) in restaurant bills. Tipping is genuinely optional and modest: rounding up, or a few euros for good service. There is no 10–20% expectation. Do not tip as you would in North America; it is unnecessary and reads oddly.

## 8. Climate and cyclones

**Climate type.** Tropical maritime, strongly modified by relief. Two seasons: a **hot, wet austral summer (roughly November–April)** and a **cooler, drier austral winter (May–October)**. The **east/windward coast is far wetter** than the **west/leeward coast** where Le Port and Saint-Gilles sit; altitude drops temperature sharply (Cilaos at 1,200 m and the Plaine des Cafres are markedly cooler than the coast, and the summits can freeze).

**Normals, 1991–2020, Saint-Denis / Gillot station** (alt. 20 m, 6 km from Saint-Denis):
- **Annual mean: 24.2 °C**
- **Coolest month (July): 21.8 °C mean**
- **Warmest month (February): 27.1 °C mean**
- **Annual precipitation: 1,530 mm**
- **Driest month (October): 45 mm**
- **Wettest month (February): 280 mm**
([Climats et Voyages — Saint-Denis](https://www.climatsetvoyages.com/climat/reunion/saint-denis), citing the 1991–2020 Gillot series; corroborated by [Infoclimat — St-Denis/Gillot station 67128](https://www.infoclimat.fr/observations-meteo/temps-reel/st-denis-reunion-gillot/67128.html))

**Note the geography:** Gillot is on the north coast. **Le Port and the west coast are drier** than these figures (rain-shadowed), and the **east coast and interior are far wetter** — parts of the island are among the wettest places on Earth for 24-hour and multi-day rainfall records.

**Cyclone season.** The official South-West Indian Ocean cyclone season runs **15 November to 30 April** ([France Info La 1ère — saison cyclonique](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/reunion/tendance-de-la-saison-cyclonique-2025-2026-une-chance-sur-deux-que-l-activite-soit-superieure-a-la-normale-1638541.html); [Météo-France La Réunion — cyclone](https://meteofrance.re/fr/cyclone)). Météo-France La Réunion is the WMO Regional Specialised Meteorological Centre for the basin.

**Recent seasons.**
- **2025–2026 season (15 Nov 2025 – 30 Apr 2026): more active than normal but less remarkable than the season before.** **11 named storms** — 10 purely tropical, 1 subtropical. **6 became tropical cyclones, of which 4 reached intense tropical cyclone stage.** Activity was concentrated in **January–February**. **Réunion itself was spared**; Madagascar was hit hard by **Fytia** and **Gezani** ([France Info La 1ère — bilan saison 2025-2026](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/reunion/saison-cyclonique-2025-2026-plus-active-que-la-normale-mais-moins-remarquable-que-la-precedente-1697929.html); [Linfo.re — 11 tempêtes](https://www.linfo.re/la-reunion/societe/saison-cyclonique-2025-2026-11-systemes-comptabilises-une-saison-plus-active-que-la-normale)). Other named systems that season included **Dudzai** ([Populaction](https://populaction.com/meteo-cyclone-a-la-reunion-etat-des-lieux-de-la-saison-cyclonique-2025-2026/)).
- **2024–2025:** **Garance** struck Réunion directly — this is the "previous, more remarkable" season the 2025–26 bilan compares against ([France Info La 1ère](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/reunion/saison-cyclonique-2025-2026-plus-active-que-la-normale-mais-moins-remarquable-que-la-precedente-1697929.html)).
- **2026–2027 season** runs **15 November 2026 – 30 April 2027** ([France Info La 1ère](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/reunion/tendance-de-la-saison-cyclonique-2025-2026-une-chance-sur-deux-que-l-activite-soit-superieure-a-la-normale-1638541.html)).

**What to expect on a March–April call, and the cancellation risk.**
- **March–April is inside the cyclone season and at the tail of the wet season.** Temperatures ~26–27 °C mean at the coast, humid, with heavy convective rain and afternoon cloud in the interior. February is the wettest month; March is not far behind.
- **The direct-hit probability on any given day is low; the disruption probability is not.** Réunion's cyclone alert system (*alerte orange / alerte rouge / phase de sauvegarde*) shuts the island down when raised — roads, businesses and the port close, and a red alert means everyone stays indoors. A ship will not berth into a raised alert.
- **The realistic risk on a March–April call is not a cyclone strike but a missed or shortened call** because the port closes, sea state is unworkable, or the ship diverts around a system in the basin. On a world cruise with a fixed onward schedule, that is a real possibility.
- **Secondary risk: the interior is closed even without a cyclone.** Heavy rain triggers landslides on the RN5 to Cilaos and the Salazie road, and the volcano road and enclos close for cloud, rain and rockfall. **A wet-season port day has a meaningfully higher chance of the mountains being unavailable or invisible.**
- **Take the helicopter early or not at all** in this season — the afternoon is usually written off.

## 9. Honest read — what to actually do on one port day

Ranked, on the assumption of a roughly 08:00–18:00 call from Port Est.

**1. Helicopter flight from Saint-Gilles, earliest available slot.** ~18 minutes from the berth, 45–55 minutes airborne, back aboard by late morning with the whole UNESCO property seen — Mafate, Salazie, Cilaos, Trou de Fer, Piton des Neiges and the volcano. €305–350 pp. It is the only way to see the two things (Mafate, Trou de Fer) that no land excursion reaches, and it eliminates the road-time risk that wrecks everything else here. Combine with lunch and the lagoon at L'Hermitage afterwards for a complete day. ([Nunkie](https://nunkie.fr/vol-en-helicoptere-a-la-reunion-comparatif-des-4-meilleures-compagnies-et-tarifs/); [Hélilagon](https://helilagon.com/))

**2. Cirque de Salazie and Hell-Bourg by road.** The most achievable cirque from this berth — Saint-André then RD48 up through the gorge, past the **Voile de la Mariée**, into Salazie and on to **Hell-Bourg**, one of France's *plus beaux villages* with restored Creole houses. Roughly 2 hours each way from Le Port. Gives you a real cirque, a real village, and lunch — without the RN5 hairpins or the volcano's five hours of driving. **View the Voile de la Mariée from the road; the base trail is prohibited by municipal order.** ([Cascades de France](https://cascadesdefrance.fr/listing/cascade-du-voile-de-la-mariee-salazie-ile-de-la-reunion/))

**3. Le Maïdo viewpoint plus the west coast.** Maïdo is the belvedere at 2,200 m looking straight down into Mafate — the single best free view on the island, and a fraction of the drive to the volcano. **Go at dawn**; it clouds over by mid-morning almost every day. Then descend to Saint-Gilles/L'Hermitage for the lagoon and lunch. *(Drive time from Le Port not independently verified — see UNVERIFIED.)*

**4. Piton de la Fournaise / Pas de Bellecombe.** Only if the excursion leaves at first light, the sky is clear, and you accept ~5 hours of driving for a caldera viewpoint with a real chance of fog. Check the prefecture's current *arrêté* first: as of 30 July 2026 the enclos is **partially reopened under order n° 2026-627 of 7 May 2026**, with the Sentier Kapor still closed under the 2026 lava. ([Préfecture de La Réunion](https://www.reunion.gouv.fr/Actualites/Communiques-de-presse/Piton-de-la-Fournaise-reouverture-partielle-de-l-enclos))

**5. Cirque de Cilaos.** The most beautiful road on the island and the least sensible port-day choice — 400 bends, 2–2.5 hours each way, and closure-prone. Magnificent if you have three days. Not on a nine-hour call.

**6. Saint-Denis and the coast, at leisure.** Creole architecture, the Barachois, a Tamil temple, lunch. The low-stress option, genuinely pleasant, and the right answer for anyone with mobility limits, a late arrival, or a cyclone-season sky. Add the L'Hermitage lagoon for a swim — **in the lagoon, inside the reef, nowhere else.**

**What NOT to do:** attempt Mafate on foot (it is a multi-day trek); swim outside the lagoon/netted/ZONEX zones (illegal and dangerous); plan around Cilaos *and* the volcano in one day (impossible); or assume a Schengen visa admits you (it does not).

## PART 2 — CORRECTIONS

1. **"Cruise ships tender at Réunion" / "anchor off Le Port."** No — **alongside at Port Est**, at the gare maritime. ([Les Ports de l'île de La Réunion](https://www.ile-delareunion.com/fr/pratique/gare-maritime.html))

2. **"Réunion is in the Schengen area" / "a Schengen visa covers Réunion."** **Wrong and consequential.** Réunion is an EU outermost region but **outside Schengen**; a visa-national needs a visa explicitly endorsed **"Valable pour la Réunion."** ([Expat.com](https://www.expat.com/fr/guide/afrique/la-reunion/13874-formalites-d-entree-a-la-reunion.html); [Air Austral](https://www.air-austral.com/preparer-mon-vol/documents-de-voyage/ile-de-la-reunion-ressortissants-etrangers.html))

3. **"Swimming is banned everywhere in Réunion because of sharks."** Over-stated. Swimming **is** permitted **in the lagoon and reef flats at Saint-Leu, L'Hermitage, La Saline-les-Bains, Saint-Pierre and Saint-Gilles**, in netted areas, and in surveilled ZONEX zones (Boucan Canot, Roches Noires, L'Étang-Salé). It is banned **everywhere else within 300 m of the coast**. ([Préfecture de La Réunion](https://www.reunion.gouv.fr/Actualites/Communiques-de-presse/Risque-requin-ou-peut-on-se-baigner-et-surfer-a-La-reunion))

4. **"The shark ban is total including snorkelling."** Stale since **March 2025**: swimming **with fins, mask and snorkel (PMT)** was re-authorised within the 300 m band, with advice not to do it alone. Plain swimming outside the permitted zones remains banned. ([France Info La 1ère, 27 Mar 2025](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/reunion/risque-requin-la-baignade-palmes-masque-tuba-de-nouveau-autorisee-dans-les-300-metres-l-etat-fait-evoluer-la-reglementation-a-la-reunion-1573393.html))

5. **"The shark crisis is over / historical."** No. **16 confirmed sightings since 4 November 2025**; a 2 m bull shark behind the reef at Roches Noires on **4 January 2026**; **30 tiger and bull sharks captured** across the west and south coasts in that period; gendarmes actively fining offenders in 2026. ([Imaz Press](https://imazpress.com/toute-l-actu/risque-requin-la-gendarmerie-serre-la-vis-controles-et-verbalisations-sur-les-plages); [Guide Réunion](https://guide-reunion.fr/breves/detail/risque-requin-reglementation-en-vigueur/))

6. **"Piton de la Fournaise last erupted in 2023."** The OVPF's own web archive lists Piton Guétali (July–Aug 2023) as its most recent *named* entry, which misleads. There were **two eruptions in 2026 alone**: 18–20 January, and 13 February – 12 April (58 days, 29–36 million m³ of lava, 8.5 ha of new land in the sea). ([IPGP](https://www.ipgp.fr/en/news-and-agenda/news/%F0%9F%8C%8B-piton-de-la-fournaise-an-eruption-underway-on-the-northern-flank-of-the-volcano/); [Imaz Press](https://imazpress.com/volcan/volcan-bilan); [Linfo.re](https://www.linfo.re/la-reunion/societe/piton-de-la-fournaise-la-plateforme-est-d-environ-8-5-hectares))

7. **"The enclos is fully open" or "the enclos is closed."** Neither. **Partially reopened under prefectural order n° 2026-627 of 7 May 2026**, with only two upper-enclos routes authorised and the **Sentier Kapor prohibited** (buried under the 2026 flow). ([Préfecture de La Réunion](https://www.reunion.gouv.fr/Actualites/Communiques-de-presse/Piton-de-la-Fournaise-reouverture-partielle-de-l-enclos))

8. **"You can hike to the base of the Voile de la Mariée."** The trail has been **prohibited by municipal order since October 2014** on rockfall grounds. ([Cascades de France](https://cascadesdefrance.fr/listing/cascade-du-voile-de-la-mariee-salazie-ile-de-la-reunion/))

9. **"The UNESCO site is the volcano."** No — it is **"Pitons, cirques and remparts of Reunion Island," inscribed 2010 under criteria (vii) and (x), 105,838 ha, ~40% of the island**, essentially the national park core. The volcano is one element within it. ([UNESCO WHC #1317](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1317/))

10. **"Mafate can be reached by 4x4."** No. **Foot or helicopter only** — there is no road. ([cirquedemafate.re](https://www.cirquedemafate.re/fr/acces-cirque-mafate))

11. **"Réunion has a 12 m draft limit."** One secondary source says so, but the port's own documentation records the access channel and turning circle deepened **from 13 m to 16 m**, a 648 m quay taking two 80,000 t ships, and vessels **over 290 m LOA** accommodated. Immaterial for Volendam either way, but the 12 m figure appears stale. ([VINCI](https://www.vinci-construction-projets.com/fr/realisations/grand-port-maritime-la-reunion/); [Présentation générale de Port Réunion](https://reunion.port.fr/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/1-presentation-generale-de-port-reunion.pdf))

12. **Distances-in-kilometres reasoning generally.** Every Réunion drive time is longer than the map suggests. **Divide by 25–30 km/h in the interior, not 60.** Cilaos is 35 km of road from Saint-Louis and takes up to 90 minutes.
