# Port Research: Takoradi (Sekondi-Takoradi), Western Region, Ghana

*Compiled 2026-07-31 for the InTheWake port pass. Standing rules in
[`README.md`](README.md): primary sources preferred, every figure carries its
URL, disagreements are recorded rather than averaged, and anything unsourced
lands in UNVERIFIED rather than in the page.*

## 1. The berth — and this one is good news

**Takoradi is a docked call, alongside a working commercial quay.** After Belém
and Lüderitz, that is worth saying first.

- Cruise ships berth at the **General Multipurpose Berths (1–6)** inside the
  main commercial harbour enclosure.
- **Depth alongside ranges 8 to 11.5 m** across those berths.
- **There is no purpose-built passenger terminal building.**
  ([CruiseAlert, Sekondi-Takoradi port guide](https://cruisealert.com/content/port-guide/sekondi-takoradi-ghana);
  [Port of Takoradi, vesseltracker](https://www.vesseltracker.com/dn/Port/takoradi/Dashboard.html))

**The precedent is direct and recent.** On **25 March 2023** the **285-metre
Zuiderdam** — Holland America Line, a larger ship than Volendam — berthed at
Takoradi with **2,272 passengers** aboard, one of **three cruise ships totalling
3,790 passengers** to call within a month.
([Ghana Ports & Harbours Authority, "Takoradi Port Receives Three Cruise Ships
Under One Month"](https://www.ghanaports.gov.gh/media/news-details/432/Q7BFVP1QK1Y/Takoradi-Port-Receives-Three-Cruise-Ships-Under-One-Month) —
*note: this GPHA page returned HTTP 503 to direct fetch on 31 July 2026;
content via search indexing*)

Volendam is 237 m and draws 8.1 m. Against 8–11.5 m alongside and a sister ship
of 285 m already handled, **there is no depth or length problem here.**

### Management
Since **April 2023** the port has been operated by **YILPORT Takoradi Port
Management Company** — a joint venture of **YILPORT Holding (70%)** and **Ibis
Tek (30%)**. Planned investment includes deepening the harbour to a maximum
draft of **16.5 m**. ([Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority,
en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Ports_and_Harbours_Authority))

### The caveat that matters
This is a **working cargo port**, not a cruise facility. Compare Abidjan, where
the berth sits inside a closed customs enclosure and nobody walks out. **I could
not establish from any source whether Takoradi passengers may walk off the
quay** or whether transport out of the enclosure is mandatory — see UNVERIFIED.
The absence of a passenger terminal, and the fact that the berths are inside the
commercial harbour, mean this must be written as an open question and not
assumed either way.

## 2. Cape Coast and Elmina — and a UNESCO correction

**The single most important correction in this file.** Cape Coast Castle and
Elmina Castle are **not two separate World Heritage Sites**. They are two
components of **one 1979 inscription**:

- **Name:** *Forts and Castles, Volta, Greater Accra, Central and Western
  Regions*
- **UNESCO reference:** [whc.unesco.org/en/list/34](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/34/)
- **Inscribed 1979**, under **criterion (vi)**
- **Scope: 3 castles and 15 forts**, the remains of fortified trading posts
  erected **between 1482 and 1786** along the coast between **Keta and Beyin**

Any description calling them "both UNESCO World Heritage Sites" is loosely true
and structurally wrong in the same way as calling the Theatro da Paz a solo
listing. **The property is the whole chain of forts, and that is the point of
the inscription** — this is a coastline of fortifications, not two famous
buildings.

**This also changes what is reachable.** **Fort San Sebastian at Shama is part
of the same inscribed property and is about 20 km from Sekondi-Takoradi** —
against 81 km to Cape Coast. For a passenger who cannot face three hours in a
vehicle, there is a component of the same World Heritage Site twenty minutes
away. ([Fort San Sebastian, viewGhana](https://viewghana.com/ghana-attractions/fort-san-sebastian-in-shama-ghana/);
[Zamani Project, Fort Saint Sebastian, Shama](https://www.zamaniproject.org/site-ghana-shama-fort-saint-sebastian.html))

### The buildings

**Elmina Castle / St George's Castle (São Jorge da Mina)**
- Founded by the **Portuguese in 1482** — the first European trading post on the
  Gulf of Guinea and **the oldest European building in existence south of the
  Sahara**
- Taken by the **Dutch in 1637**; reconstructed by them **1770–1775**
- ([GMMB, St George's Castle](https://gmmb.gov.gh/st-georges-castle-elmina-castle-elmina-1482/);
  [Elmina Castle, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmina_Castle);
  [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Elmina-Castle))

**Cape Coast Castle**
- Built by the **Swedes in 1653** as **Carolusburg**; changed hands repeatedly
  and passed to the **British in 1664**
- ([GMMB, Cape Coast Castle](https://gmmb.gov.gh/cape-coast-castle-cape-coast-1653/);
  [Cape Coast Castle Museum, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Coast_Castle_Museum))

**Fort San Sebastian, Shama**
- Portuguese, **1520s** — sources give **1523** and **1526**; record the
  disagreement rather than picking. One of the oldest European forts in
  sub-Saharan Africa, ~20 km east of Sekondi-Takoradi.

### Fees — from the Ghana Museums and Monuments Board's own schedule

For **National Museum and Castles** (which is the Cape Coast / Elmina tier):

| Category | Ghanaian | Non-Ghanaian |
|---|---|---|
| Adults | GH₵20.00 | **GH₵80.00** |
| Tertiary students / volunteers | GH₵10.00 | GH₵60.00 |
| JHS/SHS students | GH₵6.00 | GH₵30.00 |
| Primary students | GH₵5.00 | GH₵20.00 |

For **Forts and Regional Museums** (the tier Fort San Sebastian falls in):
adults **GH₵10.00** Ghanaian / **GH₵40.00** non-Ghanaian.

([GMMB, Fees and Charges](https://gmmb.gov.gh/fees-and-charges/) — **no
effective date is published on that page**, and no camera, video or guide fee is
listed. Treat as indicative.)

### Hours
Widely reported as **09:00–17:00 daily** at both castles, with entry for
international visitors around US$10–15 — which is broadly consistent with the
GH₵80 schedule above. **No official published hours were obtainable from GMMB.**
([TripZapp, Cape Coast & Elmina visitor guide](https://tripzapp.com/travel-stories/cape-coast-elmina-castles-visitor-guide))

### Distance and time
**Takoradi → Cape Coast: 81.2 km by road, about 1 h 12 min free-flow**
([DistanceCalculator](https://www.distancecalculator.net/from-takoradi-to-cape-coast)).
**Elmina is about 15 minutes' drive from Cape Coast** — the two castles are
close together, so the cost is the outbound leg, not the pairing.

**This is a full-day commitment.** Roughly 2½ hours of driving round trip before
any stop, plus two castles that deserve unhurried time. The v0.1.17 pack's
framing — *"budget the whole day and go quiet rather than pairing them with
anything else"* — is correct and should be preserved.

## 3. Kakum National Park

Rainforest reserve with a **canopy walkway of seven suspension bridges spanning
350 m**, reached by a **20-minute uphill walk** through the forest.

- **30 km from Cape Coast** — so roughly **110 km from Takoradi**
- **Open daily 06:00 to dusk**
- **Canopy walkway: GH₵130 for foreign visitors**, GH₵40 for residents; a gate
  fee of GH₵35 was reported as of October 2025

([Kakum National Park entrance fees](https://kakumnationalpark.info/pricing/);
[Akwaaba, Kakum 2026](https://akwaaba.app/kakum/);
[Laure Wanders, Kakum canopy walk 2026](https://www.laurewanders.com/kakum-canopy-walk-ghana/))

**On a port day, Kakum and the two castles do not combine.** Kakum is 30 km
*past* Cape Coast; adding it makes roughly 220 km of driving. Ship excursions
that offer "castles and canopy walk" exist, and a passenger should ask hard
about the timing before booking one.

## 4. Closer to the ship

- **Market Circle**, Takoradi's central circular market — the town's landmark
  and about 1.5 km from the harbour gate.
- **Fort Orange, Sekondi** — a Dutch fort of 1642, now a lighthouse, in the twin
  town north-east of Takoradi. Another component of the same World Heritage
  inscription.
- **Fort San Sebastian, Shama** — ~20 km east; see §2.
- **Busua Beach**, ~30 km west in the Ahanta West district, with **Fort Metal
  Cross at Dixcove** immediately adjacent — a British fort of the 1690s and
  again part of the same inscribed property.
- **Nzulezo**, the stilt village on Lake Tadane near Beyin — about **90 km west**
  and reached by canoe. A long day; not a natural pairing with anything.

**UNVERIFIED:** 2026 opening hours and admission for Fort Orange, Fort Metal
Cross and Nzulezo, and whether Fort Orange is open to visitors at all given its
lighthouse function.

## 5. Entry, health and money

### Visa
**A visa is required** of US and Canadian passport holders; Ghana does not offer
them visa-free entry. **ECOWAS nationals enter visa-free for up to 90 days.** A
Ghanaian **eVisa** is the fastest route, with standard processing of 5–10
business days and expedited service 2–3 days at some missions.
([Akwaaba, Ghana visa guide 2026](https://akwaaba.app/ghana-visa-guide-2026/);
[Ghana High Commission Ottawa, visa information](https://ottawa.mfa.gov.gh/visaapplicationinfo.aspx))

**UNVERIFIED and important:** one secondary source states that "beginning
25 May 2026, travellers need a visa to visit Ghana for business or tourism,"
which reads like a change of policy or of process. **I could not corroborate
that date from any Ghanaian government source**, and it should not be written
into a page. What is solid is that a visa is required for US, Canadian and
Australian citizens; the mechanism should be confirmed with the line.

Also unresolved, and the same question Abidjan raises: **whether a cruise
passenger arriving by ship is covered by an eVisa, a visa on arrival, or a
ship's-agent collective shore pass.** No Ghanaian government source consulted
addresses arrival by vessel. **Put it to the line in writing.**

### Yellow fever — required, and there is a documentation trap
**Yellow fever vaccination is required for all travellers aged 9 months and
over**, including travellers transiting Ghana, and documented evidence must be
presented at the port of entry. Vaccination must be **at least 10 days before
arrival**.

**The trap:** Ghana's own consular documentation still defines a valid
certificate as one where "the duration of vaccination status is not more than
ten years" — language that **predates the 2016 amendment to the International
Health Regulations**. Under that amendment, in force since **11 July 2016**,
**all ICVP yellow fever certificates are valid for the life of the holder and a
state cannot require revaccination as a condition of entry**, even where the
certificate carries a printed ten-year expiry.

([Ghana High Commission South Africa, Yellow Fever Vaccination Requirement (PDF)](https://www.ghanahighcommission.co.za/consular/visa/YellowFeverRequirements.pdf);
[Ghana vaccine requirements 2026](https://ghanacitizenship.com/ghana-vaccine-requirements-what-travelers-need-before-visiting/);
[Altrincham Travel Clinic, Ghana](https://altrinchamtravelclinic.co.uk/travel-vaccines/ghana))

**Write it as: your certificate is valid for life, Ghana's own published text has
not caught up, carry the card and do not let an old expiry date panic you into a
booster you do not need.** This is the same certificate the Abidjan call
requires, so anyone on this itinerary is carrying it already.

### Money and language
- **Ghana cedi (GHS).** Ghana is markedly more cash-friendly than Namibia or
  South Africa; the pack's own note that "Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire are
  cash-friendlier" holds.
- **English is the official language** — the only call on the West African leg
  where that is true, and a real practical difference from Abidjan.

## 6. Climate

**Köppen Am — tropical monsoon.** The wet season is warm and mostly cloudy, the
dry season hot and partly cloudy, and it is **oppressive year round**.

| Month | High °C | Low °C | Rain mm | Wet days | Sea °C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | 30.6 | 25.0 | **8** | 2.0 | 27 |
| Feb | 31.1 | 25.6 | 18 | 4.6 | 28 |
| Mar | 31.1 | 26.1 | 66 | 12.2 | 28 |
| Apr | 30.6 | 26.1 | **117** | 17.5 | 28 |
| May | 30.0 | 25.6 | **211** | 23.2 | 28 |
| Jun | 28.3 | 25.0 | **241** | 22.5 | 27 |
| Jul | 27.2 | 23.9 | 107 | 12.4 | 25 |
| Aug | **26.7** | **23.3** | 36 | 6.7 | **24** |
| Sep | 27.8 | 23.9 | 74 | 12.6 | 24 |
| Oct | 28.9 | 24.4 | 102 | 16.8 | 26 |
| Nov | 30.0 | 25.0 | 38 | 7.6 | 28 |
| Dec | 30.6 | 25.6 | 15 | 2.2 | 28 |

([Weather Spark, Sekondi-Takoradi](https://weatherspark.com/y/40133/Average-Weather-in-Sekondi-Takoradi-Ghana-Year-Round) —
figures converted from °F and inches. Cross-checked against
[climate-data.org, Sekondi-Takoradi](https://en.climate-data.org/africa/ghana/western-region/sekondi-takoradi-1355/),
which gives an annual total of **1,366 mm**, a January minimum of 50 mm and a
June maximum of 246 mm — the same shape, with January materially wetter. **The
two series disagree on January: 8 mm against 50 mm.** Weather Spark's is a
modelled reanalysis; climate-data.org's is also modelled. Neither is a national
met-service normal, and no Ghana Meteorological Agency series was obtainable.)

**Key points:**
- **Wet season 21 March – 31 October; dry season 31 October – 21 March.**
- **Wettest: June** (~241 mm, 22.5 wet days). **Driest: January.**
- **Humidity is the constant.** Weather Spark records **100% "muggy" days in
  every month except January**, which manages 93%.
- **Temperature barely moves**: highs 27–31 °C all year, lows 23–26 °C. Sea
  24–28 °C.
- Wind is light and **from the south year-round** — 5–7 mph means.

### What to expect on a world-cruise call
Leg 7 of the v0.1.17 itinerary places Takoradi between Walvis Bay and Abidjan at
roughly days 101–117 of a 129-day voyage departing early January — so **late
April into early May 2028**, which is squarely on the ramp into the wettest part
of the year. April averages **117 mm across 17.5 wet days** (roughly a 58%
chance of rain on any given day); May **211 mm across 23 days** (about 75%).

**The practical read:** this is a hot, humid, likely-wet call with a long road
day at the centre of it, and the heat is the thing to plan around rather than
the rain. Same posture as Abidjan.

## 7. The honest read — one port day in Takoradi

**The shape of the day.** You berth — a real relief after two tender ports — but
inside a working cargo harbour with no passenger terminal. **The whole question
is whether you commit to the 81 km each way to Cape Coast and Elmina.**

**If you go:** budget the entire day. Roughly 2½ hours of driving round trip,
two castles that ask for unhurried time, and no room for anything else. The v0.1.17
pack's instruction to "go quiet rather than pairing them with anything else" is
right and should be preserved verbatim in spirit. These are the dungeons and the
Door of No Return; they are not a stop on a circuit.

**If you cannot:** the honest alternative is **Fort San Sebastian at Shama, 20 km
east — a component of the same World Heritage property**, and Market Circle in
Takoradi itself. That is a real answer rather than a consolation, and no
mainstream guide gives it.

**Not feasible:** Kakum *and* the castles (about 220 km of driving); Nzulezo
paired with anything; Accra.

**The one-line verdict.** Takoradi is the easiest berth and the hardest day on
the West African leg — the only call where the right decision might be to spend
five hours in a vehicle, and where the reason to do it has nothing to do with
enjoyment.

## CORRECTIONS

1. **"Cape Coast Castle and Elmina Castle are both UNESCO World Heritage
   Sites."** Loosely true, structurally wrong. They are **two components of a
   single 1979 inscription — *Forts and Castles, Volta, Greater Accra, Central
   and Western Regions*, criterion (vi), covering 3 castles and 15 forts built
   between 1482 and 1786 between Keta and Beyin**. The property is the chain,
   not the pair. ([whc.unesco.org/en/list/34](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/34/))

2. **"There is nothing of that history near the port."** Wrong, and it matters
   for anyone who cannot do the 81 km. **Fort San Sebastian at Shama (~20 km),
   Fort Orange at Sekondi, and Fort Metal Cross at Dixcove are all components of
   the same inscribed World Heritage property.**

3. **"Takoradi is a tender port."** No. Cruise ships berth at the General
   Multipurpose Berths (1–6) with 8–11.5 m alongside; the 285 m Zuiderdam
   berthed there on 25 March 2023 with 2,272 passengers.

4. **"Your yellow fever certificate expires after ten years."** **Stale.** Under
   the 2016 IHR amendment, in force since 11 July 2016, all ICVP yellow fever
   certificates are valid for life and revaccination cannot be required as a
   condition of entry. **Ghana's own consular documentation still carries the
   pre-2016 ten-year language** — carry the card and do not buy an unnecessary
   booster.

5. **"Elmina Castle was built in 1637 by the Dutch."** The Dutch *took* it in
   1637 and reconstructed it in 1770–75. It was **founded by the Portuguese in
   1482** and is the oldest European building standing south of the Sahara.
