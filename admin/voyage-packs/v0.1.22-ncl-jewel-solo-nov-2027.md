## Welcome {#welcome}

> Made for someone sailing this cruise alone, who wants one plain read on the ten nights: the ship, the six stops, and what to check before you go.

This pack is about one cruise: **[Norwegian Jewel](/ships/norwegian/norwegian-jewel.html)**, Norwegian Cruise Line, sailing 10 nights round trip from **Miami, Monday November 29 to Thursday December 9, 2027**. NCL calls it a "10-Day Caribbean Round-trip Miami: Curaçao, Aruba & Dominican Republic." She calls at **Great Stirrup Cay, Cabo Rojo, Aruba, Curaçao, Falmouth and Grand Cayman**, with three sea days spread through the trip.

It's written for someone sailing it **solo**. You don't need a group to do this cruise well, and NCL has cabins on this ship built for one.

### A note on what's first-hand here and what isn't

I haven't sailed Norwegian Jewel. This pack comes from NCL's own pages (its schedule for this exact sailing, its ship page, its deck plans, its FAQ and its press releases), PortMiami, and government, tourism and park pages for each stop. Where only a travel site had the answer, the pack says so. Where nobody had it, the pack says that too. The sources note at the end, and the fact-check file behind this pack, list what was checked.

**Your cruise is more than a year away.** Prices and policies here were checked in **October 2026**, and NCL changes some of them every year. Check them again when NCL opens your bookings, and again the month before you sail. Where this pack and the NCL app disagree, go with the app.

It is calm by design. No urgency, no upsell, no countdown timer. The site that produced this pack is independent: no cruise-line affiliations, no shore-excursion commissions, no display ads. (See our [affiliate disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure.html).)

**A note on the season.** NOAA's National Hurricane Center says the Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. You sail on November 29, the second-to-last day of it. NOAA adds that tropical cyclone activity "sometimes occurs before and after these dates," so travel insurance still makes sense.

**Includes an Emergency Contacts section** with a fillable handoff card to leave with one trusted person at home. When you sail alone, that person is your link to home. The companion app keeps the card on your phone, and it works offline once it's loaded.

If this is your first time sailing solo, the [solo cruising stories on this site](/solo.html) are worth a read before you go.

## Section 1 — Your Ship: Norwegian Jewel {#section-1}

### The first of her class, freshened up in 2025

[Norwegian Jewel](/ships/norwegian/norwegian-jewel.html) was built by Meyer Werft in Papenburg, Germany, which handed her over to NCL on August 4, 2005. The shipyard's release calls her "the first cruise ship out of a series of four new vessels" it built for NCL, and NCL's own pages call them the Jewel class. NCL's ship page lists her as refurbished in 2025, and an NCL press release from April 2026 mentions "the recently added Vibe Beach Club," NCL's adults-only sun deck.

Gross tonnage, 93,502 for her, isn't a weight. The International Maritime Organization says it "is a function of the moulded volume of all enclosed spaces of the ship." Divide it by NCL's guest count and you get about 39 gross tons per guest. If you want to see how she compares with other ships, the [Ship Size Atlas](/tools/ship-size-atlas.html) lines them up.

### What's on board

These come from NCL's "What's On Board" page for Jewel. Line-ups can change by 2027, so check the app.

- **Included restaurants:** Azura and Tsar's Palace (the main dining rooms, Deck 6), the [Garden Café](/restaurants/ncl/garden-cafe.html) buffet and [The Great Outdoors](/restaurants/ncl/the-great-outdoors.html) (Deck 12), Topsiders Bar & Grill by the pool (Deck 12), and [O'Sheehan's](/restaurants/ncl/osheehans.html) (Deck 8), which NCL lists as open 24 hours.
- **Specialty restaurants (extra):** [Cagney's Steakhouse](/restaurants/ncl/cagneys-steakhouse.html), [Moderno Churrascaria](/restaurants/ncl/moderno-churrascaria.html), [La Cucina](/restaurants/ncl/la-cucina.html), [Le Bistro](/restaurants/ncl/le-bistro.html), [Teppanyaki](/restaurants/ncl/teppanyaki.html), [Chin Chin](/restaurants/ncl/chin-chin.html) and Sushi. NCL's page gives a cover charge of $40 to $60 a person, and its gratuity FAQ adds a 20% charge to specialty dining.
- **Bars:** among others, [Maltings](/restaurants/ncl/maltings-whiskey-bar.html), [Shaker's](/restaurants/ncl/shakers-martini-bar.html), [Bliss Ultra Lounge](/restaurants/ncl/bliss-ultra-lounge.html), the [Sugarcane Mojito Bar](/restaurants/ncl/sugarcane-mojito-bar.html), the Spinnaker Lounge, a [Sake Bar](/restaurants/ncl/sake-bar.html) and a [Starbucks](/restaurants/ncl/ncl-starbucks.html).
- **Shows** are in the Stardust Theater.
- **Pool, spa and fitness:** the Sapphire Pools on Deck 12, the Mandara Spa, and the Pulse Fitness Center. The spa's **Thermal Suite** is adults only and needs its own paid pass.
- **[Vibe Beach Club](/restaurants/ncl/vibe-beach-club.html)** on Deck 14 is adults only and needs a paid pass.

### What Jewel doesn't have

- **No Studio cabins and no Studio Lounge.** NCL lists Studios on ten ships, and Jewel isn't one of them. If you've read about NCL's solo lounge, that's on other ships.
- **No self-service laundry.** NCL's packing page says "laundry is not self-service." Fill in the order form in your cabin and your steward collects it. NCL offers express same-day service too. Prices are on the form.

### Cabin choice: sailing solo

NCL sells **Solo Inside, Solo Oceanview and Solo Balcony** cabins, and its solo page lists Norwegian Jewel among the ships that have them. NCL says "double occupancy rates don't apply" in those cabins.

If you book a regular cabin for one, expect to pay for two. NCL doesn't publish a single-supplement rule, but its promotion terms speak of "singles paying 200%." Price the solo cabins against a regular cabin before you choose. The [stateroom check](/stateroom-check.html) can tell you about a specific cabin number on Jewel.

### Free at Sea

For bookings made from November 5, 2025, NCL's package is called **Free at Sea**. Per NCL's Free at Sea page, read October 2026, it includes:

- An **open bar** for guests 21 and over, on board and at Great Stirrup Cay
- **Specialty dining**: four meals on a cruise of nine nights or more, so four on this one
- **Wi-Fi**: 150 minutes
- A **$50 shore excursion credit** at each port, for the first guest in the cabin

The open bar isn't free of charges. NCL's own Free at Sea flyer (December 2025) says: "Gratuities are $28.50/per person/per day for all Guests 21+. Please note this is the pre-cruise price. The onboard price will vary." For ten nights alone, that's **$285**, on top of the daily service charge. Guests 3 to 20 get soda and juice instead, for $12.50 a day. The [drink calculator](/drink-calculator.html?line=ncl) uses the same $28.50. What's included depends on when and how you book, so check your own confirmation.

### Connectivity

NCL's Wi-Fi runs on Starlink. Free at Sea includes 150 minutes. Beyond that, NCL's Free at Sea page lists the **Voyage Wi-Fi Pass at $29.99** and the **Streaming Voyage Wi-Fi Pass at $39.99**, each per cabin per day, plus $14.99 a day for each extra device (October 2026). The [internet at sea guide](/internet-at-sea.html) explains how ship Wi-Fi works.

## Section 2 — Day by Day {#section-2}

Each port day has the times from NCL's schedule, the all-aboard time, and a few things to know. The port pages on this site go deeper.

### Day 1 — Monday, November 29 — Miami (embarkation)

**Ship leaves:** 4:00 PM. **Be on board by 3:00 PM.**

- **The terminal.** PortMiami lists Terminal B, "The Pearl of Miami," as NCL's terminal, and Terminal C as one that "serves ships from several cruise lines." No terminal has been assigned for a 2027 sailing yet, so **your eDocs name yours.**
- **Parking.** PortMiami lists Garage B, 1756 N. Cruise Blvd., at $35 a day (effective January 1, 2025). If your eDocs say Terminal C, Garage C is at 1648 N. Cruise Blvd., at $28 a day. Debit cards aren't accepted.
- **Arrival time.** NCL asks guests who've checked in online to arrive at the terminal no later than one hour before their chosen boarding time.
- **Carry-on.** Keep your passport, medications, a swimsuit and a change of clothes with you. NCL asks you to carry a small bag with what you need on the first day "while your luggage is delivered" to your cabin.

More on the [Miami port page](/ports/miami.html).

### Day 2 — Tuesday, November 30 — Great Stirrup Cay, Bahamas {#day-great-stirrup-cay}

**In port:** 7:00 AM to 3:00 PM. **All aboard 2:00 PM.**

This is NCL's own island in the Berry Islands of the Bahamas.

- **The pier.** NCL says a multi-ship pier debuted in December 2025. When it opened, NCL said all ships visiting the island will benefit from it, with "guests no longer being required to tender from ship to shore" (quoted by Cruise Industry News). It was still being finished then, and on that first day a Jewel-class sister, Norwegian Gem, anchored and tendered (CruiseHive). In September 2026 Cruise Industry News reported that "the new pier opened in August 2026." So expect to walk ashore, and **check the app** the day before.
- **What's free:** the beaches, loungers and umbrellas, and the Splash Harbor splash pad. The Free at Sea open bar applies on the island.
- **What costs extra:** the **Great Tides Waterpark**, which opened in September 2026, was "from $89" on NCL's excursion page in October 2026. Also cabanas, the Vibe Shore Club day pass, the zipline, and snorkel and paddleboard rentals.
- **Money.** NCL's ships use US dollars, charged to your shipboard account. NCL lists your key card among the things to bring to the waterpark.

More on the [Great Stirrup Cay port page](/ports/great-stirrup-cay.html).

### Day 3 — Wednesday, December 1 — At sea

The first sea day. Book your specialty dinners and any shore excursions you still want, find the spa and the quiet corners, and sleep in if you like.

### Day 4 — Thursday, December 2 — Cabo Rojo, Dominican Republic {#day-cabo-rojo}

**In port:** 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM. **All aboard 3:00 PM.**

Cabo Rojo is a new cruise port in **Pedernales Province**, in the far southwest of the Dominican Republic. The Dominican Port Authority says its first cruise ship arrived on January 4, 2024.

- **What's around it.** NCL's own page says the region is "sparsely populated" and that "there aren't any recommended shopping areas." Plan this day around an NCL Shore Excursion, or a quiet day near the port.
- **Bahía de las Águilas.** NCL calls it "the country's most beautiful beach" and describes it as five miles long. It sits in **Jaragua National Park**, which NCL notes is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.
- **Getting there.** The Dominican tourism ministry says you reach it "by an all-terrain vehicle, or by boat from the shores of Cabo Rojo." It also says the beach has "no hotels, shops, or restaurants" and no shade, so bring water and plenty of sun protection. An NCL Shore Excursion arranges the boat for you.
- **Money.** The Dominican peso. Ask before you pay in dollars.
- **Emergency number:** 911. The UK government notes that 911 "does not have full coverage across the country."

This site doesn't have a Cabo Rojo page yet, so this pack doesn't say more than NCL and the sources above do.

### Day 5 — Friday, December 3 — Aruba (Oranjestad) {#day-aruba}

**In port:** 11:00 AM to 8:00 PM. **All aboard 7:00 PM.**

A late-morning arrival and an evening departure, so you have the afternoon and the sunset.

- **The terminal.** Aruba's tourism office says Main Street is "a ten-minute walk from the cruise terminal" and Surfside Beach a 15-minute walk, and that "all of Aruba's beaches are public and free." The Aruba Ports Authority lists Fort Zoutman and the historical museum within walking distance.
- **Arikok National Park.** Aruba's tourism office says "nearly 20% of Aruba is a designated National Park," and that visitors "need to purchase a ticket" to enter. No official page gives the entry fee, and travel guides disagree ($11 or $22 a person), so check at the gate. The Aruba Conservation Foundation, which runs the park, lists guided hikes at $25, but they start between 6:00 and 8:00 AM, before your ship arrives. Travel guides also say the park closes in mid-afternoon, so if you go, go straight from the ship.
- **Money.** Aruba's tourism office says the florin is the currency but "U.S. dollars are widely accepted," you may get change in florins, and some businesses won't take US $50 or $100 bills.
- **Emergency numbers:** 911 for ambulance and fire, **100 for police** (UK government travel advice).
- **Arrival card.** The sources don't agree. Aruba's tourism site calls its online ED card "a mandatory requirement for all individuals... entering Aruba," but its own page for cruise visitors doesn't mention it, and a January 2025 Travel Market Report guide says it "does not... apply to cruise passengers." Watch the NCL app; if a form is needed, NCL will ask.

### Day 6 — Saturday, December 4 — Curaçao (Willemstad) {#day-curacao}

**In port:** 7:00 AM to 7:00 PM. **All aboard 6:00 PM.**

A long day in a walkable old town.

- **The Queen Emma Bridge.** Curaçao's tourism site says it was built in 1888 and swings open to let ships into the harbor. While it's open, a small ferry called the ponchi carries people across free.
- **The pier.** The Curaçao Ports Authority says ships dock at the Mega Pier or the Otrobanda wharves on St. Anna Bay.
- **Money.** Curaçao's currency is now the **Caribbean guilder (XCG)**, which took over on March 31, 2025. The old Netherlands Antillean guilder stopped being legal tender on July 1, 2025 (Deutsche Bundesbank notice).
- **Emergency numbers:** 911 for police and fire, **912 for ambulance** (UK government travel advice).
- **Arrival card.** Curaçao's 2019 notice on its online immigration card describes it as required for "any person traveling to Curaçao by air." It says nothing about cruise passengers. Watch the app for anything NCL asks you to fill in.

### Day 7 — Sunday, December 5 — At sea

The second sea day. A good day for the Thermal Suite, a show, or a long lunch.

### Day 8 — Monday, December 6 — Falmouth, Jamaica {#day-falmouth}

**In port:** 7:00 AM to 5:00 PM. **All aboard 4:00 PM.**

- **The pier and the town.** The Jamaican government says the Falmouth cruise pier was developed by the Port Authority of Jamaica in the town itself, that Falmouth is "renowned for its beautiful Georgian architecture," and that the town was declared a national monument on August 21, 1996. More on our [Falmouth port page](/ports/falmouth.html).
- **Money.** The Jamaican dollar. Ask before you pay in US dollars.
- **Emergency numbers:** **119 for police, 110 for ambulance and fire** (UK government travel advice). The US Embassy in Kingston's after-hours line is 1 (876) 702-6000.
- **Travel advisory.** The US State Department rates Jamaica Level 2, "exercise increased caution" (June 2026). The companion's Alerts tab keeps this current.

### Day 9 — Tuesday, December 7 — Grand Cayman (George Town) {#day-grand-cayman}

**In port:** 7:00 AM to 5:00 PM. **All aboard 4:00 PM.**

- **It's a tender port.** George Town has no cruise pier: the Port Authority of the Cayman Islands sought bids for one in 2017, and travel guides describe ships anchoring and small boats carrying passengers ashore. Our [Grand Cayman port page](/ports/grand-cayman.html) has more. The last tender back leaves before all-aboard time. The ship announces it, so **listen for it and leave yourself extra time.**
- **If you use a wheelchair or walk with difficulty,** ask NCL about tendering before you book anything ashore.
- **Money.** The Cayman Islands dollar. Cayman's tourism office says "we use U.S. currency interchangeably."
- **Emergency number:** 911 (UK government travel advice).

### Day 10 — Wednesday, December 8 — At sea

The last sea day. Check your onboard account in the app, settle anything you want to dispute, and pack your bags.

### Day 11 — Thursday, December 9 — Miami (debarkation)

The ship is due in at 7:00 AM. Keep your passport, medications and a change of clothes with you. The ship isn't due in until 7:00 AM, so don't book an early-morning flight.

## Section 3 — Pre-Cruise Countdown {#section-3}

### Now (more than a year out)

- **Check your documents.** For a round trip from a US port, NCL accepts a valid passport, or a state-certified US birth certificate with a government photo ID. NCL also says it "strongly recommends all guests to obtain a passport." If your passport would expire around the trip, renew it.
- **Buy travel insurance** when you book. Medical care on board is billed to you.
- **Look at a Solo cabin** before a regular one. See [Section 1](#section-1).

### When NCL opens your bookings

- **Specialty dinners**, if Free at Sea gives you four.
- **Shore excursions.** Cabo Rojo is the day where an organized trip matters most.
- **Great Tides Waterpark** tickets, if you want them.

### When NCL opens online check-in

- **Check in** in the NCL app, and choose your boarding time.
- **Book your flight in for Sunday, November 28, 2027.** It's the Sunday after Thanksgiving, so book early.

### The week before

- **Fill in the handoff card** in [Section 6](#section-6) and give it to one person at home.
- **Count down** with the [countdown tool](/countdown.html), if that helps.

## Section 4 — Packing List {#section-4}

This list fits **early December in the southern Caribbean**: warm days, a chance of showers, six port days and three sea days. The [packing lists](/packing-lists.html) page has more.

### Documents and money

- **Passport** (or the birth certificate and photo ID NCL allows)
- A copy of your passport, kept somewhere else
- Your insurance policy and its emergency number, printed
- Two cards, kept in different places
- Some small US bills for taxis and tips ashore

### Carry-on

- All medications, in their original bottles
- Phone, charger, power bank
- Refillable water bottle
- Swimsuit and a change of clothes

### Daytime

- Swimsuits and a cover-up
- Light shirts and shorts
- A light sweater for the air conditioning
- A light rain jacket
- Sunglasses, hat, reef-safe sunscreen

### Evenings

NCL's restaurant dress code: "shirts and footwear required," and "swimwear should be saved for the pool." Pack whatever makes you comfortable at dinner within that.

### Footwear

- Walking shoes for Willemstad and Falmouth
- Water shoes for the beaches
- Sandals

### Wine

NCL lets you carry on bottles of wine and champagne, but not box wine or other alcohol. Its corkage fee is $15 per 750 ml bottle, waived if you have a drink package.

### What the person at home should do

If they don't hear from you by the check-in time:

1. **Don't panic.** A missed message isn't, by itself, a sign of trouble.
2. **Wait for the next check-in.** Then try your phone and messages.
3. **Call NCL's ship number** above. NCL says it's paid by card.
4. **If it's serious,** call the State Department: +1 (888) 407-4747. US embassy sites say Americans who need help can call it 24/7.

## Section 7 — Weather and Seasickness {#section-7}

### Weather

Below are typical figures for November 29 to December 9, worked out from five years of weather records (2020 to 2024) for each stop. They're averages, not a forecast. The companion app shows a real forecast as the dates get close.

| Stop | Typical high | Typical low | Days with rain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Miami | 78°F | 65°F | 20% |
| Great Stirrup Cay | 77°F | 74°F | 15% |
| Cabo Rojo | 87°F | 72°F | 16% |
| Aruba | 85°F | 79°F | 60% |
| Curaçao | 85°F | 78°F | 58% |
| Falmouth | 83°F | 73°F | 47% |
| Grand Cayman | 83°F | 78°F | 42% |

"Days with rain" counts any day with more than 1 mm (about 0.04 inch) of rain at that point, so a short shower counts the same as a wet day. Read it as how often you might see rain, not how often a day is spoiled. Cabo Rojo has the warmest afternoons, and Aruba and Curaçao are the most likely to see a shower. Miami is the coolest at night.

These come from Open-Meteo's historical weather records, at the grid point the records use for each stop. For small islands that point can sit over the water, which keeps the night-time lows close to the highs.

**Hurricanes.** NOAA says the season ends November 30, the day after you sail. NCL's own terms say "itineraries are subject to change without notice."

### Seasickness

Nobody can promise how your body will feel at sea. If you're prone to seasickness:

- **Think about where your cabin is.** A ship pitches and rolls around its middle, so a cabin low and near the middle moves the least.
- **Ask your doctor or pharmacist** about meclizine, dimenhydrinate or a prescription scopolamine patch, which the CDC names, and try anything new at home first.
- **Ginger and acupressure bands:** the CDC says ginger has "only weak and contradictory supporting evidence," and that bands are "no more effective than placebo" in laboratory trials.
- **Fresh air and a view of the horizon:** the CDC says a visible horizon gives "some protection" on ships, and suggests fresh air as a distraction.
- **The ship's medical center** can treat seasickness. It bills you. Medicare's own guide says it "doesn't cover health care services you get when the ship is more than 6 hours away from a U.S. port," so check what your plan covers.

## Section 8 — Practical Logistics {#section-8}

### Phones

- **At sea:** use the ship's Wi-Fi, and turn off cellular data so you don't pay for the ship's network.
- **In port:** every stop on this trip is outside the US. Some US plans include these islands, some charge a daily fee. **Check with your carrier before you go.**

### Money

- **Aboard:** US dollars, on your shipboard account (NCL).
- **Cabo Rojo:** the Dominican peso.
- **Aruba:** the Aruban florin; US dollars "widely accepted" (Aruba's tourism office).
- **Curaçao:** the Caribbean guilder, since March 31, 2025.
- **Falmouth:** the Jamaican dollar.
- **Grand Cayman:** the Cayman Islands dollar; US dollars used "interchangeably" (Cayman's tourism office).

### Travel advisories (US State Department)

Read October 2026:

- **The Bahamas:** Level 2, "exercise increased caution" (July 2026).
- **Dominican Republic:** Level 2, "exercise increased caution" (June 2025).
- **Aruba:** Level 1, "exercise normal precautions" (August 2026).
- **Curaçao:** Level 1, "exercise normal precautions" (September 2026).
- **Jamaica:** Level 2, "exercise increased caution" (June 2026).
- **Cayman Islands:** Level 1, "exercise normal precautions" (May 2026).

Levels change. The companion app's Alerts tab shows the current level for each, refreshed daily.

### Laundry, smoking, and the rules

- **Laundry.** Not self-service. Use the order form in your cabin; express same-day service is available.
- **Smoking** in your cabin or on your balcony carries a **$250 cleaning charge**. NCL allows cigar smoking in the cigar lounge, cigarettes in the casino for active players, and smoking outdoors on Deck 13, forward, port side.
- **Drinking age:** 21 on sailings from the US.

### Accessibility

If you use a wheelchair or a scooter, or need other help, tell NCL when you book. Grand Cayman is a tender port. Great Stirrup Cay now has a pier, but check the app that day (see [Day 2](#day-great-stirrup-cay)). The [accessibility guide](/disability-at-sea.html) on this site covers more.

## Section 9 — The Solo Cruise Rhythm {#section-9}

Ten nights alone at sea has a rhythm, and you get to set it.

- **Pick one anchor a day.** A port walk, a show, a dinner. The rest can stay loose.
- **Eat where it's easy.** The Garden Café buffet, and O'Sheehan's, which NCL lists as open 24 hours, let you eat on your own schedule.
- **Use the sea days.** Day 3, Day 7 and Day 10 are yours: the spa, a book, a long lunch.
- **One message home a day.** Tell your person where you're going ashore, and that you're back aboard.

## Section 10 — Sailing Solo on NCL {#section-10}

- **Solo cabins.** Jewel has Solo Inside, Solo Oceanview and Solo Balcony cabins, where NCL says "double occupancy rates don't apply."
- **No Studio Lounge on Jewel.** NCL's solo lounge comes with Studio cabins, and Jewel has none.
- **Solo meetups.** In 2023 NCL said solo cruisers "can enjoy activities scheduled for single travelers to mix and mingle." NCL's current pages don't say whether that happens on Jewel. On cruise forums, solo travelers describe meetups listed in the ship's daily schedule on many NCL ships, often in the early evening, and say they're as good as the people who show up. **Look in the daily schedule on your first day.** Before you go, Cruise Critic's message boards have a Roll Calls section with a thread for each sailing date, where people on the same cruise can say hello.
- **Excursions.** NCL's Shore Excursions are an easy way to spend a port day with other people, especially at Cabo Rojo.

**Safety, the same as at home:** tell someone where you're going ashore, keep your drink in sight, and be back early.

The [solo cruising hub](/solo.html) has more from people who've done it.
