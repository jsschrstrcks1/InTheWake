The itinerary calls at Puerto Plata (Amber Cove), St. Thomas, Tortola, and Great Stirrup Cay. It’s a hosted solo cruisers group cruise organized by Pam Walters.

Pam is your on-ship host. Communication for this group runs through the Thanksgiving Group Cruise Host Pam Facebook chat — join before sailing for pre-cruise logistics, the sailaway gathering, group dinner timing, and the daily where-are-we-meeting rhythm. Once aboard, Pam is the in-person organizer. For booking questions and what’s bundled with your group rate, contact your travel agent.

A note on the Thanksgiving angle. This sailing is built around the holiday. NCL serves a full Thanksgiving feast on Thursday Nov 26 — traditional turkey, stuffing, sweet potatoes, pumpkin pie — typically across all main dining rooms with no extra charge. Specialty restaurants stay open with their regular menus if you’d rather have steak or Italian. For solo cruisers, Thanksgiving aboard is often quieter and warmer than Thanksgiving alone at home — that’s part of why solo cruisers book this sailing.

A note on Friday-to-Friday. Most cruise weeks run Saturday-to-Saturday. NCL repositions Escape’s schedule to Friday-to-Friday this week so that Thanksgiving lands mid-itinerary rather than on debark day. Plan flights and pre-cruise hotels accordingly.

A note on November Caribbean weather. The official Atlantic hurricane season runs through November 30, but the late-November risk is low; the season’s peak is September and early October. November typically delivers calmer seas and lower humidity than peak summer cruising. Bring a light rain layer; otherwise, this is typically a calm Caribbean week. Section 1 — Your Ship: Norwegian Escape

The lead Breakaway Plus ship — eleven years on

Norwegian Escape was delivered October 22, 2015 by Meyer Werft at Papenburg, Germany — the lead ship of NCL’s Breakaway Plus class (the larger evolution of the Breakaway class). Her sisters arrived later: Norwegian Joy (2017), Norwegian Bliss (2018), and Norwegian Encore (2019). By your November 2026 sailing she’s eleven years into service: settled, with crew rhythms long established.

She was christened November 9, 2015 at PortMiami by Pitbull (Armando Christian Pérez) — the Miami-born rapper who became the first male godfather ever bestowed in cruise-ship christening history. The convention had been “godmother” for centuries — NCL broke it with Escape. Quietly distinctive in cruise lore.

If you’ve sailed Norwegian Bliss or Norwegian Encore, Escape is a near-identical Breakaway Plus hull and the same general layout with a few refinements that distinguish her from the later sisters. The Alaska-themed Wyland hull mural on Bliss is replaced on Escape by Guy Harvey’s marlin-and-billfish hull artwork — visible the moment the ship is at the pier. If you’re new to Breakaway Plus, the Ship Size Atlas shows how she compares against the newer Prima Plus class and other megaships.

What’s distinctive about Escape

  • The Guy Harvey hull artwork — Escape’s hull mural depicts a leaping marlin, sea turtle, swordfish, and other Caribbean game fish. Visible from any pier shot. Guy Harvey is a Caribbean marine artist; the partnership is fitting for an Eastern Caribbean ship.
  • The Sky Walk ropes course — a three-story rope-and-plank course running the width of the upper decks, with two narrow planks that extend eight feet over the side of the ship and five Sky Rail zip tracks that fly over open ocean. At launch in 2015 NCL marketed this as the largest ropes course at sea — Escape’s defining outdoor signature.
  • The Aqua Park — at launch, marketed as the largest at sea: the Aqua Racer (twin-tube racing slides), Free Fall slides (steep enclosed drops), The Whip (a tubular spiral slide), two pools, four hot tubs, and a dedicated kids’ area.
  • Spice H2O (Deck 17 aft) — the adults-only open-air lounge at the stern; the open-air viewing spot on this ship. Escape has no forward Observation Lounge, so the forward-lounge advice from other NCL packs does not apply here.
  • Vibe Beach Club — adults-only enclave with private pool, two hot tubs, dedicated bar, shaded loungers. ~$99/day or $399/week pre-cruise.
  • The Haven by Norwegian — ship-within-a-ship suite enclave with private restaurant, lounge, pool, and butler service.
  • Pincho Tapas Bar (Deck 8) — a small Spanish tapas room; specialty dining at small-bites cadence.

What’s NOT on Escape

  • No go-kart track / Speedway — NCL introduced the at-sea Speedway with Norwegian Joy in 2017, two years after Escape. The later three Breakaway Plus sisters (Joy, Bliss, Encore) all got the two-level Speedway; Escape did not.
  • No Indulge Food Hall — that’s a Prima-class signature. Escape predates the Prima Class by seven years.
  • No Penrose Atrium — also Prima-class.
  • No Aqua Slidecoaster — Prima Plus only (Aqua, Luna).

Cabin categories — the solo calculus

The group flyer shows three price points: Solo Studio $1,249, Ocean View $1,569, Balcony $1,839. Honest reads:

  • Solo Studio at $1,249 — NCL’s signature solo offering. Escape has 82 dedicated Studio cabins — one of the largest Studio inventories on any cruise ship. Roughly 100 sq ft, windowless, with no single supplement and Studio Lounge access (free coffee, social space, hosted hours, scheduled meet-and-greets for solo cruisers). The Studio Lounge is the daily social anchor; solos cluster there independent of the group meetups. At $1,249, this is the price-leader for solo travel.
  • Ocean View at $1,569 — a window cabin, daylight in the morning, the world feels different. The $320 differential buys real space + a window. The value pick for most solo cruisers.
  • Balcony at $1,839 — a private outdoor space, which on a Caribbean cruise matters more than on Alaska. Sea-day sunrises, port-day return-from-shore decompression, evenings with a book. At a $270 differential over Ocean View, the splurge that pencils out if the math works.

Before you commit, run the stateroom sanity check — a four-question filter that catches the bookings most likely to leave a cruiser unhappy. Studios screen well for confident sleepers; the filter flags the few cases where the windowless interior is a problem. Once chosen, the cabin organization guide covers small-space pragmatics.

Dining

Escape’s dining map:

Included with your fare (no extra charge):

  • Manhattan Room (Deck 7 aft) — main dining room with a small dance floor and live music most evenings. Most atmospheric of the included rooms.
  • Taste and Savor (Deck 6) — paired main dining rooms; same menu, different room atmosphere.
  • Garden Café (Deck 16) — buffet; long hours; upper-deck view earns its keep on sea days.
  • O’Sheehan’s Neighborhood Bar & Grill (Deck 7) — pub food and classics around the clock; the fallback for any hour. (This is the venue that goes by The Local on Escape’s younger sisters.)

Specialty dining (extra charge or via Specialty Dining Package):

  • Cagney’s Steakhouse — NCL’s standard steakhouse; reliably good.
  • Le Bistro — French; the most romantic specialty room on NCL ships.
  • Teppanyaki — Japanese hibachi; the showy room; group-friendly seating.
  • Q Texas Smokehouse — Texas BBQ.
  • Los Lobos — modern Mexican.
  • Ocean Blue — Geoffrey Zakarian-partnered seafood (verify whether running in 2026).
  • La Cucina — Italian.
  • Food Republic — pan-Asian small plates; one of NCL’s most distinctive specialty rooms. Worth the visit even if you’re not a sushi-and-pho person.
  • Pincho Tapas Bar — Spanish tapas; small bites and wine.

Drinks. NCL’s drink-package math: the Premium Beverage Package is commonly included in the “Free at Sea” promotional bundle when booking. If your group fare included Free at Sea, drinks may already be covered — verify what’s bundled with your booking agent. Otherwise the package runs ~$109/day per person + 20% gratuity at pre-cruise rates; breaks even at ~5–6 drinks per day. The drink-calculator lets you put in your real consumption pattern and see the breakeven. The companion article Is the drink package worth it? walks through worked examples.

The honest first-day mistake to avoid: Don’t sign up for an unlimited drink package at the front desk on Day 1 unless you’ve already done the drink-calculator math. Decide before you board, ideally as part of your Free At Sea bundle. Mid-sail upgrades cost more per day and give you fewer days of usage.

Connectivity at sea

NCL’s Wi-Fi packages are tier-based and frequently bundled into Free at Sea. The internet at sea guide covers cross-line baselines and how to avoid cellular roaming charges in port.

For most solo cruisers in this group:

  • Use included basic Wi-Fi if your fare bundles any
  • Don’t try to stream video at sea
  • Use port-day cellular when possible (DR uses Claro/Altice; USVI uses US carriers normally; BVI uses Flow/Digicel; expect no usable cellular at Great Stirrup Cay)
  • Keep one trusted person ashore informed via a daily one-line message — see Section 6

Section 2 — Day by Day

The day-by-day below uses the typical NCL Escape Eastern Caribbean rhythm. Verify your specific times in the NCL app — the published sequence sometimes shifts between sailings.

This is not your plan. What follows is one possible shape of the week, not a schedule to keep. Only a few things on it are actually fixed: the muster drill, sailaway, the ship’s arrival and all-aboard times in each port, and anything you have already booked. Everything else is a suggestion you are free to ignore, reorder, or sleep through. A day spent entirely on your balcony is not a day wasted, and nothing here is a commitment you have made to anyone.

Day 1 — Friday, November 20 — Port Canaveral (embarkation)

Terminal: Port Canaveral, Cruise Terminal 10 (NCL’s typical Central Florida berth — verify in the NCL app 14 days before sailing).

Getting to Port Canaveral:

  • Orlando International (MCO) → Port Canaveral: Uber/Lyft $60–80, 45–60 min. Shared cruise shuttle ~$30/person, slower.
  • If driving: Port Canaveral garage ~$17–22/day (significantly cheaper than PortMiami’s $35); reservable in advance via the Port Canaveral website.
  • Embark window: Typically 11:30 AM – 3:30 PM. All-aboard 3:30 PM. Ship departs ~4:00–5:00 PM.

Pre-cruise hotel — strongly recommended. Same-day flights to a 3:30 PM Friday all-aboard are risky given Thanksgiving-week travel volume. Two viable bases:

  • Cocoa Beach (15 min from port) — beach town, walking distance to the pier and beach. Right call if you want to be near the port.
  • Orlando MCO area (45 min from port) — more hotel options, longer Friday-morning transfer.

Thursday Nov 19 — fly into MCO. This is the day before your Friday embarkation — and, usefully, a full week ahead of the Thanksgiving travel crush (which peaks on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving, Nov 25, while you’re already at sea). Still, book flights early and consider arriving Wednesday Nov 18 for extra buffer. Pam will confirm any pre-cruise gathering plans in the Facebook group chat.

The realistic Day 1 timeline:

  • 11:00 AM: Terminal opens. NCL online check-in via the NCL app — complete it before arrival to skip a 20-minute queue.
  • 12:00 PM: Aboard. Cabins typically open by 1:30 PM; until then, lunch at the buffet (Garden Café, Deck 16) or O’Sheehan’s (Deck 7).
  • 1:30 PM: Cabin open. Meet your stateroom steward.
  • 3:00 PM: Mandatory muster drill — partly app-based (watch the safety video in the NCL app) plus an in-person check-in at your assigned station.
  • 4:00 PM: Sailaway. Pam’s group gathers at a spot she names in the Facebook group chat; on Escape the natural sailaway spots are Spice H2O (Deck 17 aft, adults-only, open air) and The Waterfront (Deck 8, the outdoor promenade). Both are less crowded than the main pool deck.
  • 5:30 – 8:00 PM: Dinner. Pam’s nightly group dinner is at the Manhattan Room (or one of the main dining rooms) at 5:30 PM — verify in the Facebook group chat. Show up to whichever nights work for you.
  • Later evening: NCL hosts a Studio meet-and-greet sometime on Day 1 in the Studio Lounge — check the NCL app’s daily program.

What to pack in your carry-on for Day 1: swimsuit (pools open before checked bags arrive), light layer (Florida November evenings can be 65–75°F outdoors but ship interior runs ~72°F), all medications, change of clothes, passport (required — DR, USVI, BVI are foreign or special-jurisdiction; passport is the cleanest documentation), phone charger, refillable water bottle. Your checked bags may not arrive until 7–9 PM.

Money/card: Your NCL keycard is your ID, room key, and onboard charge. Currency aboard: US Dollar.

Day 2 — Saturday, November 21 — Cruising at sea

The settling-in day. The ship is heading southeast across the Florida Straits and Gulf Stream toward the Dominican Republic.

Morning:

  • The Waterfront (Deck 8) at sunrise — quiet at 6 AM with coffee.
  • Breakfast at Garden Café (Deck 16) — upper-deck windows, sea view. Or sit-down at Manhattan Room for a longer breakfast.
  • Studio Lounge if you booked Studio — hosted Day 2 coffee hour is the NCL solo-cruiser tradition.

Mid-morning to lunch — pick ONE:

  • The Sky Walk ropes course — first hour after it opens has the shortest queues. Free.
  • Aqua Park — the Aqua Racer, Free Fall slides, The Whip. All free.
  • Mandara Spa thermal-suite day pass — sea-day intro rates appear. ~90 minutes is the right dose.
  • Port talk for Puerto Plata — NCL schedules a Day-2 briefing on tomorrow’s port. Worth attending if it’s your first time at Amber Cove.

Afternoon:

  • Pool deck or Vibe Beach Club — if you bought the Vibe pass, this is the day.
  • Trivia, live music, activities — check the daily program.
  • Specialty dining reservations — if you didn’t book online pre-cruise, walk up to a host stand mid-afternoon for late-day openings. The 7:30 – 8:30 PM slots fill first.

Evening — group dinner.

Pam’s standard 5:30 PM group dinner. The Manhattan Room seats groups easily.

Day 3 — Sunday, November 22 — Puerto Plata (Amber Cove), Dominican Republic

Arrival: Approximately 8:00 AM. Depart: 5:00 PM. Escape docks at Amber Cove — Carnival Corporation’s purpose-built cruise port complex on the Dominican Republic’s north coast, about 30 minutes from the city of Puerto Plata.

The shape of Puerto Plata / Amber Cove. Amber Cove is the cruise-port complex; Puerto Plata is the actual city. Most cruisers stay at Amber Cove and never see Puerto Plata; some venture inland. Amber Cove has a beach, multiple pools, a zip line, restaurants, shops, and a paid waterpark. The complex was built specifically for cruise visitors and is highly developed. If you want a low-stress “stay near the ship” day, Amber Cove delivers.

Free with your cruise card (at Amber Cove):

  • Multiple pool zones (free; first-come for chairs)
  • Beach access (small but real)
  • Shops and the duty-free zone
  • Restaurants for purchase (most affordable in USD)

Paid (decide ahead):

  • Damajagua Waterfalls — the inland excursion that justifies leaving Amber Cove. A series of 27 cascades you can climb and slide through. Ship excursion ~$95/person; independent operators ~$50–60. 4–5 hour day total. Wet shoes required.
  • Puerto Plata City Tour — visit the colonial fortress (San Felipe), the amber museum, the malecón waterfront. Half-day; ~$40–60 with transport.
  • Ocean World Adventure Park — dolphins, sea lions, tigers, sharks. ~$70 entry; controversial ethics for some travelers (research before booking).
  • Mount Isabel de Torres cable car — short ride to the top of the mountain with the Christ statue, botanical gardens, panoramic views. ~$25.
  • Cofresí Beach — public beach 15 minutes from Amber Cove; quieter than the cruise-port crowd.

For first-time Puerto Plata: if it’s your first Caribbean DR call, Damajagua Waterfalls is the experience that justifies leaving the cruise port. If you’d rather stay calm, Amber Cove’s pools + beach + lunch is a fine day.

Pack for Puerto Plata: swimsuit, water shoes (Damajagua requires them), sunscreen (SPF 30+), sun hat, light cover-up, $40–60 USD cash in small bills (USD widely accepted; Dominican peso not necessary), insect repellent for inland excursions.

Worth a look on the way in: the Amber Cove port page. Build your day in the Port Day Planner; record what actually happened in the Port Logbook on the way back.

Day 4 — Monday, November 23 — St. Thomas, US Virgin Islands

Arrival: Approximately 10:00 AM. Depart: 6:00 PM. Escape typically docks at Crown Bay or Havensight (WICO) — both in the harbor at Charlotte Amalie. Crown Bay is ~2 miles west of downtown; Havensight is at the eastern end of downtown.

What St. Thomas is. Charlotte Amalie is one of the busiest cruise ports in the Caribbean; on a busy day several large ships share the harbor. Downtown is well-organized for the cruise crowd: duty-free shopping (jewelry is the marquee category; St. Thomas was the duty-free engine of US Caribbean commerce for decades), the historic Synagogue, Blackbeard’s Castle (overpriced, climb-the-stairs), and ferries to the surrounding islands.

Free, walkable (from Havensight; Crown Bay requires shuttle/taxi):

  • Downtown Charlotte Amalie — Main Street is the duty-free spine; Government Hill has the historic homes; the Synagogue (1833 — the second-oldest in continuous use in the Western Hemisphere) is worth a quiet half-hour.
  • The waterfront walking path — quiet, scenic.

Paid (decide ahead):

  • Magens Bay — the beach on most lists of the Caribbean’s best. ~$12–17 r/t per person via taxi-shuttle; $5 entry. The standard St. Thomas beach day.
  • Sapphire Beach — quieter alternative, ~$15 r/t taxi; better snorkeling at the east end.
  • Mountain Top — short drive to the highest point on the island; the harbor view that ends up on most postcards. Banana daiquiri tradition. ~$25 r/t.
  • Ferry to St. John — Cruz Bay ferries leave Red Hook every 30 min; ~$8 each way. Trunk Bay beach in St. John’s national park is one of the Caribbean’s signature beaches.
  • Skyride to Paradise Point — gondola from Havensight; ~$24 r/t.
  • Coral World Ocean Park — sea-life encounters; ~$25 entry.

For first-time St. Thomas: Magens Bay or the ferry to St. John are the two great calls. Magens is the standard St. Thomas beach day; St. John’s Trunk Bay is the “this is the Caribbean of postcards” experience but takes longer (the ferry adds an hour each way).

Pack for St. Thomas: swimsuit, water shoes, reef-safe sunscreen, sun hat, $60–80 USD cash, snorkel gear if you have your own.

Worth a look: the St. Thomas port page, its gallery, and the St. Thomas logbook.

Day 5 — Tuesday, November 24 — Tortola, British Virgin Islands

Arrival: Approximately 8:00 AM. Depart: 5:00 PM. Escape docks at Tortola Pier Park in Road Town, the BVI capital — directly adjacent to the cruise terminal complex, no tender required.

What Tortola is. The largest of the British Virgin Islands; population ~24,000; a British Overseas Territory still using the US Dollar despite the British flag. The BVI is quieter than St. Thomas — fewer ships, fewer aggressive vendors, a more deliberate pace. The island is mountainous; the postcard destinations are reached by safari taxi over winding roads, not on foot.

Free, walkable from the pier:

  • Road Town — Main Street walkable; the J.R. O’Neal Botanic Gardens (~10-min walk, ~$5 entry — quietly excellent); the old courthouse and government complex.
  • Pier Park — a planned cruise-port shopping/dining complex; restaurants and shops at the gangway.

Paid (decide ahead):

  • Cane Garden Bay — one of the BVI’s signature beaches; a curve of white sand backed by green hills. ~$20–30 taxi each way. Calm water, restaurants, bars. For most cruisers in Tortola, this is the day.
  • The Baths on Virgin Gorda — the postcard of the BVI. Massive granite boulders forming sea caves and natural pools you swim through. Requires a ferry from Tortola to Virgin Gorda + taxi. A full half-day; with the 5:00 PM all-aboard there’s more margin here than on shorter Tortola calls. ~$150–200/person all in.
  • Sage Mountain National Park — Tortola’s high point and rainforest. Hiking; cool temperatures; less-crowded. Half-day excursion.
  • Sailing or snorkeling day trip — catamarans to Norman Island or the BVI’s small reef islands. $130–180/person; standout for water-lovers.

For first-time Tortola: Cane Garden Bay is the right call if it’s a beach day. If you’ve already done a Caribbean-beach excursion this trip, The Baths on Virgin Gorda is the postcard.

Weather note for late November: Tortola in November runs 78–86°F daytime, low rain risk, steady trade winds. Brief afternoon showers possible but the wet stretch (Aug–Nov) is winding down. Comfortable beach day weather. The seasonal panel and live forecast at the Tortola port page are the right read 24 hours before docking.

Currency: US Dollar (despite the British flag).

For accessibility: Pier Park is accessible. Most off-port excursions involve open-air safari taxis and unpaved beach approaches.

Worth a look: the Tortola port page, its gallery, and the Tortola logbook.

Day 6 — Wednesday, November 25 — Cruising at sea

Second sea day; the recovery day before Thanksgiving + Great Stirrup Cay tomorrow.

Morning ideas:

  • Sunrise from The Waterfront or Spice H2O — quiet before the pool deck wakes up.
  • Studio Lounge if you’re in a Studio.
  • Aqua Park slides — second-sea-day lines are settled.
  • Sky Walk ropes course — if Day 2 was too crowded; clearer skies and steady trade winds on a Caribbean sea day make this the better day to go up.

Afternoon:

  • The Pool deck with a book. Vibe Beach Club if you bought the pass.
  • Port talk for Great Stirrup Cay — NCL covers the new Great Tides Waterpark logistics on a Wednesday briefing typically; worth attending.
  • Specialty lunch — if Le Bistro is serving lunch that day (check the program), it is quieter than dinner; Cagney’s is also worth a walk-up.

Evening — the Thanksgiving prep night.

The night before Thanksgiving usually runs as a normal smart-casual evening. The next 24 hours are the holiday peak — many cruisers use this evening to make any last calls home before the cellular dark zone at Great Stirrup Cay.

Pam’s group dinner — Manhattan Room 5:30 PM standard.

Day 7 — Thursday, November 26 — Great Stirrup Cay, Bahamas (THANKSGIVING)

Arrival: Approximately 9:00 AM. Depart: 5:00 PM. Escape docks at the cay’s new multi-ship pier completed in late 2025; no tendering on a normal day. If weather closes the pier, NCL runs tenders instead, and the app will say so that morning.

What Great Stirrup Cay is right now (November 2026). NCL leased Great Stirrup Cay in 1977 — the first cruise line in the industry to take exclusive use of a private island in the Caribbean — and bought it outright in 1986. As of November 2026 the cay is in a substantially-expanded state: the headline new attraction, Great Tides Waterpark, opened September 4, 2026 — almost three months before your sailing. You’ll come ashore at the new Welcome Plaza, the arrival area at the pier, and the cay is the most developed it has ever been.

A soft-opening caveat. Great Tides opened eleven weeks before this sailing; large-scale private-island construction often carries small punch-list items past opening day. Treat the beach + BBQ lunch + pool zones as the reliable core of your cay day; treat any specific waterpark attraction as a bonus that may or may not be running the day you visit. Verify status in the NCL app the morning of the cay call before you commit to a paid slot.

Great Tides Waterpark (per NCL’s May 2026 marketing booklet):

  • Great Life Lagoon — a 28,000-square-foot pool with swim-up bars; the new social center of the cay. No upcharge marker in NCL’s own materials, so it should be included; NCL publishes no pricing outside the app, so confirm there before you budget.
  • Tidal Tower (paid) — racing slides, sea-themed.
  • Wandering River (paid) — lazy river with surprise elements.
  • Cliffside Cove (paid) — platform-jumping into clear pools. Height/swim ability requirements.
  • Splash Cay (paid) — high-energy splash pad for younger kids. (NCL’s island map labels this zone “Splash Harbor” — same area; naming still settling.)
  • Grotto Bar — cocktail bar inside the family-pad zone, so adults can drink while kids play.
  • 19 slides across the park.

Waterpark access requires a reservation; book in the NCL for Me app pre-cruise ideally 60+ days out. Thanksgiving sailings sell out earlier than typical.

Free with your cruise fare:

  • Beach access — multiple beach zones across the cay.
  • BBQ buffet lunch — at the open-air pavilion; jerk chicken, ribs, salads, hot dogs, burgers. The Thanksgiving feast also runs onboard tonight after the cay closes — see Section 12.
  • Lounge chairs and umbrellas — first-come; come ashore early for shaded ones.
  • Great Life Lagoon (the 28,000-sq-ft pool with swim-up bars) — carries no upcharge marker in NCL’s materials, so expect it to be included; confirm in the app.

Section 3 — Pre-Cruise Countdown

6+ months before — booking and big decisions

  • Confirm the booking with your travel agent. Cabin category, final price, what’s bundled with Free at Sea.

The Friday-to-Friday PTO + holiday-week travel reality

NCL repositioned Escape to Friday-to-Friday for this week so that Thanksgiving lands mid-itinerary instead of on debark day. That creates two real complications for a working solo cruiser, and the pack would be hiding the ball if it didn’t name them:

  • Friday Nov 20 is not a US federal holiday. Most employers do not give the Friday before Thanksgiving off, and Thursday Nov 26 (the actual holiday) you’re already at sea. If you take the full cruise window you’re burning four weekdays of PTO (Fri Nov 20, plus Mon Nov 23, Tue Nov 24, Wed Nov 25) since Thanksgiving Day itself is already a paid holiday at most employers, plus Friday Nov 27 (debark day) unless your employer gives the day after Thanksgiving off. That pattern is the minimum.
  • The Thanksgiving travel peak is the Wednesday before the holiday (Nov 25) and the Sunday after — and both fall while you’re at sea, so they can’t touch your outbound flight. What you’re managing on Nov 19–20 is ordinary pre-holiday-week volume plus late-November weather at whatever northern hub you connect through. The sane play: take Wednesday Nov 18 off too, fly in Wednesday afternoon, and give yourself a full buffer day. Add one day to PTO, save the entire trip from a single delayed flight cascading.
  • Working from Cocoa Beach Thursday is the compromise if your employer is flexible. Drive or fly in Wednesday, work remote from a Cocoa Beach hotel Thursday morning, then walk to the port Friday. Same number of PTO days as a non-cruise Thanksgiving week.
  • The return is Friday Nov 27 — Black Friday. Domestic MCO traffic on Black Friday afternoon is the worst of the year in either direction. Book a morning flight Friday Nov 27 if you can — anything after 1 PM is exposed to the afternoon spike. International flights have more margin (international concourses don’t crush the way domestic does), but the parking-and-rideshare bottleneck still bites.

Section 7 — Seasickness + November Caribbean Weather

Late November Eastern Caribbean is typically calmer than the summer and early-autumn weeks. The hurricane season officially ends November 30, but peak risk passed in October. The Gulf Stream and Caribbean transits this week are usually mild.

If you’re prone to motion sickness:

  • Bonine (meclizine) — once-daily, less drowsy than Dramamine.
  • Sea-bands (acupressure wristbands) — placebo or not, many find them effective.
  • Ginger (capsules, tea, candies) — mild, and many find it helps.
  • Scopolamine patches (prescription, behind-the-ear) — strongest option.
  • Stay mid-ship and low in your cabin choice. Mid-ship Deck 9–12 has the least felt motion.

If you get sick onboard: the ship’s medical center stocks IV anti-nausea medication. Bring travel insurance — onboard medical care is billed to you, and most US health plans do not cover care at sea; check yours.

Section 8 — Practical Logistics

Cell service and roaming

  • At sea — ship Wi-Fi only; turn airplane mode ON.
  • Puerto Plata — DR carriers Claro, Altice. Some US plans include the DR; verify with your carrier.
  • St. Thomas — domestic US service; works normally on most plans.
  • Tortola — BVI carriers Flow, Digicel; most US plans charge a daily roaming pass here; check yours.
  • Great Stirrup Cay — expect no usable cellular.

Currency

  • Aboard: USD.
  • Puerto Plata: USD widely accepted; Dominican peso (DOP) for off-the-tourist-track value.
  • St. Thomas: USD (US territory).
  • Tortola: USD (despite British flag).
  • Great Stirrup Cay: NCL cruise card; no cash needed on the cay.

Tipping ashore

  • Puerto Plata: 10–15% on restaurant bills, $2/person on guided tours.
  • St. Thomas / Tortola: US-standard 18–20% on restaurants; $5–10 on tour guides; $1–2/drink at bars.
  • Great Stirrup Cay: bartender tips appreciated $1–2/drink (gratuities bundled).

Onboard tipping

NCL’s auto-gratuity runs $20/day per person at most cabins; $25/day Suites/Haven. Verify with your booking agent whether your group rate has it pre-paid. Additional cash tips:

  • Stateroom attendant: $20–40 cash end of cruise
  • Specialty dining waiter: ~10–15% if outstanding
  • Bartender who remembers your name: $1–2 per drink

Section 12 — Thanksgiving Aboard

The reason this pack has a Section 12 most others don’t: the holiday is the point of this sailing. Here’s the practical Thanksgiving experience aboard Norwegian Escape.

The traditional feast (Thursday November 26 evening)

NCL serves a full Thanksgiving dinner across the main dining rooms — the Manhattan Room, Taste, and Savor — on Thanksgiving evening. Typical menu (verify in the daily program once aboard):

  • Roast turkey with stuffing, mashed potatoes, gravy
  • Cranberry sauce (typically both whole-berry and jellied)
  • Candied sweet potatoes / yams
  • Green bean casserole or roasted vegetables
  • Dinner rolls
  • Pumpkin pie, pecan pie, apple pie for dessert

No extra charge — this is part of your included main-dining-room experience.

Pam’s group typically gathers at the Manhattan Room 5:30 PM seating for the holiday meal. Verify in the Facebook group chat.

If you’d rather NOT do traditional Thanksgiving

Specialty restaurants stay open with their regular menus. Cagney’s Steakhouse, Le Bistro, Teppanyaki, and Food Republic all serve their usual menus on Thanksgiving Day. Reservations for specialty venues on Thanksgiving Day book out earliest — if you want a non-turkey night, book 60+ days pre-cruise.

The Garden Café buffet also runs a Thanksgiving spread for the lunch crowd if you’d rather have your turkey early in the day.

Lunch on Thanksgiving Day (at Great Stirrup Cay)

The cay’s BBQ buffet lunch runs as usual — jerk chicken, ribs, burgers. NCL typically adds Thanksgiving touches at the buffet too (maybe a small turkey station). Most cruisers eat lunch on the cay and dinner aboard. The shower-and-rest window between 4 PM and 5:30 PM is the unspoken rhythm of Thanksgiving Day on this sailing.

For solo cruisers specifically

Thanksgiving aboard is often quieter and warmer than Thanksgiving alone at home — this is part of why solo cruisers book holiday sailings. Pam’s group dinner is the answer to “what do I do on Thanksgiving as a single person.” The Studio Lounge crowd typically gathers there too around 4–5 PM for a “before dinner” coffee or drink.

No one will judge if you want to spend Thanksgiving evening at Spice H2O, or on your balcony, with a book and a glass of wine. That’s the version some cruisers come for.

The Thanksgiving gratitude tradition (informal)

A small but recurring solo-cruiser tradition: share what you’re grateful for with the table at the start of the Thanksgiving meal. Pam may invite this at the group dinner. If you’d rather skip, smart-casual table conversation is fine. Both rhythms work.

The 5 PM moment

The hardest minute of the day for many solo Thanksgiving cruisers is around 4:30–5:00 PM ship time. Back home in the Eastern time zone, the family is sitting down. The grandkids are at the table. The chair you usually sit in is occupied by someone else, or it’s empty. You can feel that from a ship in the Bahamas.

This is the part nobody briefs you for, and the part this pack will not pretend doesn’t happen.

Some options for that hour:

  • Be at Pam’s group dinner at 5:30 PM. The Manhattan Room with the rest of the group, most of them doing the same emotional accounting at the same hour, is the version of Thanksgiving the cruise delivers. The table is full, the conversation is light, and names get learned by dessert.
  • Step away if you need to. A solo cruiser in tears at the rail at 5 PM on Thanksgiving is not unusual and not weak. Take ten minutes. Call home on the ship’s Wi-Fi if you can; send a text if you can’t. Then come down to dinner.
  • Skip dinner entirely if that’s the answer. Room service runs around the clock (NCL charges a convenience fee on most orders; check the app); the cabin door locks; the pillow is yours. Some solo cruisers come on Thanksgiving sailings specifically because they don’t want to perform celebration. That is also a valid version of this trip.

Pam knows the rhythm of this hour. You don’t have to explain anything.
