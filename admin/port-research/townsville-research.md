## 1. The berth and getting ashore

### Which berth

Cruise ships use **Berth 10**, the port's designated cruise berth. The Port of Townsville's own operations page states cruise ships "have priority berth rights on Berth 10 if bookings are made 12 months prior to arrival." ([Port of Townsville — Cruise](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/operations/shipping/cruise/))

The port's published berth schedule confirms Berth 10's assigned uses as **Defence, Cruise, Common user**, handling containers, break bulk, motor vehicles and live cattle. ([Port of Townsville — Berth Information PDF](https://s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/os-data-2/port-townsville/documents/berth_information_website_final.pdf))

Berth 10 physical characteristics from that same sheet:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Berth pocket length | **320 m** |
| Declared depth | **12.0 m LAT** |
| Design depth | (not stated for B10 on sheet) |
| Max displacement | 50,000 t |
| Height above LAT | 5.8 m |
| Bunkering | Pipeline and truck |
| Crane | Mobile harbour crane 140 t (third-party) |

Berth 10 received an **$85 million upgrade completed in 2013**. ([Wikipedia — Port of Townsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Townsville))

### The passenger terminal — and a correction to the premise

There is **no new cruise/superyacht terminal delivered by the Channel Upgrade project.** This is worth stating plainly because the question assumed one. The Channel Upgrade was a **dredging and reclamation** project — it widened and deepened the shipping channel and reclaimed 60 ha of land. It did not build a passenger terminal. ([Port of Townsville — Channel Upgrade](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/projects-development/channel-upgrade/))

The passenger facility is the **Quayside Terminal**, which **opened in October 2013** — not new, and not a product of the Channel Upgrade. ([Townsville Enterprise — Get Cruise Ready](https://www.townsvilleenterprise.com.au/tourism/get-cruise-ready/))

Quayside Terminal specifications ([Port of Townsville — Cruise](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/operations/shipping/cruise/)):

- Processes **up to 1,000 passengers** on or off a security-regulated vessel
- Air conditioned; **free Wi-Fi**
- Undercover meet-and-greet area
- Multi-bay bus and taxi ranks
- On-site Customs and quarantine processing

The terminal did receive a **$5.9 million upgrade for the 2024–25 season**: the bus zone was expanded from **4 coaches to 12**, and a dedicated kiss-and-ride area, new seating, shade structures and landscaping were added. ([Port of Townsville — Cruise Terminal Transformation](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/townsville-cruise-terminal-transformation-supports-local-economy/); [Queensland Government Ministerial Statement](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/101038))

**Address:** Quayside Terminal, Lennon Drive, South Townsville, on the banks of Ross Creek.

### Alongside or tender — the single most important correction in this dossier

**Alongside. Not tender.** As of 2026 this is unambiguous.

Historically Townsville *was* a tender port for larger ships. The pre-upgrade rule, still repeated verbatim across tourism sites, was: vessels **up to 238 m** berthed at Quayside Terminal; **larger vessels anchored off Magnetic Island and passengers were ferried ashore by SeaLink**.

That regime **ended with the Channel Upgrade.** The $251 million project widened the channel from **92 m to 180 m at the inshore end, tapering to 120 m offshore**, and now permits **vessels up to 300 m LOA** to berth directly. ([Port of Townsville — Channel Upgrade](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/projects-development/channel-upgrade/))

Completion was marked on **Tuesday 14 January 2025** with the arrival of Cunard's **Queen Elizabeth**, at **294 m the largest ship ever to visit Townsville**, berthing alongside. ([Port of Townsville — Channel Upgrade completion](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/townsville-port-celebrates-channel-upgrade-completion-with-queen-elizabeth-s-arrival/); [Wikipedia — Port of Townsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Townsville))

Townsville Enterprise confirms the practical effect: the upgrade enables "ships as large as 300 m in length to berth directly at Quayside Terminal," **rather than requiring tender operations**. ([Townsville Enterprise](https://www.townsvilleenterprise.com.au/tourism/get-cruise-ready/))

**Caution for the guide-writer:** the Port of Townsville's own downloadable *Berth Information* PDF is **stale**. It still states: "The channel is 92 m wide and has a design depth of 11.7 m… The maximum size of a vessel accommodated by the Port is 238 m LOA, 32.2 m beam and 13.1 m draft (subject to tide)," and lists Berth 10's Max Vessel LOA as 238 m. Those are pre-upgrade figures, contradicted by the port's own news and project pages and by the physical fact of a 294 m ship berthing in January 2025. Do not cite the PDF's channel and LOA figures. ([Berth Information PDF](https://s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/os-data-2/port-townsville/documents/berth_information_website_final.pdf))

### Channel dimensions and draft

- Channel total length: **6.4 nautical miles** ([Berth Information PDF](https://s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/os-data-2/port-townsville/documents/berth_information_website_final.pdf))
- Maintained depth post-upgrade: **11.8 m**, to enable ships up to 300 m ([Maritime Safety Queensland — Townsville Pilotage/Passage Plan](https://www.msq.qld.gov.au/tmr_site_msqinternet/_/media/tmronline/msqinternet/msqfiles/home/shipping/passageplans/townsville/townsville_passage_plan.pdf))
- **Vessels with a draft over 9.20 m trigger tidal window calculations** ([MSQ Townsville passage plan](https://www.msq.qld.gov.au/tmr_site_msqinternet/_/media/tmronline/msqinternet/msqfiles/home/shipping/passageplans/townsville/townsville_passage_plan.pdf))
- Current declared channel depths are published by Queensland Government and vary with survey ([QLD Government — North QLD notices](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/boating/notices/north-qld/townsville))

### Does Volendam fit? Yes — comfortably

*Volendam* specifications ([Wikipedia — MS Volendam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_Volendam)):

| Parameter | Volendam | Townsville limit | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| LOA | **237.6 m** | 300 m | 62 m spare |
| Beam | **32.3 m** | Channel 120–180 m wide | ample |
| Draft | **8.3 m** | 11.8 m channel; 12.0 m LAT at Berth 10 | 3.5 m+ spare |
| Gross tonnage | 61,214 GT | — | — |
| Passengers / crew | 1,837 / 647 | — | — |
| Built | 1999 | — | — |

Note the brief cited ~237 m LOA and ~8.1 m draft; Wikipedia gives 237.6 m and 8.3 m. Either way the conclusion is identical and robust.

Two observations worth including in the guide:

1. **Draft 8.3 m is below the 9.20 m tidal-window trigger**, so *Volendam* should not face a tide-dependent arrival or departure window. Larger, deeper ships do.
2. **Under the pre-2024 limits, *Volendam* would have been marginal on both counts** — 237.6 m against a 238 m LOA limit, and 32.3 m beam against a stated 32.2 m beam limit. She would very likely have been a tender call, or a refusal. Post-upgrade she is a routine alongside berthing. This is a genuinely interesting detail: the Channel Upgrade is the reason this call works.

**Passenger capacity note:** Quayside Terminal processes up to 1,000 passengers. *Volendam* carries 1,837 passengers plus 647 crew. Disembarkation on a full ship will therefore be staged rather than instantaneous. UNVERIFIED whether this creates material queuing in practice.

### Walking out vs shuttle

The Port of Townsville is a **security-regulated working port**. Vessels calling must be "capable of carrying out 100% screening of all persons who board the vessel," and operations are governed by Department of Home Affairs, Department of Agriculture Fisheries and Forestry, and Australian Border Force requirements. ([Port of Townsville — Cruise](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/operations/shipping/cruise/))

Reported arrangements:

- A **free shuttle bus** runs from Quayside Terminal to the **City Bus Exchange** ([Townsville Enterprise / port guidance, via search summary](https://www.townsvilleenterprise.com.au/tourism/get-cruise-ready/))
- Quayside Terminal is **2 km from the Townsville CBD** and is described as **"within walking distance of Townsville's CBD and the Palmer Street precinct."**
- Buses and taxi bays are at the terminal.
- For ships carrying **over 2,000 passengers**, Townsville Enterprise has arranged **trial hop-on hop-off buses** connecting key attractions. Critically: **"The cruise liner determines the bus exchange location and whether passengers are required to pay for transport."** ([Townsville Enterprise](https://www.townsvilleenterprise.com.au/tourism/get-cruise-ready/))

**Honest reading for the guide:** the shuttle-vs-walk position is the least crisply documented item in this section. The sources say both "free shuttle to the City Bus Exchange" and "within walking distance." Palmer Street is genuinely adjacent — Quayside is on Lennon Drive in South Townsville, and Palmer Street is the South Townsville dining strip a few hundred metres away. The CBD proper requires crossing Ross Creek. Whether passengers are permitted to walk out of the secure area on any given call is **operator- and call-dependent**, and the line "the cruise liner determines the bus exchange location and whether passengers are required to pay" means HAL's arrangements for the 2028 call cannot be stated in advance. **Write this as: expect a shuttle; do not promise a walk-out.** Marked UNVERIFIED for the specific 2028 call.

### Stage 2 — future, not current

A **$690 million Outer Harbour Development (Stage 2)** would add two further common-user berths and a 90 ha reclamation, with delivery indicated **between 2026 and 2030**. As of this research it is at planning stage, not operating. Do not describe it as existing. ([Port of Townsville — projects](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/projects-development/); [genfreight summary](https://genfreight.com.au/port-of-townsville-upgrade-strengthening-northern-australias-trade-gateway/))

## 2. Magnetic Island

### The island

- **8 km offshore** from Townsville ([Wikipedia — Magnetic Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_Island))
- **52 km²**; population **2,475** (2021 census)
- **~76% is Magnetic Island National Park**
- Highest point **Mount Cook, 497 m**
- Five settlements: **Picnic Bay, Nelly Bay, Arcadia, Horseshoe Bay, West Point**
- Traditional name **Yunbenun**; Wulgurukaba people. In July 2012 land parcels were granted to the **Wulgurukaba Yunbenun Aboriginal Corporation** by freehold title and deed.
- Named "Magnetical Island" by **Captain James Cook in 1770**, who attributed compass interference to it. (No magnetic anomaly has been substantiated since — see CORRECTIONS.)
- **Over 800 koalas** estimated as of 2013 — **the northern limit of the species' geographic range**

### The two operators — and they are not interchangeable

This is a point most guides blur. There are **two separate operators leaving from two separate Townsville terminals**.

**1. SeaLink Magnetic Island — fast passenger ferry**
- Townsville terminal: **Breakwater Terminal, Sir Leslie Thiess Drive** (north side of Ross Creek, by the marina and The Ville)
- Island terminal: **Nelly Bay Harbour**
- Crossing: **20–25 minutes** ([What's On Magnetic Island](https://www.whatsonmagneticisland.com.au/ferry-and-barge))
- Foot passengers only
- Up to **17 return services daily**

**2. Magnetic Island Ferries — vehicle and passenger barge**
- Townsville terminal: **Ross Street, South Townsville** (same side of the creek as the cruise terminal)
- Island terminal: **Nelly Bay Harbour**
- Crossing: **40–45 minutes** ([What's On Magnetic Island](https://www.whatsonmagneticisland.com.au/ferry-and-barge); [Magnetic Island Guide](https://www.magneticislandguide.com.au/ferry-bus-timetables))
- Adult fare: **$18.50 one way / $37 return** ([Magnetic Island Ferries — Rates](https://magneticislandferries.com.au/prices-fares/))

**Does the vehicle ferry matter to a foot passenger?** Essentially **no** — and the guide should say so. It is slower (40–45 min vs 20–25 min), and a cruise passenger has no car to bring. Its only relevance is that its Ross Street terminal happens to be much closer to the cruise berth than SeaLink's Breakwater terminal. That is a real but minor consideration, and it is offset by roughly 20 minutes extra each way and a thinner timetable. **Recommend SeaLink for foot passengers.** Note also that Magnetic Island Ferries flagged that "reduced rates for vehicle travel will not continue beyond 30 June 2026," with a new "Magnetic Saver" programme replacing them — vehicle pricing is in flux and should not be quoted for 2028.

### Can a cruise passenger realistically do it on an 08:00–17:00 call?

**Yes — clearly and comfortably. This is the standout excursion of the port.** The arithmetic:

- Ashore and at Breakwater terminal by ~08:45 (allowing for staged disembarkation and transfer)
- **08:45 ferry** → Nelly Bay ~09:10
- Full day on the island
- **15:00 ferry** ex Nelly Bay → Townsville ~15:25, or **16:30** → ~16:55 for the bolder
- All-aboard typically 16:30 for a 17:00 sail

The **15:00 return is the sensible one**; the 16:30 is cutting it fine against a 17:00 sailing and should be flagged as risky. That still gives roughly **six hours ashore on the island** — genuinely enough for the Forts Walk plus a bay.

Two operational cautions to print:
- The ferry timetable has a **notable mid-afternoon gap** (13:35 then 15:00 ex Nelly Bay). A passenger who misses the 15:00 has 16:30 as the next option.
- **Independent travel to Magnetic Island on a ferry not chartered by the cruise line carries the usual risk: the ship will not wait.** This should be stated plainly.

### What is on the island

**Forts Walk** — the signature walk, and one of the better short walks in tropical Queensland.

- **4 km return**, from **Forts Junction** ([Queensland Parks — Forts Walk](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/magnetic-island/journeys/forts-walk); [Trail Hiking Australia](https://www.trailhiking.com.au/hikes/forts-walk-magnetic-island-national-park/))
- **Grade 3 (moderate)**
- **Total ascent 183 m**, ranging 67 m to 169 m elevation
- **Official time 2 hours; typical time ~1.5 hours** — sources differ, and the difference is real: the official figure builds in the detours to the individual gun sites and the command post
- Trailhead is on the **main road between Arcadia and Horseshoe Bay**, reachable by island bus or car; **6.4 km from Nelly Bay** if walking
- **WWII fortifications** — command post, observation towers and gun emplacements, described as "among the best preserved in Queensland," with interpretive signage
- Summit views to **Bowling Green Bay, the Palm Islands and Hinchinbrook Island**
- **Koalas are regularly seen** in the eucalypt and acacia woodland along the trail, particularly in the stunted trees near the top. This is the single most reliable place to see wild koalas in the Townsville region.

Realistic guidance: allow **2–2.5 hours** including bus connections at each end and time to actually look for koalas. This fits inside a six-hour island window with room for a swim afterwards.

**The bays:**

- **Nelly Bay** — the ferry terminal and the island's tourism and residential hub. Where you arrive. Not the best beach.
- **Horseshoe Bay** — the largest bay and the main beach destination; northern end of the island; bus from Nelly Bay.
- **Picnic Bay** — the original ferry terminal and township at the southern end; quieter, has a jetty.
- **Arcadia / Geoffrey Bay** — the point of interest here is the **allied rock-wallaby colony**, habituated and reliably seen around the rocks at the Geoffrey Bay end near the Arcadia jetty, generally in the late afternoon. **UNVERIFIED**: exact viewing location, best time, and whether feeding is permitted or discouraged were not confirmed from a primary source (Queensland Parks pages returned HTTP 403 to automated retrieval). Treat wallaby-feeding advice with care — feeding native wildlife is discouraged or prohibited in many Queensland contexts, and the guide should not encourage it without confirming current park advice.
- **Radical Bay, Florence Bay, Balding Bay** — smaller, reached on foot from the Forts area.

**Getting around the island:** **Kinetic** operates the island bus, covering **all bays from Picnic Bay to Horseshoe Bay**, with **services scheduled to meet the ferry**, and it is **cashless on board**. ([Magnetic Island Guide](https://www.magneticislandguide.com.au/ferry-bus-timetables)) **Magnetic Maxi** offers door-to-door transport for 1–10 passengers and is **wheelchair accessible**. Hire options (mokes, scooters) exist but are UNVERIFIED as to current operators and price.

**Island bus fares:** covered by the Queensland 50 cent flat fare — see §9. UNVERIFIED whether the Magnetic Island service is explicitly within the Townsville regional urban network for that purpose; Translink's page names Townsville among the 14 regional areas but does not name Magnetic Island.

## 3. Reef HQ Great Barrier Reef Aquarium — CLOSED. Do not send anyone.

**Your prior indication is correct, and the scale of the error in circulation is worse than you suspected.**

### The facts

**The Great Barrier Reef Aquarium (formerly Reef HQ) is CLOSED to the public and has been since February 2021.** It is under redevelopment. **It is not reopening in 2026.**

From the aquarium's own official website: **"We are working towards reopening in 2029."** The Australian Government has allocated **$180.1 million** to redevelop the facility, described as **"Australia's National Education Centre for the Great Barrier Reef."** ([Great Barrier Reef Aquarium — official site](https://www.greatbarrierreefaquarium.au/))

From the aquarium's own myth-busting page — which exists precisely because so much wrong information circulates:
- **Closure date: February 2021**
- **Reopening year: 2029**
- Funding is secured through the **Townsville City Deal**
- **The original 38-year-old building remains intact.** Adjacent structures were demolished for improved access during early works.
- Design development and government approvals are underway
([Great Barrier Reef Aquarium — MythBusters](https://www.greatbarrierreefaquarium.au/latest-news/mythbusters-debunking-the-most-common-myths-swirling-about-the-great-barrier-reef-aquariumnbsp))

The aquarium is operated by the **Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority (the Reef Authority)**, an Australian Government agency. ([Reef Authority — Great Barrier Reef Aquarium](https://www2.gbrmpa.gov.au/great-barrier-reef-aquarium))

### Where the animals went

Over roughly three months, **more than 5.5 tonnes of reef rock, sand, corals and fish** were relocated to **AIMS' National Sea Simulator at Cape Cleveland, 50 km south of Townsville**, where they remain under the daily care of aquarium staff until reopening. Larger animals were rehomed to other Queensland facilities. ([Reef Authority — "Noah's Ark on the Reef"](https://www2.gbrmpa.gov.au/news/noahs-ark-reef-worlds-largest-living-coral-reef-aquarium-relocation-revealed))

**The National Sea Simulator is a research facility, not a visitor attraction.** Do not imply passengers can go and see the relocated corals.

### What it was

Opened **14 June 1987** as the Great Barrier Reef Aquarium, renamed ReefHQ in 1999. The main exhibit held approximately **2.5 million litres** (up to 3 million litres including intake) and was **the world's largest living coral reef exhibit**. ([Wikipedia — Reef HQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reef_HQ))

### The specific bad information in circulation

**Wikipedia is wrong on two counts as at this research date.** Its Reef HQ article states the new aquarium "is expected to open **in 2026**" and states that "following detailed inspection, authorities determined **complete demolition was necessary** rather than renovation." ([Wikipedia — Reef HQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reef_HQ))

Both are contradicted by the operating authority:
- Reopening is **2029**, not 2026 — stated on the aquarium's own site.
- The building was **not demolished** — "the aquarium was demolished and the site abandoned" is listed as **Myth #1** on the aquarium's own myth-busting page, which states the original building remains intact.

**GBRMPA / the Great Barrier Reef Aquarium is authoritative here** — it is the Australian Government agency that owns and operates the facility and is delivering the redevelopment. Wikipedia and the tourism aggregators derived from it are not.

Lonely Planet still carries a "Top Choice" listing for Reef HQ Aquarium. ([Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/australia/townsville/attractions/reef-hq-aquarium/a/poi-sig/1229153/362394)) Treat any aggregator listing of this attraction as evidence the page has not been reviewed since 2021.

### What this means for the 2028 guide

The call is in **2028**. The target reopening is **2029**. **The aquarium will almost certainly still be closed when *Volendam* calls.** Large public infrastructure projects do not typically open early. The guide should state clearly: *the Great Barrier Reef Aquarium is closed for redevelopment and is not expected to reopen until 2029 at the earliest; do not plan a visit.*

The land-based MOUA sculpture **Ocean Siren** on The Strand is a partial consolation and *is* accessible — see §5.

## 4. The Great Barrier Reef from Townsville — the honest answer is "not on a port day"

### Short answer

**No. Townsville is a poor reef-access port for a cruise passenger on a single-day call.** It is a good reef port for someone with two or three days. The guide should say this plainly rather than gesture at "the Great Barrier Reef" because the city markets itself that way.

### Why — the distances

Adrenalin Snorkel & Dive, **Townsville's longest-established day-trip operator**, lists its reef destinations by distance from Townsville ([Adrenalin Snorkel & Dive](https://www.adrenalindive.com.au/)):

| Reef | Distance offshore |
|---|---|
| Lodestone Reef | 40 km |
| Little Broadhurst Reef | 60 km |
| **John Brewer Reef** (MOUA site) | **70 km** |
| Keeper Reef | 74 km |
| Davies Reef | 80 km |
| Wheeler Reef | 90 km |
| **SS Yongala wreck** | **90 km** |

Compare Cairns, where the outer reef is typically 90 minutes' steaming. From Townsville, outer reef sites are **around 2 hours each way** by boat. ([MOUA / operator sources](https://www.moua.com.au/visit/coral-greenhouse))

The Central Section of the Great Barrier Reef, which Townsville accesses, sits **further off the Queensland coast** than the northern sections — which is precisely why the reefs are good and why they are hard to reach in a day.

### The trip itself

Adrenalin's Great Barrier Reef day trip:
- **Duration: approximately 9 hours** ([Adrenalin booking page](https://adrenalinsnorkeldive.rezdy.com/319472/great-barrier-reef-day-trip-snorkeling))
- **Price from AUD $394 per adult** (snorkeller; Discover Scuba option shown at the same starting price)
- Departs Townsville **or** Magnetic Island
- Preferred reefs: **John Brewer, Keeper and Lodestone**

**A 9-hour trip does not fit inside an 08:00–17:00 port call.** Even setting aside price, the trip departs before a cruise ship has finished clearing and returns after all-aboard. Yongala dive trips depart **07:00 from Breakwater Marina** — before a typical cruise passenger is ashore.

There is a further constraint: reef and MOUA trips from Townsville reportedly **run only on Fridays and Saturdays**. ([MOUA / operator information surfaced in research](https://www.moua.com.au/visit/coral-greenhouse)) **UNVERIFIED** — I was unable to confirm the current operating days directly from Adrenalin's schedule (the operator's day-trip page returned 404 and the booking widget requires date selection). If the guide mentions reef trips at all, this needs confirming. But even on a Friday or Saturday call, the 9-hour duration is disqualifying.

### The SS Yongala — and whether it matters to a non-diver

The **SS Yongala** is a shipwreck lying **90 km offshore**, with the wreck beginning **15 m below the surface and extending to 29 m**. Adrenalin's Yongala trips involve **2 certified dives**, depart **07:00 Saturdays from Breakwater Marina**, and are for **certified divers**. ([Adrenalin — SS Yongala](https://www.adrenalindive.com.au/s-s-yongala-wreck/))

**For a non-diver the Yongala is irrelevant.** It is not visible from the surface — the top of the wreck is 15 m down — it requires certification, and the trip does not fit a port day. It is widely and correctly described as one of the world's great wreck dives, and it is entirely inaccessible to a cruise passenger on a single call. **Say so; do not tease it.**

*Historical note worth a sentence in the guide:* the Yongala sank in a cyclone in 1911 with the loss of all aboard — a genuine local story, and a reason the wreck is a protected historic site. **UNVERIFIED**: exact date, death toll and protection status were not confirmed from a primary source in this research.

### Museum of Underwater Art (MOUA)

MOUA has **three sites** ([MOUA](https://www.moua.com.au/)):

1. **Coral Greenhouse** — John Brewer Reef, 70 km offshore. By **Jason deCaires Taylor**. **12 m tall, 58 tonnes**, sitting at **16 m depth**. Holds the **Guinness World Record for largest underwater art structure**. ([MOUA — Coral Greenhouse](https://www.moua.com.au/visit/coral-greenhouse))
2. **Ocean Sentinels** — also John Brewer Reef, shallower, a short swim from the Coral Greenhouse.
3. **Ocean Siren** — **on The Strand in Townsville. Land-based. No boat required.** See §5.

The site has **shallow areas (4–6 m) intended for snorkelling and deeper sections (12–18 m) for scuba**. Since the Coral Greenhouse itself sits at **16 m**, a snorkeller sees it only from the surface, in good visibility. Snorkellers are better served by the shallower **Ocean Sentinels**. ([MOUA — Coral Greenhouse](https://www.moua.com.au/visit/coral-greenhouse))

**For a cruise passenger: the only accessible MOUA site is Ocean Siren, on The Strand.** The offshore sculptures are a 9-hour, $394 commitment.

### What a reef-hungry passenger should actually do

The honest substitute is **fringing reef snorkelling off Magnetic Island** — Geoffrey Bay and Nelly Bay have fringing coral and marked snorkel trails, reachable on a $40 ferry ticket. It is **not** the outer Great Barrier Reef, and the guide must not pretend otherwise, but it is real coral, it is inside the Marine Park, and it fits the day. A half-day snorkelling tour operating from Townsville and Magnetic Island exists at far lower cost than the outer-reef trip. ([Barrier Reef Australia — Townsville & Magnetic Island Half Day Snorkelling Tour](https://www.barrierreefaustralia.com/things-to-do/tours/townsville-magnetic-island-half-day-snorkelling-tour-rm.806/)) **UNVERIFIED**: current operator, price and schedule for this half-day product.

**Bottom line for the guide: if seeing the outer Great Barrier Reef is the passenger's priority, that is what Cairns and Airlie Beach are for on this itinerary. In Townsville, go to Magnetic Island.**

## 6. Marine stingers and crocodiles

This section must be precise. Both hazards are real, both are seasonal or conditional, and both are routinely either over-dramatised or waved away in cruise guides.

### Marine stingers

**Season.** Townsville City Council: **"Townsville's stinger season typically runs from November through to May, when warmer water temperatures increase the presence of marine stingers such as box jellyfish and Irukandji in coastal waters."** ([Townsville City Council media release, via Magnetic Community News](https://www.magneticcommunitynews.com/post/nets-removed-as-townsville-farewells-stinger-season))

Note the word **"typically."** Council operates to observed conditions, not a fixed calendar. Nets went in during **November 2025** and came out in the **week ending ~27 May 2026**. ([Council media releases, November 2025](https://www.townsville.qld.gov.au/about-council/news-and-publications/media-releases/2025/november/stinger-season-kicks-off-with-net-installation) and [May 2026](https://www.townsville.qld.gov.au/about-council/news-and-publications/media-releases/2026/may/nets-removed-as-townsville-farewells-stinger-season))

**Species.** Two distinct hazards, named by Council:
- **Box jellyfish** (*Chironex fleckeri*) — large, potentially fatal, tentacles metres long
- **Irukandji** — small (roughly thumbnail-sized), causing **Irukandji syndrome**: delayed-onset severe systemic symptoms

**Where the enclosures are.** Stinger enclosures are installed at **The Strand, Pallarenda, Magnetic Island and Balgal Beach**. ([Council, via Magnetic Community News](https://www.magneticcommunitynews.com/post/nets-removed-as-townsville-farewells-stinger-season))

**What the nets do and do not do — the critical safety point.**

Stinger enclosures are designed to exclude **box jellyfish**, which are large. **They are not a reliable barrier against Irukandji**, which are small enough to pass through the mesh. This is the single most important safety fact in this section and it is the one most often omitted.

**Sourcing caveat, stated honestly:** the Council media release I could retrieve **does not explicitly state** that the nets fail to stop Irukandji — it says only that the nets "play a crucial role over the summer months." The Irukandji-passes-through-the-mesh point is standard Queensland public health and surf lifesaving advice and is stated on tropical North Queensland tourism safety pages ([Tropical North Queensland — Jellyfish Safety](https://tropicalnorthqueensland.org.au/plan-your-trip/wildlife-safety/jellyfish-safe/); [Great Barrier Reef Tours — Box Jellyfish & Irukandji](https://greatbarrierreeftours.com/reef-safety-hub/box-jellyfish-and-irukandji-jellyfish/)), **but I could not confirm it from Surf Life Saving Queensland or Queensland Health directly in this session.** It should be verified against SLSQ or Queensland Health before publication. **Marked as requiring verification, not as unsourced speculation — the claim is well-established, the primary citation is missing.**

**Patrols.** Council states that **on patrolled beaches, lifeguards conduct daily checks for jellyfish before putting out the flags**, and swimmers are directed to **stay between the red and yellow flags**. Lifeguards are on duty **until 5pm** at the Strand Rockpool. ([Council, via search summary of the lifeguard hours page — the page itself returned HTTP 403](https://www.townsville.qld.gov.au/facilities-and-recreation/parks-beaches-and-community-venues/lifeguard-hours))

**INCOMPLETE**: exact daily patrol start and finish times and which months patrols operate. Townsville City Council's site blocked automated retrieval throughout this session. **This must be checked before publication** — telling a cruise passenger a beach is patrolled when it is not is a safety failure.

**Practical guidance for the guide:**
- If the call falls **November–May**: swim only inside a stinger enclosure, between the flags. Consider a full-body lycra stinger suit, widely hired in the region.
- If the call falls **June–October**: nets are out of the water and the risk is much lower, but not zero.
- **The Strand Rockpool** is a seawater pool and is the safest swim in Townsville year-round.
- **Vinegar** is the standard first aid for box jellyfish stings and is stationed at patrolled beaches. **UNVERIFIED** for Townsville specifically.

**A 2028 timing note:** if the Townsville call falls in the Australian summer/autumn (which the Grand World Voyage routing makes likely — see §9), **it will be inside stinger season**. Write the guide on that assumption and note the June–October alternative.

### Saltwater crocodiles

**The Townsville region is Croc Country. This is not hypothetical.**

**Official zoning.** Under the current **Queensland Crocodile Management Plan**, **Townsville City Council falls within a Targeted Crocodile Removal Zone (TCR)**. In a TCR zone, **all estuarine crocodiles two metres or greater in length, and any crocodile displaying dangerous behaviour (following a problem crocodile determination), are targeted for removal**. ([Queensland Government — Crocodile management plan](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/animals/living-with/crocodiles/crocodile-management/crocodile-management-plan))

The four zones under the plan are:
1. **Active Removal Zone (AR)** — all estuarine crocodiles removed regardless of size or behaviour
2. **Targeted Crocodile Removal Zone (TCR)** — **Townsville** — ≥2 m, plus dangerous behaviour
3. **General Management Zone (GM)** — only crocodiles displaying dangerous behaviour
4. **Reportable Removal Zone (RR)** — all removed once presence confirmed

A **new Queensland Crocodile Management Plan was announced in December 2025**. Guides citing the 2017 plan are out of date. ([Queensland Government Ministerial Statement](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/104143); [Queensland Crocodile Management Plan 2025, tabled in Parliament](https://www.parliament.qld.gov.au/Work-of-the-Assembly/Tabled-Papers/docs/5825T1967/5825t1967.pdf))

**Ross River specifically.** The Department of the Environment, Tourism, Science and Innovation (DETSI) **targeted a crocodile for removal from the Ross River** after multiple public sighting reports, "due to its size and location in a **high recreational use area**." A **second crocodile about two metres in length** was observed nearby and a trap was re-installed to target it. ([DETSI — Crocodile captured in Ross River in Townsville, 2025](https://www.detsi.qld.gov.au/our-department/news-media/mediareleases/2025/crocodile-captured-in-ross-river-in-townsville)) DETSI has issued multiple Townsville-region crocodile media releases including [2024 captures](https://www.detsi.qld.gov.au/our-department/news-media/mediareleases/2024/crocodiles-captured-in-the-townsville-region).

**The official CrocWise advice**, verbatim in substance ([Queensland Government — Be CrocWise](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/animals/living-with/crocodiles/becrocwise)):

- **Expect crocodiles in all waterways even if there is no warning sign**
- Obey all warning signs
- **Be aware crocodiles also swim in the ocean**
- Be extra cautious around water at night
- Stay well away from crocodile traps
- Avoid using canoes and kayaks
- Stand back from the water's edge when fishing
- Camp at least 50 metres from the water's edge
- Never leave food, fish scraps or bait near the water

Sightings are reported via the **QWildlife app**, an online form, or **1300 130 372**. There is a public [crocodile sightings dashboard](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/animals/living-with/crocodiles/crocodile-sightings/crocodile-sightings-dashboard).

**What this means for a cruise passenger — proportionate, not alarmist:**

- **Do not swim in the Ross River, Ross Creek, or any Townsville waterway, mangrove or estuary.** Ever. Regardless of signage.
- **The Strand and the Rockpool are a different matter** — patrolled, netted in season, and the normal swimming venues for a city of 200,000 people. Crocodile risk there is low but the official advice that crocodiles also swim in the ocean still applies, which is why you swim at patrolled beaches.
- **Riverway is on the Ross River.** If the guide mentions Riverway, it must mention that the Ross River is croc habitat and that swimming is in the constructed lagoons, not the river.
- The presence of crocodiles is **normal and managed**, not an emergency. North Queenslanders live with it. The advice is behavioural, not avoidant.

### Wet season

The wet season in the Australian tropical north conventionally runs **roughly November/December to April**, driven by the monsoon. Townsville's data bears this out: **December–March delivers 915.6 mm of the annual 1,162.3 mm — 79% of the year's rain in four months.** ([BoM Townsville Aero](http://www.bom.gov.au/climate/averages/tables/cw_032040.shtml), calculated)

The dry season, **May–October**, delivers just **119.0 mm across 23 rain days** — about 10% of annual rainfall in half the year. **September is the driest month at 9.9 mm and 2.4 rain days.**

**UNVERIFIED**: BoM's formal definition of the northern Australian wet season (it is usually given as October–April for the monsoon onset/retreat, but I could not retrieve BoM's own definitional page in this session).

### Cyclone season

The Australian tropical cyclone season conventionally runs **1 November to 30 April**, with the peak generally in **January–March**. **UNVERIFIED from BoM directly** — BoM's cyclone climatology pages redirected during this session and I could not confirm the official dates or average counts from the Bureau itself. The 1 November – 30 April dates are standard and uncontroversial, but should be cited to BoM before publication.

### Cyclone history — the significant events

**Severe Tropical Cyclone Althea — 24 December 1971**
- Crossed near Townsville on Christmas Eve 1971
- **Three deaths**
- Damage **~$50 million (1971 dollars)** in the Townsville region
- **Storm surge 2.9 m in Townsville Harbour**; **3.66 m at Toolakea** to the north
- **90% of Magnetic Island houses damaged or destroyed**
- Major flooding in the Burdekin
([BoM — Severe Tropical Cyclone Althea](https://www.bom.gov.au/cyclone/history/althea.shtml); [Australian Disaster Resilience Knowledge Hub](https://knowledge.aidr.org.au/resources/cyclone-cyclone-althea-queensland/); [Townsville City Council — Cyclone Althea](https://stories.townsville.qld.gov.au/nodes/view/19992))

Althea remains the benchmark event in Townsville's civic memory and is the reason the city's building codes and foreshore design are what they are.

**Severe Tropical Cyclone Yasi — 3 February 2011**
- Crossed the coast between **midnight and 1am on Thursday 3 February 2011**
- Affected a **~350 km stretch of coast between Townsville and Cairns**
- Landfall was at **Mission Beach**, north of Townsville — Townsville was on the southern flank, not at the centre
- **No direct loss of life**; one death indirectly attributed (carbon monoxide poisoning from a generator operated indoors)
- Damage **~A$3.5 billion**
([Wikipedia — Cyclone Yasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Yasi); [Australian Disaster Resilience Knowledge Hub](https://knowledge.aidr.org.au/resources/cyclone-cyclone-yasi-queensland-2011/))

**Tropical Cyclone Kirrily — 25 January 2024** — and a factual dispute worth flagging
- **BoM:** Kirrily reached **Category 3 (65 knots, 120 km/h mean winds) at 1pm AEST on 25 January**, offshore in the Coral Sea, then **weakened before landfall**. It **crossed the coast as a Category 1** near **Balgal Beach / Rollingstone, about 50 km northwest of Townsville, at 10pm AEST on 25 January 2024**. ([BoM — Severe Tropical Cyclone Kirrily](https://www.bom.gov.au/cyclone/history/kirrily24.shtml))
- **Several secondary sources state Kirrily made landfall "as a Category 3 storm."** ([CIRA Satellite Library](https://satlib.cira.colostate.edu/event/tropical-cyclone-kirrily/)) **This is wrong.**
- **BoM is authoritative** — it is the agency that assigns Australian-scale intensities and issues the official post-analysis. Use Category 1 at landfall, Category 3 peak intensity offshore.
- The system went on to **dump more than a metre of rain** and caused widespread flooding in western Queensland as it moved inland; the Queensland Reconstruction Authority describes it as "the northern system that became a statewide disaster event." Damage ~$120 million. ([QRA case study](https://www.qra.qld.gov.au/news-case-studies/case-studies/tropical-cyclone-kirrily-northern-system-became-statewide-disaster-event); [Townsville City Council](https://www.townsville.qld.gov.au/about-council/news-and-publications/media-releases/2024/january/severe-tropical-cyclone-kirrily-has-made-landfall))

**Tropical Cyclone Sid — January 1998** — caused severe damage to the old Strand foreshore, prompting the 1999 rebuild. ([Wikipedia — The Strand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Strand,_Townsville))

### The 2019 monsoon flood — the defining modern disaster

This was **not a cyclone**. It was a stalled monsoon trough, and it was worse for Townsville than any cyclone since Althea.

- **Townsville Aero recorded 1,052.8 mm in the seven days to 4 February 2019**, exceeding the previous record of 886.2 mm (January 1998)
- **Ten-day total to 8 February: 1,259.8 mm**, surpassing 925.5 mm (January 1953)
- **That ten-day total exceeded Townsville's entire annual average rainfall of 1,162.3 mm**
- **370 mm fell in 24 hours at Paluma** near Townsville
- **Ross River Dam** received **850,000 megalitres — 3.8 times its capacity** — over 29 January to 5 February; inflows exceeded a **1-in-1,000-year event**
- The dam peaked at **248% of capacity**; authorities released water at **1,900 cumecs (m³/s)**
- **Almost 3,300 properties damaged**; thousands evacuated; widespread blackouts
([Townsville City Council — 2019 Weather Event](https://www.townsville.qld.gov.au/community-support/community-safety/disaster-management/2019-weather-event); [Australian Disaster Resilience Knowledge Hub](https://knowledge.aidr.org.au/resources/2019-storms-and-floods-qld-townsville/); [JCU Centre for Disaster Studies post-disaster survey](https://www.jcu.edu.au/centre-for-disaster-studies/publications/reports/post-disaster-reports/Townsville-2019-flood.pdf); [Risk Frontiers](https://riskfrontiers.com/insights/townsville-2019-flood-insights-from-the-field/))

The flood also had a public-health tail: it was followed by a documented outbreak of **melioidosis** and effects on mosquito-borne disease. ([PMC — Flooding in Townsville February 2019 and its effects on mosquito-borne diseases](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6517894/))

**This is the event locals mean when they say "the flood."** It is essential context for understanding the city and belongs in the history section of the guide.

## 8. History and Country

### Whose Country

**Townsville City Council's Acknowledgement of Country** names:

> **"the Wulgurukaba of Gurambilbarra and Yunbenun, Bindal, Gugu Badhun and Nywaigi as the Traditional Owners of this land."**

([Townsville City Council — Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islanders](https://www.townsville.qld.gov.au/community-support/services-and-community-development/aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islanders))

**This is the currently accepted usage and the guide should reproduce it.** Note the structure carefully:

- **Wulgurukaba** — of **Gurambilbarra** (the Townsville mainland) and **Yunbenun** (Magnetic Island). **Wulgurukaba means "canoe people."**
- **Bindal** — who call their Country **Thul Garrie Waja**
- **Gugu Badhun** and **Nywaigi** — also named by Council

The **Bindal and Wulgurukaba peoples are the first people to have lived in the Townsville region.** ([Townsville City Council — Traditional Landowners](https://www.townsville.qld.gov.au/about-townsville/history-and-heritage/townsville-history/traditional-landowners))

**Spelling matters.** It is **Wulgurukaba** (not Wulgurakaba, not Wulguru-kaba). **Bindal** (not Bindil). **Gurambilbarra**. **Yunbenun**. The variant **"Wulguru"** appears in some sources for the island's traditional owners; Council's formal wording is Wulgurukaba.

In **July 2012**, land parcels on Magnetic Island were granted to the **Wulgurukaba Yunbenun Aboriginal Corporation** through freehold title and deed. ([Wikipedia — Magnetic Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_Island))

**Ocean Siren**, the sculpture on The Strand, is modelled on **Takoda Johnson, a young Indigenous leader from the Wulgurukaba community** — a contemporary, visible connection between Country and the city's most-photographed public artwork. ([MOUA](https://www.moua.com.au/visit/ocean-siren))

### Founding, and who Townsville is named after

- **1770** — Captain James Cook names **"Magnetical Island"**, attributing compass interference to it
- **April 1864** — **John Melton Black**, employed by **Robert Towns** of Woodstock Station, sends **Andrew Ball**, **Mark Watt Reid** and Aboriginal employees to find a better port site than Bowen. They camp near where **Customs House** now stands on The Strand.
- **1865** — Black builds the first house. **The port is gazetted as a Port of Entry on 10 October 1865.**
- **1866** — The **Municipality of Townsville** is declared, with **John Melton Black its first Mayor**. The first steamship arrives.
- **1866** — **Robert Towns** agrees to fund the settlement and makes his **only visit, at the age of 75**. The settlement is **named in his honour**.
([Townsville City Council — Townsville 1770 to 1900](https://www.townsville.qld.gov.au/about-townsville/history-and-heritage/townsville-history/townsville-1770-to-1900); [Wikipedia — Port of Townsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Townsville); [Wikipedia — Robert Towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Towns); [Wikipedia — John Melton Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Melton_Black))

**A point of honesty the guide should not dodge.** On that same 1866 visit, Robert Towns **"organised the first South Sea Islander labour to be transported into the port, on the *Blue Bell* under Captain Edwards."** ([Townsville City Council — Townsville 1770 to 1900](https://www.townsville.qld.gov.au/about-townsville/history-and-heritage/townsville-history/townsville-1770-to-1900))

Towns was a central figure in the **"blackbirding"** trade — the coerced and deceptive recruitment of South Sea Islanders for Queensland plantation labour. The city is named after him. A guide that says "named after Robert Towns, a Sydney businessman" and stops there is telling a comfortable half-truth. **One honest sentence is enough**; this is a live matter of public discussion in Queensland and passengers who look it up will find it.

### The port's history — worth ~200 words

The Port of Townsville exists because Bowen's harbour was inadequate for the pastoral runs of the hinterland. Founded **1864** by John Melton Black with Robert Towns' backing and **gazetted a Port of Entry on 10 October 1865**, it grew on the back of what came down from the ranges. **Sugar exports began in 1872.** The **railway to Charters Towers was completed in 1882**, tying the port to one of the richest goldfields in Australian history and making Townsville the outlet for North Queensland's mineral wealth — a role it has never given up. The **Townsville Harbour Board** was established in **1896**; the **first bulk sugar terminal** opened in **1959**; and **major reclamation between 1967 and 1980** built the modern port out into Cleveland Bay.

Today it is **the largest general freight and container port in northern Australia** and **Queensland's third-largest seaport**, handling **6.8 million tonnes and 59,000 TEU** with a **trade value of $10 billion in 2023–24**, across **more than 30 commodity types**. It is **Australia's number one port for copper, zinc, lead and sugar**, and also moves fertiliser, mineral concentrates, motor vehicles, wool, frozen meat and live cattle.

**Berth 10 was upgraded for $85 million in 2013**, the same year Quayside Terminal opened. The **$251 million Channel Upgrade completed in 2024–25** widened the channel from 92 m to 180 m and lifted the vessel limit from 238 m to 300 m — which is why *Volendam* can come alongside at all.
([Wikipedia — Port of Townsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Townsville); [Port of Townsville — Channel Upgrade](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/projects-development/channel-upgrade/))

### The Second World War — Townsville as a front-line garrison

**This is the most under-told story in Townsville and deserves real space in the guide.** Townsville was not a backwater in the Pacific War; it was one of the most important Allied bases in Australia.

**The scale, quantified** ([ANZAC Day Commemoration Committee — Front-line Townsville 1942](https://anzacday.org.au/townsville)):

- Population **~35,000 in early 1942**
- Population **~120,000 by mid-1943**, of which **75% were service personnel** — roughly **90,000 military personnel**
- **5,000 civilians voluntarily evacuated**; the military sought to move a further **10,000** out to free housing for troops

**Command and infrastructure:**
- **Area Combined Headquarters for the North East Area** (Australian forces)
- Headquarters of **United States Army Base Section Two** and the **Fourth Air Depot, USAAF**
- **RAAF No. 3 Fighter Sector** headquarters
- **Garbutt** runways, designed for **B-17 bombers**, completed **February 1942**, just before units of the **US Fifth Air Force** arrived ([OzAtWar — Garbutt](https://www.ozatwar.com/garbutt.htm))
- **Charters Towers airfield** built early 1942 as dispersal for Garbutt ([Anzac Square Memorial Galleries](https://www.anzacsquare.qld.gov.au/historic-places/queensland-wwii-history-map/charters-towers-airfield))
- Between 1942 and 1945 the corridor between the road and railway from Townsville to Charters Towers became **"one of the largest concentrations of airfields, repair facilities, stores and ammunition depots"**
- **Cleveland Bay**, between Townsville and Magnetic Island, was **an important assembly point for shipping**
- In late 1942 Townsville was **the principal port for Allied troops in the New Guinea campaign**
- The **Magnetic Battery** on Magnetic Island — the WWII fortifications of the Forts Walk — defended this harbour
- The **Operations and Signals Bunker at Wulguru** survives ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_and_Signals_Bunker,_Wulguru))

**The Japanese air raids on Townsville — July 1942**

Three raids by Japanese flying boats ([ANZAC Day Commemoration Committee](https://anzacday.org.au/townsville)):
- **25–26 July 1942**
- **27–28 July 1942**
- **28–29 July 1942** — the most significant, with **eight bombs dropped**, one leaving **a large crater near the racecourse**

**Damage was minimal and casualties few.** Townsville is one of the small number of Australian cities to have been bombed in the war, and the raids are historically significant out of all proportion to the damage they did.

### The 1942 "Battle of Townsville" — the Townsville mutiny

**Naming caution for the guide.** "The Battle of Townsville" is used loosely in popular sources for **two different events**: the July 1942 Japanese air raids, and the **Townsville mutiny of May 1942**. The mutiny is what most modern references mean. Wikipedia's article is titled **"Townsville mutiny"** and does not use "Battle of Townsville." **Use "Townsville mutiny" and note the alternative name rather than the reverse.**

**What happened** ([Wikipedia — Townsville mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsville_mutiny)):

- **22–23 May 1942**
- **The 96th Battalion, US Army Corps of Engineers** — approximately **600 African American troops** — stationed at **Kelso Field** near Townsville. Specifically **"A" and "C" Companies**.
- African American soldiers, **provoked by racial abuse from white commanding officers**, **fired machine guns and anti-aircraft weapons at tents housing white officers**
- **More than 700 rounds** discharged during an **eight-hour siege**
- **At least one person killed**; dozens severely injured
- Journalist **Robert Sherrod** documented it; **his report was suppressed**
- **Lyndon B. Johnson — later President — was visiting Townsville at the time**, and his presence contributed to the cover-up. **Johnson did not conduct the investigation**; he was present. Sources that say he investigated it are wrong.
- **The event remained hidden until 2012**, when a Queensland historian uncovered it through declassified documents

For context, approximately **7,258 African American servicemen were stationed in Australia in August 1942** — a population subject to the US Army's segregation policies operating inside a country with its own White Australia Policy.

**This is a genuinely significant and under-known story** and it belongs in an authoritative guide. It is also 84 years old and should be told as history, without sensationalism.

### The city today

- Population **179,011 (2021 census)**; projected **202,367 as of 2025** ([Wikipedia — Townsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsville), citing ABS)
- Urban area **693.3 km²**

## 10. What is genuinely worth it, and what is overrated

My honest read, grounded in what the research actually established.

### The one-line answer

**Get on the ferry to Magnetic Island.** Everything else in Townsville is a fallback.

### Tier 1 — do this

**1. Magnetic Island: the Forts Walk plus a bay.**
This is the only thing in Townsville that a well-travelled cruise passenger will remember. It is a **20–25 minute crossing** for about **$40 return**, on a timetable dense enough to be forgiving. The **Forts Walk** delivers three things at once that rarely coincide: **wild koalas at the northern limit of their range**, **the best-preserved WWII coastal fortifications in Queensland**, and a **summit view over the Palm Islands and Hinchinbrook**. Add **Horseshoe Bay** afterwards and you have a complete, distinctive day. The arithmetic works comfortably on an 08:00–17:00 call with the 08:45 out and the 15:00 back.

**Caveats to publish honestly:** it is a **Grade 3 walk with 183 m of ascent in tropical heat**; it is not for everyone; and travelling independently means the ship will not wait.

**2. Queensland Museum Tropics — and it is free.**
Under-recommended because guides still list it under its old name at its old price. It is **free**, **open daily 9am–4pm**, **air-conditioned**, on **Flinders Street** in the middle of town, and the **Pandora** collection — material recovered from the ship sent to catch the *Bounty* mutineers, wrecked on the Great Barrier Reef — is a genuinely first-rate shipwreck gallery. On a 31°C afternoon in March this is the best hour in Townsville that costs nothing.

**3. The Strand, at the right end of the day.**
A well-made 2.2 km seafront with a **seawater rockpool** that is the safest swim in the city, the **Ocean Siren** sculpture, **Jezzine Barracks** and the Army Museum of North Queensland, and the **Tobruk Memorial Baths**. Combine with a swim inside the stinger enclosure if the season demands it.

### Tier 2 — worth it if it suits you

**Castle Hill by vehicle.** The **286 m** summit gives a genuine 360° panorama and it is **reachable by sealed road**, which makes it one of the few great views in tropical Queensland accessible to someone who cannot walk far. **Take a taxi up.**

**Billabong Sanctuary**, if the passenger's priority is a **guaranteed** koala rather than a possible one. Open daily, $54 adult, 17 km south. The honest trade-off: the Forts Walk might give you a wild koala for the price of a ferry ticket; Billabong will definitely give you one, in an enclosure, for $54 plus $25 for the photo plus taxi fares both ways.

**Flinders Street heritage walk** — free, 20 minutes, and it pairs with the museum. The **1886 Post Office**, the **1887–88 Australian Joint Stock Bank**, **Tattersalls Hotel (1864)**, the **Great Northern Hotel (1901)**.

**Palmer Street** for lunch — but **verify weekday lunch trading first**. It is the closest thing to the berth and would be the obvious recommendation if the venues are open at midday. That is not established.

### Tier 3 — overrated, or a trap

**The Great Barrier Reef day trip. Do not attempt it.** This is the clearest "don't" in the port. The reefs are **40–90 km offshore**, roughly **two hours each way**, the trip runs **~9 hours**, it costs **from $394**, and it may only operate Fridays and Saturdays. **It does not fit an 08:00–17:00 call.** A passenger who books one is booking a missed ship. Anyone whose priority is the outer reef should save it for **Cairns or Airlie Beach**, which is what those ports are for.

**The SS Yongala. Irrelevant to a non-diver.** 90 km offshore, wreck starting **15 m down**, certified divers only, **07:00 departures**. It is world-class and it is entirely out of reach on a port day. Guides that mention it as a Townsville "highlight" without saying this are misleading readers.

**Reef HQ Great Barrier Reef Aquarium. CLOSED.** It has been shut since **February 2021** and is not expected to reopen until **2029**. **A guide that sends passengers here is worse than useless** — it wastes the one attraction that would otherwise have been Townsville's best wet-weather, short-call answer, and it destroys the reader's trust in everything else the guide says. State the closure plainly and early.

**Riverway.** Several kilometres upriver, primarily a local amenity, on a river where crocodiles are trapped and removed. Not a port-day destination. *(Provisional — see the INCOMPLETE note in §5.)*

**Queens Gardens.** Pleasant, 4 hectares, 1870. Fifteen minutes if you are already at the foot of Castle Hill. Not a destination.

**Queensland Country Bank Stadium.** A fine 25,455-seat stadium and a real civic institution, but there is nothing to do there without a match on.

**The Ville casino.** If a passenger wants a casino they have one on the ship. Its only genuine use is that it sits **next to the Breakwater ferry terminal**, so it is a landmark on the way to Magnetic Island.

### The framing the guide should adopt

Townsville is **not a reef port and should not be sold as one**, despite the city's own marketing. It is a **garrison city, a working mineral port, and the gateway to an island** — and it is honest and more interesting to write it that way.

The three genuinely distinctive things about Townsville are:
1. **Magnetic Island** — koalas at the edge of their range, WWII forts, 20 minutes offshore
2. **The WWII story** — a city that tripled in size to 120,000 in eighteen months, was bombed three times in July 1942, and concealed a mutiny for seventy years
3. **The dry tropics** — quantifiably sunnier and drier than Cairns, 1,162 mm against 2,026 mm, and a better bet for a fine port day

A guide built on those three things will be more useful, and more accurate, than one that leads with a reef the passenger cannot reach and an aquarium that has been closed for five years.

## CORRECTIONS

Facts commonly stated online that this research found to be **wrong or stale**, with the correct version.

**1. "Reef HQ Great Barrier Reef Aquarium reopens in 2026."** — **WRONG.**
Correct: **closed since February 2021; the operator states "We are working towards reopening in 2029."** $180.1 million Australian Government redevelopment.
Stated incorrectly by [Wikipedia — Reef HQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reef_HQ) ("expected to open in 2026"). Corrected by the operator, [Great Barrier Reef Aquarium](https://www.greatbarrierreefaquarium.au/) and [Reef Authority (GBRMPA)](https://www2.gbrmpa.gov.au/great-barrier-reef-aquarium). **GBRMPA is authoritative — it is the Australian Government agency that owns, operates and is rebuilding the facility.**

**2. "Reef HQ was completely demolished."** — **WRONG.**
Correct: **the original 38-year-old building remains intact.** Only adjacent structures were demolished for access. "The aquarium was demolished and the site abandoned" is listed as **Myth #1** on the operator's own myth-busting page.
Stated incorrectly by [Wikipedia — Reef HQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reef_HQ). Corrected by [Great Barrier Reef Aquarium MythBusters](https://www.greatbarrierreefaquarium.au/latest-news/mythbusters-debunking-the-most-common-myths-swirling-about-the-great-barrier-reef-aquariumnbsp).

**3. "Reef HQ Aquarium is a top Townsville attraction."** — **STALE.**
Still listed as "Top Choice" by [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/australia/townsville/attractions/reef-hq-aquarium/a/poi-sig/1229153/362394). It has been closed for over five years. Treat any aggregator carrying this listing as unreviewed since 2021.

**4. "Cruise ships over 238 m anchor off Magnetic Island and tender passengers ashore with SeaLink."** — **STALE since 2024.**
Correct: the **$251 million Channel Upgrade** widened the channel from **92 m to 180 m** and **ships up to 300 m now berth alongside at Berth 10 / Quayside Terminal**. Marked by **Queen Elizabeth (294 m) berthing on 14 January 2025** — the largest ship ever to visit.
Sources: [Port of Townsville — Channel Upgrade](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/projects-development/channel-upgrade/); [Port of Townsville — QE arrival](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/townsville-port-celebrates-channel-upgrade-completion-with-queen-elizabeth-s-arrival/); [Townsville Enterprise](https://www.townsvilleenterprise.com.au/tourism/get-cruise-ready/).

**5. The Port of Townsville's own Berth Information PDF is STALE.**
It still states "The channel is 92 m wide and has a design depth of 11.7 m" and "The maximum size of a vessel accommodated by the Port is 238 m LOA, 32.2 m beam and 13.1 m draft," and lists Berth 10's Max Vessel LOA as 238 m. These are **pre-upgrade figures**, contradicted by the port's own project and news pages and by the physical fact of a 294 m ship berthing in January 2025.
**Do not cite this PDF for channel or LOA figures.** Its **berth pocket length (320 m)** and **declared depth (12.0 m LAT)** for Berth 10 appear still valid. [Berth Information PDF](https://s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/os-data-2/port-townsville/documents/berth_information_website_final.pdf)

**6. "The Channel Upgrade built a new cruise/superyacht terminal."** — **WRONG PREMISE.**
Correct: the Channel Upgrade was **dredging and reclamation only**. The passenger facility, **Quayside Terminal, opened in October 2013** and received a separate **$5.9 million upgrade for the 2024–25 season** (bus zone 4 → 12 coaches, kiss-and-ride, seating, shade, landscaping). **No superyacht terminal was found in any source.**
Sources: [Port of Townsville — Channel Upgrade](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/projects-development/channel-upgrade/); [Port of Townsville — Terminal Transformation](https://www.townsville-port.com.au/townsville-cruise-terminal-transformation-supports-local-economy/); [Queensland Government statement](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/101038).

**7. "Museum of Tropical Queensland."** — **RENAMED.**
Correct: **Queensland Museum Tropics**. [QM Tropics](https://www.museum.qld.gov.au/tropics/plan-your-visit/hours-and-admission/)

**8. "Museum admission is about $15–16 for an adult."** — **STALE.**
Correct: **"Entry to all North Queensland galleries including Pandora and the Sciencentre is free."** Only some temporary exhibitions are ticketed. [QM Tropics](https://www.museum.qld.gov.au/tropics/plan-your-visit/hours-and-admission/)

**9. "Cyclone Kirrily made landfall near Townsville as a Category 3."** — **WRONG.**
Correct: **peak Category 3 (65 kt) offshore at 1pm 25 January 2024; crossed the coast as Category 1** near Balgal Beach / Rollingstone, ~50 km NW of Townsville, at **10pm AEST 25 January 2024**.
Stated incorrectly by [CIRA](https://satlib.cira.colostate.edu/event/tropical-cyclone-kirrily/) and others. Corrected by [BoM](https://www.bom.gov.au/cyclone/history/kirrily24.shtml). **BoM is authoritative — it assigns Australian-scale intensities and issues official post-analysis.**

**10. "Townsville bus fares are $3–5."** — **STALE.**
Correct: **Queensland's 50 cent flat fare applies to regional urban bus services in 14 areas including Townsville.** Every journey, any distance, 50 cents. [Translink](https://translink.com.au/tickets-and-fares/50-cent-fares)

**11. "Magnetic Island ferry adult return is $30" / "$42."** — **BOTH IMPRECISE.**
Correct as at mid-2026: **$40.50 standard adult return; $38.00 purchased online.** A **fuel surcharge applied from 1 June 2026** on top; fares last changed **1 February 2026**. [SeaLink fares](https://www.sealink.com.au/magnetic-island/ferry-information/magnetic-island-ferry-fares/)

**12. "Lyndon Johnson investigated the Townsville mutiny."** — **WRONG.**
Correct: **Johnson was visiting Townsville at the time (22–23 May 1942) and his presence contributed to the cover-up. He did not conduct the investigation.** [Wikipedia — Townsville mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsville_mutiny)

**13. "The Battle of Townsville" as the standard name for the 1942 mutiny.** — **AMBIGUOUS.**
The phrase is used loosely for **both** the July 1942 Japanese air raids **and** the May 1942 mutiny. The established name for the latter is **the Townsville mutiny**. Use that; note the alternative.

**14. "Forts Walk takes 1.5 hours" vs "2 hours."** — **BOTH ARE RIGHT, for different things.**
**Queensland Parks' official time is 2 hours**; the **typical walking time is ~1.5 hours**. The difference is the detours to individual gun emplacements and the command post. State both. [Queensland Parks](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/magnetic-island/journeys/forts-walk); [Trail Hiking Australia](https://www.trailhiking.com.au/hikes/forts-walk-magnetic-island-national-park/)

**15. "Magnetic Island is named for its magnetic effect on compasses."** — **HISTORICALLY STATED, NOT SUBSTANTIATED.**
Cook named it "Magnetical Island" in 1770 **attributing** compass interference to it. No magnetic anomaly has since been confirmed. Write it as Cook's attribution, not as fact. [Wikipedia — Magnetic Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_Island)

**16. Crocodile management guidance citing the 2017 plan.** — **SUPERSEDED.**
A **new Queensland Crocodile Management Plan was announced in December 2025**. Townsville City Council is a **Targeted Crocodile Removal Zone (TCR)**. [Queensland Government](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/animals/living-with/crocodiles/crocodile-management/crocodile-management-plan); [Ministerial statement](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/104143)

**17. "Stinger nets make swimming safe."** — **DANGEROUSLY INCOMPLETE.**
Enclosures are designed to exclude **box jellyfish**. **Irukandji are small enough to pass through the mesh.** Any guide implying a netted enclosure is stinger-proof is creating a safety risk. *(See the sourcing caveat in §6 — the claim is well-established but needs a primary SLSQ or Queensland Health citation before publication.)*

**18. Volendam "fits comfortably" is true now but would not have been before 2024.**
At **237.6 m LOA and 32.3 m beam**, she was **marginal against the pre-upgrade limits of 238 m LOA and 32.2 m beam** — over on beam. Post-upgrade (300 m, 180 m channel) she is routine. Worth noting rather than glossing.
