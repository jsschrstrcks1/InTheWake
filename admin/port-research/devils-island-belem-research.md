# PART 1 — ÎLES DU SALUT / "DEVIL'S ISLAND", FRENCH GUIANA

## 1. The geography — and the single most-repeated error about this call

**Your prior is correct, and it is not a marginal correction — it is the central fact of the day.**

The Îles du Salut are **three** islands roughly 11–14 km off Kourou in the Atlantic:

| Island | Area | Max elevation | Status for visitors |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Île Royale** | 28 ha | 66 m | The island you land on. Auberge, restaurant, museum, prison ruins. |
| **Île Saint-Joseph** | 20 ha | 30 m | Landable, sea state permitting. Réclusion cells, cemetery, beach. |
| **Île du Diable** (Devil's Island) | 14 ha | 40 m | **Landing strictly prohibited.** Viewed from Royale only. |

Areas and elevations: [Wikipédia FR, Îles du Salut](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Eles_du_Salut). Distance offshore: CNES gives "une vingtaine de kilomètres au large de Kourou"; the Guyane tourism board gives "14 km au large de Kourou" ([Comité du Tourisme de la Guyane](https://www.guyane-amazonie.fr/activites-de-loisirs/ASCCTG973V50005U/detail/kourou/iles-du-salut)); English Wikipedia gives ~14 km ([Devil's Island (Kourou)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil's_Island_(Kourou))). **Use "about 14 km"** — the CTG figure is the local authority's and matches Wikipedia; CNES's "twenty-odd km" is a rounded approximation.

### Île du Diable cannot be landed on — confirmed from the managing authority

CNES, which **owns** the archipelago, states plainly that Île du Diable "reste **interdite au débarquement** en raison des conditions de mer" — *remains forbidden for landing because of sea conditions* ([CNES, Les îles du Salut](https://cnes.fr/geoimage/iles-salut)). The French Wikipedia article puts it the same way: "l'île du Diable, la plus au nord, étant **strictement interdite d'accès**, notamment à cause des forts courants" ([fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Eles_du_Salut)).

The physical reason: the island is ringed by "rocky promontories and shoals, strong cross-currents and shark-infested waters" ([Devil's Island (Kourou), en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil's_Island_(Kourou))). There is no jetty and never has been one usable in normal sea states.

**The cable car.** Because boat landing was impracticable, the prison administration strung an aerial cableway — a *va-et-vient* — across the ~180–250 m channel between Île Royale and Île du Diable to move supplies, mail and (occasionally) people. English Wikipedia gives the span as 180 m (600 ft) and confirms it was "used for years"; French sources describe it as a *va-et-vient* for provisioning the detainees, hauled by convicts, and **no longer in service** ([Devil's Island (Kourou)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil's_Island_(Kourou)); [Criminocorpus, Ullmo exhibition](https://criminocorpus.org/fr/expositions/charles-benjamin-ullmo-matricule-2/deuxieme-partie-quinze-ans-sur-lile-du-diable-17-juillet-1908-15-mars-1923/)). The island's permanent population under the bagne never exceeded about ten people: two warders plus family and three or four convicts to do the work and haul the cable (same Criminocorpus source).

### What a passenger can and cannot do — say this plainly

- **CAN:** land by tender on **Île Royale**; walk the ring path; see the prison buildings, church, hospital, warders' quarter, cemeteries; look across the channel at Île du Diable from ~200 m and photograph it; take the small inter-island shuttle to **Île Saint-Joseph** if it is running and the ship's schedule allows.
- **CANNOT:** set foot on Île du Diable. Not on a ship excursion, not privately, not by paying extra. There is no legal or practical way to land.

**Marketing language to distrust.** Cruise lines and port-day guides routinely bill this call as "Devil's Island." The *archipelago* is colloquially called Devil's Island in English, and that usage is centuries old — but a passenger reading it will reasonably expect to walk on Île du Diable, and will not. Any brochure line implying a landing on Devil's Island is wrong. The honest framing is: *"you land on Île Royale, the administrative island of the penal colony, and you see Devil's Island across the water."*

## 2. The call mechanics

### Tender, not alongside

**Tender.** There is no berth on any of the three islands capable of taking a 237 m ship. Ships anchor off Île Royale and tender in; smaller ships tender passengers to the main jetty on Royale ([whatsinport.com, Îles du Salut](https://www.whatsinport.com/Iles-du-Salut.htm); [CruiseMapper, Iles du Salut port](https://www.cruisemapper.com/ports/iles-du-salut-port-1990)). The regular Kourou–Royale shuttle catamaran also lands at the same Île Royale jetty ([Promaritime Guyane](https://www.promaritimeguyane.fr/a-propos-de-nous)).

Île Saint-Joseph got a **floating pontoon in 2019**, but CNES notes its use "reste tributaire de la houle et des courants" — *remains dependent on swell and currents* ([CNES](https://cnes.fr/geoimage/iles-salut)). That single sentence is the best available official statement on how marginal boat transfers here are.

### How rough, and how often cancelled

**UNVERIFIED — I could not source a cancellation rate.** No port authority, CNES page, or line publishes one, and I found no incident database for this call. What *can* be said on evidence:

- The islands sit in open Atlantic with no sheltering landmass; the channels between them carry "forts courants" strong enough that the managing authority bans landing on one island outright.
- The Kourou shuttle's departure times are tide-dependent because the Kourou river channel is shallow and silted ([Routard, Île Royale](https://www.routard.com/fr/guide/ameriques/guyane/cote-atlantique/iles-du-salut/ile-royale)) — that constrains local boats, not a ship anchored offshore, but it indicates the local sea/tide regime.
- Feb–April is inside the main rainy season (see §7), which brings squalls but not the strongest swell.

Treat this as a **genuine tender call with real weather risk**, and do not promise it. Do not repeat any specific cancellation percentage you see in travel writing — I found no sourced figure anywhere.

### Who manages the islands — CNES

**Yes, your prior is right, and the reason is exactly the one you guessed.**

- **CNES has owned the Îles du Salut since 1971** ([CNES](https://cnes.fr/geoimage/iles-salut)). CNES's own copy notes it is "la seule agence spatiale au monde propriétaire de terrains inscrits à l'inventaire des monuments historiques" — *the only space agency in the world that owns land listed in the historic monuments register.*
- The reason is the rocket trajectory: the islands lie "sous la trajectoire des lancements vers l'est" — under the flight path of eastward (equatorial) launches from the Centre Spatial Guyanais at Kourou ([CNES](https://cnes.fr/geoimage/iles-salut)).
- CNES manages the land in coordination with the **Conservatoire du littoral**, the French coastal-protection body ([official Mérimée record, see below](https://pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/merimee/PA00105905)).
- Gendarmerie maintain a presence on the islands ([Gendarmerie nationale, Gendinfo](https://www.gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr/gendinfo/dossiers/la-gendarmerie-en-guyane-securiser-et-proteger-un-territoire-d-exception/en-guyane-les-gendarmes-se-font-les-gardiens-des-iles-du-salut)).

**Heritage status:** the whole archipelago — Royale, Saint-Joseph and Diable, including all penal-colony structures, Amerindian remains and the land itself — was **inscrit au titre des monuments historiques by arrêté of 21 November 2012**, reference **PA00105905**, commune of Cayenne ([Plateforme Ouverte du Patrimoine / base Mérimée, Ministère de la Culture](https://pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/merimee/PA00105905)). Earlier protections in 1986 and 2000 were annulled or superseded. Separately, the **case Dreyfus** (Dreyfus's hut on Île du Diable) was **classée** in 1987 and restored in 1993–94 ([France-Guyane, "Les îles du Salut, une inscription historique"](https://www.franceguyane.fr/actualite/culture-et-patrimoine/patrimoine-les-iles-du-salut-une-inscription-historique-584119.php)).

### The launch-day closure issue — this can kill the call outright

This is the single most under-reported operational risk of this port.

The archipelago sits under the eastward launch corridor and **must be evacuated for every equatorial launch**. CNES: "pour garantir la sécurité des personnes, les îles, placées sous la trajectoire des lanceurs, sont évacuées à chaque lancement" ([CNES, Les îles du Salut](https://cnes.fr/iles-salut)). To enforce it, the **Préfecture issues a navigation-ban order (arrêté préfectoral) before each launch**; evacuation must be effective from the specified day until **one hour after the end of the launch window**, and the order **stays in force through launch postponements**, though it may be temporarily suspended to let transport operators reach the islands (same CNES source).

Everyone leaves except the auberge keeper and a few gendarmes ([fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Eles_du_Salut)).

**Practical read:** a launch or a scrubbed-and-slipped launch on the ship's call date closes the islands and the call is lost. Ariane 6 and Vega-C launch cadence from Kourou is the variable. Anyone planning a 2028 call should check the CSG launch manifest against the itinerary date, and understand that a *postponed* launch keeps the ban alive — the risk window is wider than the launch date itself.

### Entry fee

**No landing/entry fee for the islands is documented.** What is documented:

- **Guided walking tour of Île Royale: €7.50, about 2 hours**, covering the church, hospital, children's cemetery and the warders' quarter ([Routard, Île Royale](https://www.routard.com/fr/guide/ameriques/guyane/cote-atlantique/iles-du-salut/ile-royale)).
- **Royale → Saint-Joseph inter-island shuttle: €5 round trip**, departures 09:30, 12:00, 14:00, 15:30 — and **cash only**: "Munissez-vous d'argent liquide car les autres moyens de paiement vous seront refusés" (*bring cash, other payment methods will be refused*) ([Îles du Salut official access page](https://ilesdusalut.wixsite.com/ilesdusalut/compagnie-maritime)).

Whether the ship's tender operation attracts any per-head fee to CNES is **UNVERIFIED**.

### What is on Île Royale

- **The Auberge des Îles du Salut** — the only establishment on the archipelago, housed in former penal-colony buildings on Royale, with a **restaurant** and accommodation ranging from hotel rooms to hammock spaces in old bagne buildings ([Air Caraïbes](https://www.aircaraibes.com/iles-du-salut-guyane); [CTG](https://www.guyane-amazonie.fr/activites-de-loisirs/ASCCTG973V50005U/detail/kourou/iles-du-salut); [Routard](https://www.routard.com/fr/guide/ameriques/guyane/cote-atlantique/iles-du-salut/ile-royale)). CTG confirms: "un restaurant vous accueille sur l'Ile Royale."
- **Former prison buildings** — the administrative penitentiary structures and common-prisoner quarters; Royale was the archipelago's administrative centre and held the most dangerous *transportés* ([Criminocorpus/HUGO, Pénitencier des Îles du Salut](https://hugo.criminocorpus.org/fr/lieu/penitencier-des-iles-du-salut/)).
- **The church** — on the standard guided route ([Routard](https://www.routard.com/fr/guide/ameriques/guyane/cote-atlantique/iles-du-salut/ile-royale)).
- **The hospital** — Royale operated the colony's main hospital, completed 1864 ([Criminocorpus/HUGO](https://hugo.criminocorpus.org/fr/lieu/penitencier-des-iles-du-salut/)).
- **Cemeteries** — including the **cimetière des enfants** (children's cemetery), on the guided route ([Routard](https://www.routard.com/fr/guide/ameriques/guyane/cote-atlantique/iles-du-salut/ile-royale)). Convicts were not buried; their bodies went to the sea.
- **A small museum** with historical context ([Guyane Experience](https://www.guyane-experience.com/iles-du-salut-le-guide-honnete-pour-une-visite-qui-depasse-le-bagne)).

**Wildlife.** Agoutis and iguanas are commonly seen, plus seabirds including frigatebirds and terns ([Guyane Experience](https://www.guyane-experience.com/iles-du-salut-le-guide-honnete-pour-une-visite-qui-depasse-le-bagne)). Monkeys — usually reported as capuchins — and macaws are very widely described in visitor accounts of Île Royale, but I could **not** source them from CNES, the Conservatoire du littoral, or the tourism board. **Mark the monkeys as UNVERIFIED at the authority level**, though the reports are numerous and consistent. Whatever is there, feeding it is a bad idea.

**Swimming — the actual advice.** Do not free-swim off Royale. Open-water swimming around the islands is described as dangerous because of strong currents, and historically the waters were shark-heavy — attracted, among other things, by the abattoir on Royale ([search-consolidated French sources incl. Routard, Carnet de Route](https://www.routard.com/fr/guide/ameriques/guyane/cote-atlantique/iles-du-salut)). The safe option is the **"piscine des bagnards"** — a natural rock pool on Île Royale that the convicts built/adapted so they could wash sheltered from sharks. Île Saint-Joseph has a small cove/beach, coconut-fringed and partly protected by a rock bar, but currents there can still be dangerous (same sources). For a ship's day this is at best an incidental dip, not a beach call.

## 3. Île Saint-Joseph

**Accessible — conditionally.** A floating pontoon was installed in 2019, but CNES warns its usability "reste tributaire de la houle et des courants" ([CNES](https://cnes.fr/geoimage/iles-salut)). The €5 shuttle from Royale is cash-only, four departures a day ([Îles du Salut access page](https://ilesdusalut.wixsite.com/ilesdusalut/compagnie-maritime)). All three of the local catamaran operators (La Hulotte, Tropic Alizés, L'Albatros) include Saint-Joseph in their day trips — but those serve Kourou day-trippers, not ship's tenders. **Whether a cruise call can practically add Saint-Joseph inside a port day depends entirely on the ship's tender schedule; assume most passengers will not get there.**

**What is there.** Saint-Joseph held the **réclusion cellulaire** — the solitary-confinement quarters, built in **1898** ([Criminocorpus/HUGO](https://hugo.criminocorpus.org/fr/lieu/penitencier-des-iles-du-salut/)). This was the punishment island: recalcitrant convicts and recaptured escapees were sent here, into individual cells with barred open ceilings so warders could walk the catwalk above in silence. The **cemetery** here is the warders'/free-population cemetery.

**Current state of the ruins.** Roofless, jungle-invaded, structurally degraded — the cell blocks stand but the vegetation is taking them. Guyane Experience describes the island as "plus basse et plus austère" (*lower and more austere*) than Royale, with "alignements de cellules de réclusion" (*rows of solitary-confinement cells*) ([Guyane Experience](https://www.guyane-experience.com/iles-du-salut-le-guide-honnete-pour-une-visite-qui-depasse-le-bagne)). One reason Île du Diable is closed is explicitly that its buildings are in bad condition; Saint-Joseph is in better but not good repair. There is no restoration programme documented at the level of Royale's.

## 4. The penal colony — history, precisely

### Dates of operation

- **1852** — first *transportés* land in French Guiana; the system is established by decree of **27 March 1852** ([Criminocorpus/HUGO](https://hugo.criminocorpus.org/fr/lieu/penitencier-des-iles-du-salut/); [Bagne de la Guyane française, fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagne_de_la_Guyane_fran%C3%A7aise)).
- **1938** — transportation abolished by Popular Front décret-loi. New convoys stop; existing convicts stay.
- **1946** — Îles du Salut penitentiary evacuated and closed ([Criminocorpus/HUGO](https://hugo.criminocorpus.org/fr/lieu/penitencier-des-iles-du-salut/)). CNES gives 1947 for the definitive end of prison operations ([CNES](https://cnes.fr/geoimage/iles-salut)).
- **1 August 1953** — the last repatriated convicts leave Guiana: "Les derniers bagnards rapatriés quittent la Guyane le 1er août 1953" ([fr.wikipedia, Bagne de la Guyane française](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagne_de_la_Guyane_fran%C3%A7aise)).

**On the dates, be careful.** You will see "1852–1953" (the full span from first arrivals to last departure), "1854–1947" (CNES/Wikipedia's operational framing), and "1852–1946" (the islands specifically). All three are defensible for different things. The cleanest formulation: *convict transportation to French Guiana ran 1852–1938; the Îles du Salut penitentiary closed in 1946; the last convicts left Guiana on 1 August 1953.*

### How many were sent

French Wikipedia, drawing on the standard literature, breaks it down by legal category ([fr.wikipedia, Bagne de la Guyane française](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagne_de_la_Guyane_fran%C3%A7aise)):

| Category | Period | Number |
|---|---|---|
| **Transportation** (hard labour) | 1852–1938 | 77,520 men + 315 women |
| **Relegation** (habitual offenders) | 1887–1970 | 22,163 men + 519 women |
| **Deportation** (political) | 1848–1938 | 329 persons |

Michel Pierre, the standard historian of the bagne (*Bagnards. La terre de la grande punition, Cayenne 1852–1953*, Autrement, 2000), gives **approximately 70,000** as the total who served sentences in Guiana across all three categories, first arrivals 1852, last 1938 ([publisher listing, Decitre](https://www.decitre.fr/livres/bagnards-9782746700215.html)). A French Ministry of Justice study gives **54,288 transportés (52,905 men, 393 women, 990 other) for 1852–1953** — a subset covering transportation only ([Direction de l'administration pénitentiaire, *Cahiers d'études pénitentiaires et criminologiques* n°39](https://www.justice.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/migrations/portail/art_pix/cahiers_detudes_39.pdf)).

**These do not reconcile, and you should say so rather than pick one.** The Wikipedia transportation figure (77,520) counts sentences pronounced/convoys; the Justice Ministry figure (54,288) counts a narrower population. Michel Pierre's ~70,000 is the figure to quote if you quote one, attributed to him, for *all categories combined*. **Never write "70,000 prisoners passed through Devil's Island"** — that conflates the whole Guiana system with three small islands that held a few hundred at a time.

### The *bagne* system and the islands vs. the mainland

The critical distinction, and the one travel writing gets wrong: **the Îles du Salut were not the deadly part of the bagne.**

- The islands were the **administrative and disciplinary** core: Royale for administration, the hospital, and dangerous convicts; Saint-Joseph for solitary confinement; Diable for political deportees ([Criminocorpus/HUGO](https://hugo.criminocorpus.org/fr/lieu/penitencier-des-iles-du-salut/); [CTG](https://www.guyane-amazonie.fr/activites-de-loisirs/ASCCTG973V50005U/detail/kourou/iles-du-salut)).
- The **mainland camps** killed people. Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni (the Camp de la Transportation, the great convict depot), Cayenne, Mana (women), and above all the forest camps — **Charvein**, known as the *"Camp de la Mort"* — plus the notorious road-building details ([fr.wikipedia, Bagne de la Guyane française](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagne_de_la_Guyane_fran%C3%A7aise)). Malaria, yellow fever, dysentery and forced labour in the interior, not island confinement, produced the mortality.
- Sea breezes, and the fact that the islands were malaria-free relative to the mainland, made a posting to Royale comparatively survivable. The islands' reputation as "le bagne le plus dur au monde" (CNES's own phrase) is about their symbolic and disciplinary role, and about Dreyfus, not about the death rate.

**Mortality.** French sources state that sanitary conditions were so bad that average life expectancy in the system did not exceed **3 to 5 years** ([Bagne de Cayenne, fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagne_de_Cayenne)). Treat that as a widely repeated figure for the system as a whole, weighted by the mainland camps; **I found no per-island mortality data — UNVERIFIED.**

### The *doublage* rule

Established by the **loi du 30 mai 1854** ([Bagne de Cayenne, fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagne_de_Cayenne); [fr.wikipedia, Bagne de la Guyane française](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagne_de_la_Guyane_fran%C3%A7aise)):

- Convicts sentenced to **less than 8 years** hard labour had to remain resident in French Guiana, after release, **for a period equal to their sentence**.
- Convicts sentenced to **8 years or more** had to remain in Guiana **for life**.

This is why "released" convicts — the *libérés* — accumulated as a destitute underclass in Cayenne and Saint-Laurent, and why so few ever came home. Doublage, not the sentence, is what made the bagne a life sentence in practice. It is the single most useful thing to explain to a passenger standing on Royale.

### Alfred Dreyfus — exact dates and conditions

- Convicted December 1894; publicly degraded 5 January 1895.
- Shipped to Guiana; **interned on Île du Diable from 13 April 1895 to 9 June 1899** — **1,517 days** ([INA, "Dans quelles conditions le capitaine Dreyfus a été interné 1517 jours sur l'île du Diable"](https://www.ina.fr/ina-eclaire-actu/dreyfus-ile-au-diable-guyane-bagne-antisemitisme-armee-justice); [Herodote.net](https://www.herodote.net/Le_bagne_en_solitaire-synthese-2629-254.php)).
- **Beware the earlier March 1895 dates you will see** (8 or 12 March 1895): those are his arrival *in Guiana / at the Îles du Salut*, not on Île du Diable. He was held on Royale first. The Île du Diable internment date is 13 April 1895.
- He was the island's **sole prisoner**, in a small stone hut (*la case*), guarded around the clock by a rotating detail of warders forbidden to speak to him. In September 1896, after a false escape rumour, conditions were tightened savagely: a double palisade was built to block his view of the sea, and he was shackled at night to his bed by the *double boucle* — a bar-and-ring device pinning his ankles. He was released to France after the Cour de cassation quashed the verdict; he sailed 9 June 1899, was retried at Rennes, and was finally exonerated in 1906.
- **Fully rehabilitated 12 July 1906.** ([Ministère de la Culture Dreyfus site — note: `dreyfus.culture.fr` no longer resolves as of 30 July 2026; the INA and Herodote sources above are the working references.])
- Dreyfus's hut on Île du Diable was **classée monument historique in 1987** and restored 1993–94 ([France-Guyane](https://www.franceguyane.fr/actualite/culture-et-patrimoine/patrimoine-les-iles-du-salut-une-inscription-historique-584119.php)) — but you still cannot go and see it.
- A footnote worth having: **Charles Benjamin Ullmo**, a naval officer convicted of treason in 1908, spent fifteen years on Île du Diable (17 July 1908 – 15 March 1923) — "dans la case même où vécut Dreyfus," in the very hut where Dreyfus lived ([Criminocorpus, Ullmo exhibition](https://criminocorpus.org/fr/expositions/charles-benjamin-ullmo-matricule-2/deuxieme-partie-quinze-ans-sur-lile-du-diable-17-juillet-1908-15-mars-1923/); [fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Eles_du_Salut)). Ullmo, not Dreyfus, was Devil's Island's long-term inhabitant.

### Henri Charrière / "Papillon" — what is disputed, specifically

The book is a bestseller and largely a fabrication. Be specific about which parts.

**What the records establish** ([Henri Charrière, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Charri%C3%A8re), citing French penal and justice-ministry records):

- Convicted **26 October 1931** of the murder of **Roland Le Petit**, a pimp — a charge Charrière always denied. Sentence: life at hard labour.
- Departed **Saint-Martin-de-Ré on 29 September 1933**; arrived **Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni on 14 October 1933**.
- Worked as a **nursing assistant at the André-Bouron colonial hospital**.
- **He was never held on Île du Diable.** The book's central set-piece — the coconut-sack leap from Devil's Island cliffs, riding the seventh wave — did not happen to him.
- He escaped on **18–19 March 1944**, from the **Cascades forest camp** — a mainland camp, not the islands.
- Pardoned 1970; died of throat cancer in Madrid, **29 July 1973**.

**The demolition.** Gérard de Villiers' **_Papillon épinglé_ (1970)** — researched in the penal archives — concluded that "**only about 10 percent** of Charrière's book represents the truth." A French justice ministry assessment said the book "should be divided by at least 10 to get near the truth." Charrière's own defence: he claimed the work was "**75 percent true**," and excused the discrepancies with "I didn't have a typewriter with me" (all: [en.wikipedia, Henri Charrière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Charri%C3%A8re)).

**What historians actually think happened.** Charrière compiled the escape narratives and prison experiences of other convicts into a single first-person account. English Wikipedia's summary of the current position: "modern researchers believe that much of the book's material came from other inmates, rather than Charrière himself" ([Papillon (book)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papillon_(book))). Two named candidates for material Charrière absorbed: **Charles Brunier**, a bagne convict with a butterfly tattoo who in 2005, aged 104, claimed to be the real Papillon; and **Clément Duval**, the anarchist Devil's Island escapee and memoirist (both: [Papillon (book)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papillon_(book))).

**The honest line for a passenger:** *Papillon is a novel with a real man's name on it. The bagne it describes existed and was worse than most of what it describes. Charrière's own share of the story is small, and he was never on the island in the title.*

### René Belbenoît

Belbenoît (1899–1959) was transported to French Guiana in 1923, escaped repeatedly, finally got out via British Guiana and reached the United States, and wrote **_Dry Guillotine: Fifteen Years Among the Living Dead_ (1938)** and **_Hell on Trial_ (1940)**. *Dry Guillotine* was a bestseller in the US and is generally credited with contributing to the political pressure that ended transportation in 1938. **His account is considered substantially more reliable than Charrière's** — he really did serve the time and really did escape — but it is a memoir written for effect and is not itself an archival source. **I could not source Belbenoît's specifics from a primary or scholarly authority in this research pass — treat the biographical detail above as UNVERIFIED pending a check against ANOM records or Michel Pierre.**

### An archival note worth knowing

The **Archives nationales d'outre-mer (ANOM)** in Aix-en-Provence hold the convict *registres matricules*. Only the matricule cards are digitised and online, not the full dossiers, and **the online search is limited to persons incarcerated before 1890** ([Revue française de Généalogie](https://www.rfgenealogie.com/infos/les-registres-matricules-des-bagnards-en-ligne-sur-le-site-des-anom)). Charrière (convicted 1931) is therefore not in the public online index — which is why so much loose claim survives around him.

## 5. Kourou and the Guiana Space Centre

**Relationship to the call:** the CSG at Kourou is why the islands are owned by a space agency, why they are evacuated for launches, and why they were preserved rather than sold. It is ~14 km away across open water.

**Is it a feasible excursion from the ship?** In practice, **no, not on a tender day at the islands.** The reasons are hard:

- The ship anchors at the islands, not at Kourou. Getting to the CSG means a boat transfer to Kourou (about an hour on the scheduled shuttle) plus a road transfer, plus the tour, plus the return — inside a single port day, with tender queues at both ends.
- **CSG guided tours are free but require compulsory advance booking through CNES, at least 48 hours ahead, with limited places.** They run as a **3-hour bus tour** covering the Ariane 5, Ariane 6 and Vega launch zones and the Jupiter 2 control centre; minimum age 8; **photo ID or passport required for entry** ([summary of CNES booking conditions via Jumbo Car Guyane 2026 guide](https://www.jumbocar-guyane.com/que-faire-guyane/activites-decouvertes/centre-spatial-guyanais); [Air Caraïbes](https://www.aircaraibes.com/destinations/guyane/visiter-centre-spatial)).
- The **Musée de l'Espace reopened in July 2024 as "Guyaspace Expérience"**, a paid, interactive/immersive museum ([Guyane Amazonie / CTG listing](https://www.guyane-amazonie.fr/activites-de-loisirs/ASCCTG973V500072/detail/kourou/guyaspace-experience-centre-spatial-guyanais)).

If the ship berths or tenders at **Kourou or Dégrad des Cannes** instead — some itineraries do — the CSG becomes feasible, but only as a pre-booked ship excursion, because of the CNES booking lead time and ID requirement.

**The launch-viewing angle.** Île Royale is one of the classic *viewing* positions for a Kourou launch — the trajectory passes overhead. But this is a trap: because the islands are evacuated for every launch, **you cannot be standing on Île Royale watching a launch go over.** Sea-level launch viewing from a ship standing off is theoretically spectacular and completely outside a scheduled port call's control. If the itinerary date coincides with a launch, the realistic outcome is that **the port call is cancelled**, not that passengers get a show.

## 6. Practicalities

**Status and currency.** French Guiana (Guyane) is an **overseas department and region of France** and an **outermost region of the European Union**. Currency: the **euro (€)**.

**Entry requirements — and the trap.** French Guiana is **part of the EU but NOT part of the Schengen Area.** A Schengen visa — even one issued by France — **is not valid for entry into French Guiana**. Nationals who need a visa must hold one specifically issued for Guyane or for the French overseas departments; the *départements français d'Amérique* (Guadeloupe, Martinique, Guyane) are covered by a common visa, but that is separate from Schengen ([France-Visas, Guyane](https://france-visas.gouv.fr/en/guyana); [France-Visas, La France dans l'espace Schengen](https://france-visas.gouv.fr/en/la-france-dans-l-espace-schengen); [Direction générale des étrangers en France — dispenses de visas](https://www.immigration.interieur.gouv.fr/limmigration-en-france/visas-en-france/dispenses-de-visas)). Most cruise passengers on a US/UK/EU/Canadian/Australian passport are visa-exempt for short stays and will not notice; passengers on passports that need a French visa **must not assume a Schengen visa works**.

**Yellow fever.** **Vaccination against yellow fever is mandatory for entry to French Guiana** for all travellers above 9 months (some sources say 1 year); without a valid International Certificate of Vaccination, entry can be refused. One injection, at least 10 days before travel; protection is treated as lifelong under current French rules ([Ambassade de France, "Going to French Guiana: yellow fever vaccination"](https://se.ambafrance.org/Going-to-French-Guiana-yellow-fever-vaccination); [NaTHNaC/TravelHealthPro, French Guiana](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/country/82/french-guiana)).

**In practice on a cruise ship this is enforced unevenly** — some lines require the certificate at embarkation for the whole voyage, some do not check for a tender call. **Passengers should carry the yellow card regardless.** The requirement is real and is the department's own rule, not a recommendation.

**Other mosquito-borne disease.** Dengue, chikungunya and Zika all circulate in French Guiana; malaria risk exists but is concentrated in the interior and along the Maroni and Oyapock, not the coastal strip or the islands ([NaTHNaC, French Guiana](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/country/82/french-guiana)). For a day on Île Royale, **the practical advice is insect repellent, not antimalarials** — but the day-biting *Aedes* species that carry dengue/chikungunya are the ones you actually meet, so repellent matters during daylight, which is the whole visit.

**Language.** French. English is not widely spoken on the islands beyond the auberge staff and guides. The guided tour is in French unless the ship supplies its own guide.

**Connectivity.** French mobile networks reach the islands (they are a French department; there is a permanent inhabited establishment and a gendarmerie presence), but coverage 14 km offshore is not to be relied on. **UNVERIFIED — I found no authoritative statement of coverage or of Wi-Fi at the auberge.** Assume patchy-to-none and plan accordingly.

**Cards and cash.** **Carry euros in cash.** The islands' own access page is explicit that non-cash payment will be refused for the inter-island shuttle ([ilesdusalut.wixsite.com](https://ilesdusalut.wixsite.com/ilesdusalut/compagnie-maritime)). The auberge restaurant likely takes cards; the €7.50 tour and €5 shuttle should be assumed cash-only. There is **no ATM on the islands**.

**Food, water, toilets.** The Auberge restaurant on Île Royale is the only food service; there is no shop, no supermarket, nothing on Saint-Joseph. CNES has at times had to work to restore the **water supply to Royale** ([Blada.com, "Îles du salut : le CNES œuvre pour rétablir la fourniture en eau à Royale"](https://www.blada.com/boite-aux-lettres/infos-citoyennes/18296-Iles_du_salut_le_CNES_oeuvre_pour_retablir_la_fourniture_en_eau_a_Royale.htm)) — **do not assume drinkable tap water.** Bring water from the ship: the standard local advice is **2 litres per person**, plus sun cream, a hat and a light rain jacket ([Guyane Experience](https://www.guyane-experience.com/iles-du-salut-le-guide-honnete-pour-une-visite-qui-depasse-le-bagne)). Toilets exist at the auberge; assume nothing beyond that.

**Heat, humidity and the paths.** Equatorial: ~29–32 °C by day, high humidity, little seasonal variation (see §7). The island paths are unpaved, uneven, root-crossed, and **"sentiers parfois glissants"** — sometimes slippery, especially after rain, which in Feb–April means most days ([Guyane Experience](https://www.guyane-experience.com/iles-du-salut-le-guide-honnete-pour-une-visite-qui-depasse-le-bagne)). **Closed, comfortable, grippy shoes — "chaussures fermées et confortables"** — are the explicit local advice. Île Royale rises to 66 m and the walk is a genuine climb in heat. Flip-flops and smooth-soled deck shoes are the wrong choice, and this is worth saying loudly because passengers step off a tender expecting a beach.

### The seasons

French Guiana's coast runs on a four-season cycle, not a simple wet/dry:

1. **"Petite saison des pluies"** — roughly December to February.
2. **"Petit été de mars"** — the *little March summer*, a short drier break between the two rainy phases, traditionally around late February–early March. **It moves.** Météo-France Guyane reported that in 2026 it came a month early: starting **30 January** and ending around **5 March**, with February 2026 running about a **70 % rainfall deficit** ([Météo-France Guyane, bulletins climatiques mensuels](https://meteofrance.gf/fr/climat/bulletin-climatique-mensuel-de-guyane-mars-2025); [Météo-France Guyane climat](https://meteofrance.gf/fr/climat)).
3. **"Grande saison des pluies"** — roughly April to July, peaking in **May** (471.6 mm, 24 rain days).
4. **"Grande saison sèche"** — August to November, the genuinely dry period; September averages **23.7 mm over 2.9 rain days**.

### What to expect on a February–April world-cruise call

This is the awkward window.

- **February:** ~237 mm across ~15–16 rain days — but this is when the *petit été de mars* may already have started, and in some years February is markedly dry. High variance.
- **March:** the **driest of the three** at ~208 mm / ~15 rain days, on the tail of the little dry season. **March is the best of the three months for this call.**
- **April:** the **wettest** at ~357 mm / ~18.5 rain days, with the great rainy season established. Expect rain on most days.

Across all three: mean maxima 29–30 °C, minima 25–26 °C, humidity high throughout, and essentially no diurnal or seasonal relief. Rain arrives in heavy convective bursts rather than all-day drizzle, so a wet-month call is usually still walkable between showers — but the paths will be slick, and that is the practical consequence.

## 1. The berth — the critical practical question

**Your prior is correct, and I am confident. Belém is a TENDER call at Icoaraci, roughly 18–20 km from the city centre. Cruise ships do not berth downtown.**

Confidence: **high** — this is confirmed by the ship-specific record of Volendam's own recent call, by state and municipal government sources, and by an independent physical constraint (draft).

### What actually happened, with Volendam

On **12 January 2026**, the **ms Volendam** — 900 passengers, mostly North American, having sailed from Port Everglades on 4 January on a voyage through ~20 countries to May — arrived at the **Terminal Hidroviário e Turístico de Icoaraci at about 08:30**. It was Pará's first cruise arrival of 2026 and fell on Belém's founding anniversary ([Agência Pará via search index](https://agenciapara.com.br/noticia/73880/primeiro-navio-de-cruzeiro-de-2026-chega-a-belem-com-900-passageiros-a-bordo); [Bacana News](https://bacananews.com.br/primeiro-navio-de-cruzeiro-de-2026-atraca-em-belem-com-900-turistas-no-aniversario-da-cidade/); [DOL](https://dol.com.br/noticias/para/932173/primeiro-cruzeiro-de-2026-chega-a-belem-com-900-turistas)).

**Note on sourcing:** `agenciapara.com.br`, the Pará state government news agency, is **suspended until the end of the 2026 elections under Brazilian electoral law** (checked 30 July 2026 — the site serves only an electoral notice). Its content is therefore quoted here via search indexing and mirror outlets, not fetched directly. This is a live constraint on verifying Pará state facts right now.

### Why they anchor: draft and siltation

Two independent reasons, both hard:

1. **The downtown commercial quay is too shallow.** The Port of Belém (Reduto district, right bank of Guajará Bay, ~120 km from the Atlantic, administered by **Companhia Docas do Pará**) has a quay with **"pouco mais de sete metros de calado"** — *a little over seven metres of draft* — which **"não permite atracação de grandes navios"** — *does not allow large ships to berth* ([Porto de Belém, pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_de_Bel%C3%A9m); [Ambiente Brasil, Porto de Belém](https://ambientes.ambientebrasil.com.br/agua/hidrovias_e_portos_da_amazonia_legal/porto_de_belem.html)). **Volendam draws ~8.1 m. The number does not work.** The approach itself is a ~110 km natural navigable stretch, 3.2–15 km wide, ~9 m deep, entering Guajará Bay between Ilha da Barra and Ilha do Forte through a 350 m-wide, 10 m-deep gap (same sources).

2. **The Icoaraci foreshore is silted.** The operative statement: **"Os transatlânticos ficam fundeados fora, não podem vir para a orla devido o canal de navegação e o assoreamento do rio. Tecnicamente essas embarcações possuem um calado muito grande e ficariam encalhadas se ficassem na orla."** — *The liners stay anchored offshore; they cannot come to the waterfront because of the navigation channel and the silting of the river. Technically these vessels have a very deep draft and would run aground if they stayed at the waterfront.* (Reported in Pará local coverage of the Icoaraci terminal project; consolidated via [Belemtur](https://belemtur.belem.pa.gov.br/transatlantico-norte-americano-aporta-em-icoaraci-com-1200-turistas/) and [Agência Pará terminal announcement](https://agenciapara.com.br/noticia/25483/icoaraci-vai-ganhar-terminal-hidroviario-turistico-construido-pela-cph).)

So: **the ship anchors in Guajará Bay off Icoaraci; passengers come ashore by launch/tender to the Icoaraci terminal.**

### The Icoaraci terminal itself

**Terminal Hidroviário e Turístico de Icoaraci**, Rua Siqueira Mendes, Icoaraci district, Belém. Inaugurated **8 October 2025** by Governor Helder Barbalho, built and operated by **CPH — Companhia de Portos e Hidrovias do Estado do Pará** (the *state* ports company, **not** the federal Companhia Docas do Pará) ([O Liberal, 8 Oct 2025](https://www.oliberal.com/para/icoaraci-ganha-novo-terminal-hidroviario-turistico-as-vesperas-do-cirio-e-reforca-conexao-com-cotiju-1.1031266); [ALEPA](https://alepa.pa.gov.br/Comunicacao/Noticia/11270/chicao-participa-da-inauguracao-do-terminal-hidroviario-turistico-de-icoaraci)).

Specification ([O Liberal](https://www.oliberal.com/para/icoaraci-ganha-novo-terminal-hidroviario-turistico-as-vesperas-do-cirio-e-reforca-conexao-com-cotiju-1.1031266)):

- **650 m² built area** (some state releases say 1,000 m² — see CORRECTIONS)
- **350 m² operational area**
- **5 floating docks (flutuantes)** and a covered walkway
- **Capacity up to 2,000 passengers per day**
- Ticket windows, accessible toilets, shops, restaurants
- Also the terminal for the river route to **Ilha de Cotijuba** (~27,000 passengers/month), serving 10,000+ island residents
- Project budget originally estimated at **R$ 8 million**

**This is a river-boat terminal that cruise tenders use.** Five floating docks sized for launches; it was not built to take a 237 m ship alongside and cannot.

### What about Outeiro?

**There is now a deep berth — and cruise ships are still not using it.** This is the most important recent nuance.

For COP30, the **Terminal Portuário de Outeiro** was completely requalified, with **a new pier approximately 710 m long** plus passenger reception, boarding and disembarking facilities, at a cost of **over R$ 233 million** funded by Itaipu Binacional. Work started **17 April 2025** and finished before the conference. The purpose was to berth **two transatlantic vessels as floating hotels**, adding ~6,000 beds for 50,000+ COP30 participants. A **cable-stayed bridge linking Outeiro and Icoaraci opened 18 October 2025**, giving ~30-minute transit to the venues ([Diário do Pará, 23 October 2025](https://diariodopara.com.br/belem/terminal-portuario-de-outeiro-quase-pronto-para-receber-transatlanticos/)).

But **CPH's own explanation for the January 2026 calls was that low tide at the ships' arrival and departure times makes the manoeuvres for berthing at Outeiro difficult, "tornando Icoaraci a opção mais viável"** — *making Icoaraci the most viable option* (CPH, reported in Pará coverage of the 2026 season). And a cruise-schedule aggregator reviewing the 2026/27 season notes the modern Outeiro terminal "remains underutilised, with ships preferring locations nearer downtown Belém," and that **"the majority of ships have been anchoring in Guajará Bay"** with transfers by tender ([Portal World Cruises, Belém calls](https://portalworldcruises.com/escalas/belem/)).

**Read for 2028:** the tender-to-Icoaraci pattern is the working assumption. Outeiro alongside is *possible* and the infrastructure now exists, but it is tide-window-constrained and has not become the norm. **Do not write the guide around a downtown berth, and do not write it around Outeiro either.** Write it around a tender to Icoaraci, and flag Outeiro as the thing that could change.

### The transfer to the centre

- **Distance:** Icoaraci to central Belém is **18–20 km** ([Icoaraci, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icoaraci); [Melhores Destinos, Orla de Icoaraci](https://guia.melhoresdestinos.com.br/orla-de-icoaraci-236-6604-l.html)).
- **Time:** allow **40–60 minutes each way** by road in normal traffic; longer at peak. (Derived from distance and Belém's road conditions — **the specific transit time is UNVERIFIED**; no operator-published figure found.)
- **Practical consequence:** on a 08:00–17:00 call, **2 hours of the day is transfer**, before tender queues. Independent passengers face a real decision: go downtown properly, or stay local. Icoaraci itself has a genuine attraction — it is Belém's **ceramics district**, the home of *cerâmica marajoara*-style pottery, with a riverside promenade (Orla de Icoaraci) and workshops. That is a legitimate half-day for someone who does not want to spend two hours in a bus.

## 2. Ver-o-Peso market

### The founding date — your prior is right

**1625 is correct as the origin; 1688 is a later royal provision about revenue, not the founding.**

- **1625:** the Portuguese installed the fiscal and commercial inspection post **"Casa de Haver o Peso"** in the area of the **igarapé do Piri** (the site of today's market), to weigh goods and collect duty on merchandise brought to the seat of the Captaincy of Grão-Pará. IPHAN's official record dates the origin to 1625 at the old Porto do Piri ([IPHAN, Mercado Ver-o-Peso](http://portal.iphan.gov.br/pagina/detalhes/828) — note: this URL now redirects to `gov.br/iphan`; [Complexo do Ver-o-Peso, pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexo_do_Ver-o-Peso), citing IPHAN).
- **21 March 1688:** the **Provisão Régia** by which the King of Portugal granted the **Câmara de Belém the revenue of the Ver-o-Peso**. The provision's own language: it was *"conveniente ao serviço de V. Majestade para que se não embarquem sem despacho as drogas que saem daquela Conquista, e se concedeu aos Officiaes da Câmara da dita capitania o rendimento do dito Ver-o-Peso"* — *convenient to Your Majesty's service that the drugs [drogas do sertão — Amazon forest products] leaving that Conquest not be shipped without clearance, and the revenue of the said Ver-o-Peso was granted to the Officers of the Chamber of the said captaincy.* ([ANPUH XXVI Simpósio Nacional de História proceedings](https://anpuh.org.br/uploads/anais-simposios/pdf/2019-01/1548856591_2d9d882eb730bb354f1f501b933f066e.pdf); corroborated via [Encontra Belém](https://www.encontrabelem.com.br/sobre/ver-o-peso-belem))

**So: 1688 is a fiscal grant to the municipal chamber, 63 years after the weighing station was established.** Guides that give 1688 as "the founding of the market" have collapsed the revenue grant into the origin.

### The name

*Ver-o-Peso* is a corruption of **"haver o peso"** — from the Portuguese *aver/haver de peso*, itself from the French *avoir du poids* (avoirdupois), the Hanseatic weighing standard adopted in Portugal. Goods sold by weight were checked at the *Casa do aver do peso*, where the public scales and measures stood ([Antonio de Moraes Silva, *Diccionario da lingua portugueza*, Lisbon 1813, p.239, cited in pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexo_do_Ver-o-Peso)). "See the weight" is a folk etymology that happens to describe the function.

### Chronology of the site

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1625 | Casa de Haver o Peso established at the igarapé do Piri |
| 21 Mar 1688 | Royal provision grants the Ver-o-Peso revenue to Belém's Câmara |
| 1803 | Igarapé do Piri filled in under the Conde dos Arcos; the mouth becomes the **Doca do Ver-o-Peso** and the Pedra do Peixe, built with English lioz stone |
| Oct 1839 | President Bernardo de Souza Franco abolishes the fiscal office; the building is leased for fresh-fish sales |
| 1847 | Lease ends; **Casa de Haver o Peso demolished** |
| 1867 | Mercado Municipal de Carnes (Mercado Francisco Bolonha — the Meat Market) |
| 1897 | La Rocque Pinto & Cia wins the public tender for the fish market, authorised by municipal law nº 173 |
| 1899 | Construction begins, designed by **Henrique La Rocque** |
| **1901** | **Mercado de Ferro inaugurated** — a dodecagon of 1,197 m², prefabricated *vieille-montagne* zinc/metal structure shipped from England and New York, Art Nouveau / Belle Époque |
| 9 Nov 1977 | **IPHAN tombamento** |

All: [Complexo do Ver-o-Peso, pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexo_do_Ver-o-Peso), citing IPHAN, UFPA's *Ver-o-histórico* project and the INRC Ver-o-Peso inventory.

**Heritage listing:** IPHAN's official designation is **"Conjunto arquitetônico e paisagístico 'Ver-o-Peso' e áreas adjacentes"**, process **812-T-1969**, inscribed **9 November 1977** in three registers — Arqueológico/Etnográfico/Paisagístico (nº 69), Histórico (nº 460), and Belas Artes (nº 525). Protection covers Praça Dom Pedro II, Boulevard Castilhos França, the Meat Market and the Bolonha Fish Market. The first tombamento request was made in 1969 by Ernesto Cruz ([IPHAN SICG record 780](https://sicg.iphan.gov.br/sicg/bem/visualizar/780); [ipatrimônio, Belém – Ver-o-Peso](https://www.ipatrimonio.org/belem-ver-o-peso/)). The complex covers roughly **25,000 m²** ([pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexo_do_Ver-o-Peso)).

- Works executed by Seurb via contractor Multisul; vendor relocation handled by SECON

**Anything describing Ver-o-Peso as dilapidated, or describing the old stall layout, is out of date.**

### What is there

- **Mercado de Ferro** (1901) — the twelve-sided iron-and-zinc fish market with the four turrets, the postcard building. Prefabricated in England/New York, shipped up the river.
- **Mercado Francisco Bolonha** — the 1867 meat market, reopened January 2026.
- **The fish market proper** — best early; the catch (filhote, pirarucu, tambaqui, tucunaré, dourada) comes in overnight and by dawn.
- **Doca do Ver-o-Peso** — the dock, where the açaí boats come in before first light.
- **Feira do Açaí** — the open-air açaí fair **behind the Forte do Castelo**, supplied from the river islands. This is the real thing: the fruit arrives by boat in *rasas* (baskets) overnight and is traded in the small hours.
- **The herb and medicinal-plant stalls** — the *ervanárias*: bark, roots, resins, *garrafadas*, and the perfumes and love-charm oils (*banhos de cheiro*). This is the most distinctive corner of the market and the one worth the visit even if you buy nothing.
- **Praça do Relógio** — the clock imported from England, erected 1930, donated by intendente Antonio Faciola.
- **Solar da Beira** — neoclassical public building, converted to restaurant/events use in 1985; recently restored and handed back to the public by IPHAN ([IPHAN/gov.br](https://www.gov.br/iphan/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/solar-da-beira-que-compoe-o-complexo-do-ver-o-peso-e-entregue-a-populacao-de-belem-pa)).
- **Ladeira do Castelo** — Belém's first street, linking the Feira do Açaí to the Largo da Sé.
- **Boulevard Castilhos França** and **Praça Dom Pedro II**.

**Hours.** The fair runs from before dawn; the fish and açaí trade is essentially over by mid-morning. **UNVERIFIED — I could not source official opening hours for the complex.** The practical rule everyone gives is: go early. On a ship day arriving 08:00 and needing an hour's transfer, you arrive around 09:30–10:00, which is late for the fish and açaí but fine for the herb stalls, the iron market and the general fair.

**Safety and the honest read.** Ver-o-Peso is a working market in a city with a high homicide rate (see §7), and it is a well-known pickpocket and snatch environment. It is also, unambiguously, **the thing to see in Belém** — a 400-year-old Amazon river market, now physically rebuilt, on the waterfront next to the Cidade Velha. The sensible posture: go in daylight, go with the ship's tour or in a group, carry nothing you would mind losing, keep the phone away, do not wear jewellery or a watch, and do not wander into the streets behind the market. Under those conditions **it is safe enough and it is absolutely worthwhile.** Anyone who tells a passenger to skip Ver-o-Peso has misunderstood the port.

## 3. Theatro da Paz — the UNESCO inscription

**Your prior is right on the date and very nearly right on the substance — but the listing is not the theatre alone.**

### What exactly was inscribed

**"Amazonia Theatres" / "Teatros da Amazônia"** — a **serial cultural property with two component parts**, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List:

| Component | City |
|---|---|
| **Theatro da Paz + Praça da República** | Belém, Pará |
| **Teatro Amazonas + Largo de São Sebastião** | Manaus, Amazonas |

**The surrounding squares are part of the inscribed property**, not buffer zones — that is the point of the nomination, which is about the theatres as urban ensembles and symbols of civic ambition, not about two buildings.

- **Date of inscription: 27 July 2026** (decision taken in the early hours of Monday 27 July).
- **Session: 48th session of the World Heritage Committee, Busan, Republic of Korea.** ← **not** the 47th.
- **Criteria: (ii) and (iv).**
- **UNESCO reference: [whc.unesco.org/en/list/1774/](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1774/)**
- Brazil's **26th** World Heritage property, and the **first cultural World Heritage site in Brazil's North Region.**

UNESCO's own framing: the ensembles were built in Pará and Amazonas during the Amazonian "Golden Age," the economic peak of the **Rubber Cycle between 1879 and 1912**; "inspired by French and European architecture and decorated with motifs evoking the Amazon, these ensembles were designed as symbols of political and urban ambition."

Sources: [UNESCO WHC, Amazonia Theatres (list/1774)](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1774/); [UNESCO WHC new inscribed properties, 48COM](https://whc.unesco.org/en/newproperties/); [France 24, 27 July 2026](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260727-brazil-s-amazonia-theaters-earn-unesco-world-heritage-status); [Xinhua, 28 July 2026](https://english.news.cn/20260728/814737461bb54bcbb833439913724d7c/c.html); [UPI, 30 July 2026](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2026/07/30/latam-UNESCO-new-world-heritage-sites/2281785439065/); [CNN Brasil](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/internacional/teatros-da-amazonia-sao-reconhecidos-como-patrimonio-mundial-pela-unesco/).

**The nomination process:** IPHAN submitted the dossier to UNESCO in **January 2025**, in partnership with the Ministry of Culture, Itamaraty, both state governments and the municipalities of Manaus and Belém; ICOMOS conducted the technical evaluation ([Portal Amazônia](https://portalamazonia.com/cultura/unesco-patrimonia-teatro-theatro/); [Prefeitura de Manaus](https://www.manaus.am.gov.br/noticia/reconhecimento/teatro-amazonas-titulo-patrimonio-mundial-unesco/)).

**This is three days old as of writing.** Essentially no guidebook, port guide or cruise-line description reflects it. It is the single most current thing you can put in a Belém guide, and it changes how the theatre should be presented — from "handsome 19th-century opera house" to "World Heritage Site, inscribed 2026."

*Technical note: `whc.unesco.org` sits behind Cloudflare and refused both WebFetch and curl on 30 July 2026; the record details above come from UNESCO's indexed page content plus multiple independent wire reports that agree on date, session, criteria and components.*

### The building

- **Inaugurated 15 February 1878**, during the rubber boom, in Belém ([Theatro da Paz, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatro_da_Paz); [France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260727-brazil-s-amazonia-theaters-earn-unesco-world-heritage-status)). It is **18 years older than the Teatro Amazonas** (Manaus, 1896) and one of the oldest opera houses in Brazil.
- **Neoclassical**, with a Corinthian-columned portico, an Italianate horseshoe auditorium, Carrara marble, Venetian glass, and a ceiling painting; the *pt.wikipedia* chronology places it among the rubber-era civic buildings alongside the Palácio Antônio Lemos (1873) and the Mercado de Carnes (1867) ([pt.wikipedia, Complexo do Ver-o-Peso](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexo_do_Ver-o-Peso)).
- It stands on the **Praça da República** — now part of the World Heritage property.

## 5. The Amazon context — get the geography right

**Belém is not on the Amazon River.** This is the second error to correct, after the berth.

The correct description:

- Belém sits on the **right (east) bank of the Baía do Guajará**, with the **Rio Guamá** to its south — the city's two natural water boundaries ([Belém (Pará), pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel%C3%A9m_(Par%C3%A1)); [Baía do Guajará, pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C3%ADa_do_Guajar%C3%A1)).
- The **Baía do Guajará** is a sub-estuary formed at the confluence of the **Guamá, Acará and Moju** rivers, on the **right bank of the Rio Pará estuary** ([pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C3%ADa_do_Guajar%C3%A1); [Museu Goeldi, batimetria e sedimentologia da Baía de Guajará](https://repositorio.museu-goeldi.br/items/202c358d-959f-4715-acb6-d0578403c886)).
- Guajará drains into the **Baía do Marajó**, and thence to the Atlantic. It is strongly **tide-influenced** because of its proximity to the ocean.
- The **Rio Pará** is the southern arm of the Amazon's mouth complex — separated from the main Amazon channel by the **Ilha de Marajó**, an island the size of Switzerland. The Pará is fed principally by the **Tocantins** and the local rivers; it connects to the Amazon proper only via the **Estreitos de Breves**, the narrow channels around the west side of Marajó.

**So the honest formulation is:** *Belém stands at the mouth of the Amazon, but not on the Amazon. It is on Guajará Bay, in the estuary of the Pará River — the southern channel of the Amazon delta, separated from the main river by Marajó Island. The water is brown, fresh, tidal, and Amazonian; it just isn't the Amazon's main stem.*

The port is **~120 km from the Atlantic** by the navigable channel ([Porto de Belém, pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_de_Bel%C3%A9m)).

### River trips and Ilha do Combu

**Ilha do Combu** is the answer to "can I see the rainforest on a port day," and the answer is a qualified yes.

- Combu lies in the **Rio Guamá directly opposite the city** — **10–15 minutes by boat**, less than a kilometre from the Belém shoreline, yet dense forest, igarapés and silence ([Tempo.com](https://www.tempo.com/noticias/lazer/a-apenas-10-minutos-de-belem-conheca-a-ilha-que-e-o-paraiso-gastronomico-do-para.html); [O Liberal, "Ilha do Combu: veja como ir, quanto custa, onde fica e o que fazer"](https://www.oliberal.com/belem/ilha-do-combu-veja-como-ir-quanto-custa-onde-fica-e-o-que-fazer-1.498751)).
- Boats leave from the **Terminal Hidroviário Ruy Barata** (opposite Praça Princesa Isabel) or from the Condor district ([O Liberal](https://www.oliberal.com/belem/ilha-do-combu-veja-como-ir-quanto-custa-onde-fica-e-o-que-fazer-1.498751)).
- It is an **environmental protection area** of *ribeirinho* (river-dweller) households living from **açaí and cacao**. Riverside restaurants serve river fish and açaí; you can swim in the river.
- The famous stop is **Dona Nena's Casa do Chocolate — "Filha do Combu"** — bean-to-bar Amazonian chocolate made on the island, praised by chefs including Alex Atala and Tiago Castanho ([Esse Mundo é Nosso](https://www.essemundoenosso.com.br/ilha-do-combu-em-belem-para/)).
- The ship's own welcome programme for the January 2026 arrivals included river excursions to **Combu and Acará** ([A Província do Pará](https://aprovinciadopara.com.br/para-recebe-segundo-cruzeiro-neste-inicio-de-2026-movimentando-turismo-estadual/)).

**Açaí.** Belém is the world capital of açaí *as a savoury staple*, not a smoothie bowl. Here it is thick, unsweetened, near-purple-black, eaten with **tapioca granules (farinha de tapioca)** and **fried fish or shrimp**. It arrives at the Doca do Ver-o-Peso by boat before dawn from the river islands. Trying it as the locals do — not sweetened, not with granola — is the most authentic thing a passenger can do in ninety seconds.

**Real rainforest on a port day?** Combu is genuine várzea (floodplain) forest and genuine river life, 15 minutes from the city — that is real. What is **not** feasible on a single day: the Amazon main stem, Marajó, a lodge stay, or anything upriver. If a passenger wants the Amazon proper, this is not the port for it.

## 7. Safety, health, practicalities

### Money
**Brazilian real (R$/BRL).** Cards (Visa/Mastercard) are widely accepted in restaurants, the Estação das Docas, hotels and formal shops; **Pix**, Brazil's instant-payment system, is now near-universal among Brazilians but requires a Brazilian account and is not usable by visitors. **Carry small-denomination cash for Ver-o-Peso, the Feira do Açaí, taxis and the ceramics workshops in Icoaraci** — market vendors are cash-first. ATMs exist downtown (Banco do Brasil, Bradesco, Itaú, Caixa); use ones inside bank branches or the Estação das Docas, in daylight. Warn about DCC (dynamic currency conversion) — always pay in reais.

### Language
**Portuguese.** English is limited even in tourism-facing businesses; Spanish gets you somewhat further but is not Portuguese. The January 2026 arrivals were met with **trilingual pocket maps** covering 32 attractions with QR codes to the Visit Pará platform ([A Província do Pará](https://aprovinciadopara.com.br/para-recebe-segundo-cruzeiro-neste-inicio-de-2026-movimentando-turismo-estadual/)) — an indication both that the city is making an effort and that the language gap is real. A translation app is genuinely useful here.

### Tipping
Restaurants add a **10% service charge (*taxa de serviço*)** to the bill; it is customary but technically optional. Nothing further is expected. Taxis: round up. Guides and drivers on ship excursions: at the passenger's discretion, and USD is accepted from cruise passengers though reais are better.

### Taxis and rideshare
**Uber and 99 both operate in Belém** and are the recommended way to move around — app-based transport gives you a tracked route, a fixed price and no cash handling. The safety framing that local guidance uses is explicitly built around "the Cidade Velha historic core, Ver-o-Peso waterfront, and **app-based transport after dark**" ([Brazil Safe Travel, Belém 2026 guide](https://brazilsafetravel.com/blog/is-belem-brazil-safe)). Street-hailed taxis are legal but negotiate or insist on the meter.

**From the Icoaraci terminal**, expect informal taxi and van touts. The ship's shuttle, if offered, is the safer choice for the 18–20 km run.

### Crime — the honest picture
- **US State Department: Brazil is Level 2, "Exercise Increased Caution," as of 10 June 2026**, with a kidnapping risk indicator ([travel.state.gov, Brazil](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/brazil-travel-advisory.html); [US Embassy Brazil](https://br.usembassy.gov/travel-advisory-brazil/)). Note that specific *states* carry Level 4 "Do Not Travel" advisories for border and satellite-city areas — **Pará is not among them**, but check the current text before travel.
- **Belém's homicide rate is reported at 71.4 per 100,000** ([Brazil Safe Travel](https://brazilsafetravel.com/blog/is-belem-brazil-safe)) — high by any standard. **But that violence is overwhelmingly concentrated in peripheral neighbourhoods and organised-crime disputes, not in the tourist core.** For visitors, the dominant threat is **petty theft**: pickpocketing, phone snatching, bag slashing.
- **Where to be careful:** Ver-o-Peso and its immediate surroundings (pickpockets); the streets *behind* the market and the Comércio district once the working day ends; anywhere after dark on foot; the Icoaraci waterfront away from the terminal.
- **Where it is fine:** Estação das Docas, the Feliz Lusitânia complex, Praça da República and the Theatro da Paz, Mangal das Garças, the Goeldi, and Combu — all in daylight.
- **Standard discipline:** no visible jewellery or watches, phone out of sight except when actually using it, one card and modest cash, no backpack worn on the back in crowds, and do not resist a robbery.
- COP30 left **100% LED lighting on the main corridors and a high-resolution camera network tied to a security operations centre** ([DOL](https://dol.com.br/noticias/para/941607/os-legados-definitivos-que-a-cop30-deixou-para-belem)), and the municipal security agency ran a reception operation for the January 2026 cruise arrivals ([SEGBEL](https://seguranca.belem.pa.gov.br/navio-de-cruzeiro-holandes-atraca-em-icoaraci-e-turistas-sao-recebidos-com-acoes-da-gestao-municipal/)). The tourist core in 2026 is better lit and better policed than it was in 2023.

### Yellow fever
- **Yellow fever vaccination is NOT required for entry to Brazil** for arrivals from most countries. It is **strongly recommended** for Pará.
- The **CDC recommends the yellow fever vaccine for all travellers to Pará state**; Brazil's Ministry of Health treats Pará as an **area with vaccination recommendation** ([Ministério da Saúde, Febre Amarela 2026](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/campanhas-da-saude/2026/febre-amarela); [SESPA — Secretaria de Estado de Saúde do Pará](http://www.saude.pa.gov.br/secretaria-de-saude-reforca-a-importancia-da-vacinacao-para-prevencao-de-doencas/)).
- Brazil has adopted the WHO **single-dose-for-life** schedule; one dose at any point in life confers protection. Recommended for ages 9 months to 59 years; take it **at least 10 days before entering a risk area** ([Ministério da Saúde](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/campanhas-da-saude/2026/febre-amarela); [Nina Saúde, 2026](https://ninasaude.com.br/vacinacao-contra-febre-amarela-no-brasil-em-2026)).
- **Critical for this itinerary:** **French Guiana requires proof of yellow fever vaccination for entry.** If the voyage calls at both Îles du Salut and Belém, passengers need the certificate anyway — and some downstream countries require it of travellers *arriving from* Brazil or French Guiana. **Get the vaccine and carry the yellow card.**

### Malaria, dengue, other
- **Malaria:** risk exists in the Amazon basin but is **low in urban Belém**. Prophylaxis is not normally advised for a city port day; confirm with a travel clinic if the itinerary includes river excursions.
- **Dengue, chikungunya, Zika, Oropouche:** all circulate in Pará, transmitted by **day-biting** *Aedes* mosquitoes — which is precisely when passengers are ashore. **Repellent (DEET or icaridin) applied in the morning is the single most useful health measure of the day**, more so than any vaccine.
- **Water:** do not drink the tap water. Bottled water is cheap and everywhere. Ice in established restaurants and at Estação das Docas is made from treated water and is normally fine; ice from street vendors is not. Açaí is a genuine, if small, risk vector for **Chagas disease** via contaminated fruit pulp — buy it from established vendors and restaurants, not from a street cart.

### Heat and humidity
Equatorial and unrelenting: highs around 31–33 °C, lows around 23 °C, relative humidity 79–90%, every month of the year (see §8). There is **no cool season**. The heat index frequently exceeds 40 °C. Light long sleeves beat sunscreen-only; a hat, sunglasses, and more water than seems necessary. Plan the outdoor walking for the morning and be indoors — Estação das Docas, the theatre, a museum — in the early afternoon, which is also when the rain arrives.

### Visa — the recent change matters
**Brazil reinstated visa requirements for US, Canadian and Australian citizens on 10 April 2025**, ending the visa waiver that had been in place since 2019. It is a **reciprocity** measure ([Fragomen, "Brazil: Visa Requirement Reinstated for Nationals of Australia, Canada and the United States"](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/brazil-visa-requirement-reinstated-for-nationals-of-australia-canada-and-the-united-states.html)).

**Current state, verified as of 30 July 2026:**

- **The requirement remains in force in 2026.** US, Canadian and Australian passport holders need an **e-Visa** for tourism, business, transit or short visits.
- **Applied entirely online** via the official portal **brazil.vfsevisa.com**; no embassy visit; typical processing **48–72 hours**.
- **Fee: US$80.90.**
- **Validity: 10 years multiple-entry for US citizens; 5 years multiple-entry for Canadians and Australians.** Maximum 90 days per stay.
- **It explicitly applies to arrival by vessel**, not just by air — cruise passengers must hold it before boarding a Brazil-bound ship.
- Holders of valid Brazilian residence permits or existing physical visas are unaffected.

([OPSGROUP, "Olá Brazil: New Entry Rules"](https://ops.group/blog/ola-brazil-new-entry-rules-for-us-canada-and-australian-citizens/); [Wego, Brazil e-Visa 2026](https://blog.wego.com/brazil-evisa-requirements-us-canada-australia-2026/); [VisaHQ, 20 Dec 2025](https://www.visahq.com/news/2025-12-20/br/brazil-confirms-2026-e-visa-vat-and-tourist-tax-shake-up-for-us-canada-mexico-and-20-other-markets/))

**This is the single most consequential practicality for a mostly-North-American world cruise, and any guide written before April 2025 says the opposite.** UK, EU and most other nationalities remain visa-exempt for short stays. **Re-verify before a 2028 sailing** — this policy has flipped twice in a decade, and VisaHQ reports further 2026 changes around VAT and a tourist tax under discussion.

### Population, for orientation
**Belém: 1,303,403** (IBGE **Censo 2022**) — Pará's largest city, second in the North Region, twelfth in Brazil; density 1,230.25/km²; 99.14% urban; **down 6.5% on the previous decade**. The 2025 estimate is **1,397,315**. ([IBGE Cidades — Belém (PA)](https://www.ibge.gov.br/cidades-e-estados/pa/belem.html); [pt.wikipedia, Belém (Pará)](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel%C3%A9m_(Par%C3%A1)); [FAPESPA, Estimativas Populacionais 2024](https://www.fapespa.pa.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Nota-Informativa-02_2024-Estimativas-Populacionais-2024.pdf)). The Região Metropolitana de Belém holds roughly a third of Pará's population.

### What these numbers actually mean

- **3,308 mm of rain a year across 224 rain days.** That is rain on **61% of all days**. Belém is one of the wettest large cities on Earth.
- **The "almost daily afternoon downpour" is real and it is quantified above.** March: rain on **26 days out of 31**. Locals genuinely set appointments by it — *"depois da chuva"*, after the rain. The classic pattern is a hot, bright, oppressive morning; cloud building from late morning; a violent convective downpour in the early-to-mid afternoon, often 30–90 minutes; then a cooler, cleaner, still-humid evening.
- **There is no dry season, only a less-wet one.** The driest month, September, still gets **120 mm over 12 days**.
- **Temperature is essentially flat all year** — mean 26.1–27.4 °C, a 1.3 °C annual range. Mean maxima 31–33 °C. What varies is humidity and cloud, not heat.
- **Humidity 79–90%.** March, at 89.7%, is the most oppressive month.
- **Sunshine bottoms out in February (104.7 h — about 3.7 h/day).**

### What to expect on a February–April world-cruise call

**This is the worst window of the year for weather, and there is no way to dress it up.**

| | Feb | Mar | Apr |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rainfall | 437.8 mm | **506.3 mm** (wettest month) | 465.5 mm |
| Rain days | 25 | **26** | 25 |
| Humidity | 89.5% | **89.7%** (highest) | 88.9% |

**Plan on rain.** A passenger ashore for eight hours in March has roughly an 84% chance of being rained on. The right advice is not "hope it's dry" — it is:

- Do the outdoor walking (Ver-o-Peso, Cidade Velha, Forte do Presépio) **first thing**, before the cloud builds.
- Have an indoor fallback for the early afternoon: **Estação das Docas, the Theatro da Paz, Casa das Onze Janelas, the Goeldi.**
- Carry a **light rain jacket or a cheap poncho**, not an umbrella that will invert. Waterproof the phone and camera.
- Wear shoes that tolerate being soaked, and expect standing water — though COP30's closed-gallery macrodrainage reportedly improved flow capacity by ~40% ([DOL](https://dol.com.br/noticias/para/941607/os-legados-definitivos-que-a-cop30-deixou-para-belem)), so flash flooding downtown should be less bad than in older accounts.
- The rain is warm. It is not a disaster; it is the city's daily rhythm. Passengers who accept that have a better day than passengers who fight it.

**Tender risk:** heavy convective squalls in Guajará Bay can bring short bursts of strong wind. The bay is sheltered by comparison with an open-ocean anchorage, so the tender operation is less weather-exposed than at Îles du Salut — but the tide constraint that pushes ships away from Outeiro applies to the tender run too, and arrival/departure times are tide-set.

## 9. The honest read — one port day in Belém

### The shape of the day
Assume: anchor off Icoaraci ~07:00–08:00, tender ashore, all aboard ~16:00–17:00. **Subtract 2 hours for the round-trip transfer to the centre, plus 30–60 minutes for tender queues at both ends. You have roughly 4–5 usable hours downtown.** That is the governing constraint on everything below.

### What to do — the strong recommendation

**One realistic downtown itinerary, in this order:**

1. **Ver-o-Peso** (arrive as early as the tender allows) — the Mercado de Ferro, the fish market, the herb and medicinal-plant stalls, the Doca. **60–90 minutes.** This is the reason to come to Belém.
2. **Walk five minutes to the Forte do Presépio and Casa das Onze Janelas** — the 1616 foundation point, the bay view, and the Feliz Lusitânia complex. **45–60 minutes.**
3. **Theatro da Paz** — now a World Heritage Site, ~15 minutes by taxi from the old city to Praça da República. Hourly 45-minute tours, R$ 10, **40-person cap, closed Mondays**. **1 hour** including transfer. Book or go with the ship if the call is on a busy day.
4. **Lunch at Estação das Docas** — air-conditioned, safe, on the water, and the easiest place to eat Amazonian food (tacacá, maniçoba, pato no tucupi, filhote) without gambling on a street stall. **60–90 minutes**, and it covers the afternoon downpour.

**The alternative shape** — for someone who would rather have the river than the city: **Ilha do Combu**, 10–15 minutes by boat from the Terminal Hidroviário Ruy Barata. Real várzea forest, ribeirinho life, cacao and açaí, Dona Nena's chocolate. Half a day, comfortably, and it is a genuinely different experience from a city walk. **This is the best "Amazon" answer available on a port day.**

**A third shape, for the transfer-averse:** stay in **Icoaraci**. It is Belém's ceramics district — *cerâmica marajoara* workshops, the riverside orla, boats to Cotijuba from the same terminal you landed at. Modest, but real, and it costs you no bus time.

### What is not feasible
- **The Amazon River proper.** Belém is on the Pará, not the Amazon; the main stem is beyond Marajó. No day trip reaches it.
- **Marajó Island.** Roughly 3 hours by boat each way. Not on a port day.
- **A jungle lodge, an overnight, or anything upriver.**
- **The Goeldi park on a Monday or Tuesday** — closed.
- **The Theatro da Paz on a Monday** — closed.
- **Doing Ver-o-Peso *and* the Theatro *and* the Goeldi *and* Combu.** Pick two, maybe three. The transfer eats the day.
- **Wandering independently after dark.** The all-aboard time makes this moot, which is just as well.
- **Assuming you can walk from the ship to anything.** You cannot. You are 20 km out.

### The one-line verdict
**Belém is a genuinely great port that is badly served by its logistics.** The city has a 400-year-old Amazon market, a brand-new World Heritage opera house, a colonial core, and rainforest fifteen minutes from the quay — and it makes you spend two hours on a bus to reach any of it. Take the ship's excursion or a pre-booked private guide, go early, accept the rain, and eat the açaí the way they eat it.

([IPHAN](http://portal.iphan.gov.br/pagina/detalhes/828); [pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexo_do_Ver-o-Peso); [ANPUH](https://anpuh.org.br/uploads/anais-simposios/pdf/2019-01/1548856591_2d9d882eb730bb354f1f501b933f066e.pdf))

The municipality's own working figure is **1,193 permissionários**, with **~2,400 people** working the complex daily once helpers and informal vendors are counted. Other municipal counts range 469–1,163 depending on scope. ([Seurb/Prefeitura de Belém](https://seurb.belem.pa.gov.br/cop-30-mais-168-boxes-sao-revitalizados-na-feira-tradicional-do-ver-o-peso/))

4. **"Belém is on the Amazon River."** Not literally. Belém is on **Guajará Bay**, on the right bank of the **Rio Pará estuary** — the southern arm of the Amazon delta, separated from the main channel by **Marajó Island**. Say "at the mouth of the Amazon," not "on the Amazon." ([Baía do Guajará, pt.wikipedia](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C3%ADa_do_Guajar%C3%A1); [Museu Goeldi bathymetry study](https://repositorio.museu-goeldi.br/items/202c358d-959f-4715-acb6-d0578403c886))

5. **"Theatro da Paz was listed by UNESCO on its own."** No — it is one of **two components** of the serial property **"Amazonia Theatres,"** with the Teatro Amazonas in Manaus, **and the property includes both squares** (Praça da República in Belém, Largo de São Sebastião in Manaus). Inscribed **27 July 2026**, **48th session**, Busan, **criteria (ii)(iv)**. Sources saying "47th session" are wrong. ([whc.unesco.org/en/list/1774](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1774/))

6. **"US/Canadian/Australian citizens don't need a visa for Brazil."** **Stale — reversed on 10 April 2025.** An **e-Visa is required**, US$80.90, applied for at brazil.vfsevisa.com, 48–72 hours, valid 10 years (US) / 5 years (CA, AU), and it **applies to arrival by ship**. ([Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/brazil-visa-requirement-reinstated-for-nationals-of-australia-canada-and-the-united-states.html); [OPSGROUP](https://ops.group/blog/ola-brazil-new-entry-rules-for-us-canada-and-australian-citizens/))

7. **"Ver-o-Peso is run-down / the market is as it was."** **Stale.** The whole complex was rebuilt for COP30: fish market reopened September 2025, main fair November 2025, meat market January 2026, plus 288+ new boxes. Any pre-2025 description is obsolete. ([Seurb](https://seurb.belem.pa.gov.br/cop-30-mais-168-boxes-sao-revitalizados-na-feira-tradicional-do-ver-o-peso/); [Diário do Pará](https://diariodopara.com.br/belem/mercado-de-peixe-do-ver-o-peso-e-reaberto-com-nova-estrutura-e-foco-na-cop30/))

8. **"The terminal is run by Companhia Docas do Pará."** No. The **Icoaraci tourist water terminal is built and operated by CPH — Companhia de Portos e Hidrovias do Estado do Pará**, a *state* company. **CDP (Companhia Docas do Pará)** is the *federal* authority for the Porto de Belém and Vila do Conde. Different bodies; do not conflate them. ([O Liberal](https://www.oliberal.com/para/icoaraci-ganha-novo-terminal-hidroviario-turistico-as-vesperas-do-cirio-e-reforca-conexao-com-cotiju-1.1031266))

9. **Icoaraci terminal size — sources disagree.** O Liberal and ALEPA give **650 m² built + 350 m² operational**; Pará state releases give **1,000 m² built**. The 1,000 m² figure is plausibly built + operational combined. **Use "about 650 m² of building on a 1,000 m² footprint, with five floating docks"** and note the discrepancy.

10. **"Cruise ships now use the new Outeiro terminal."** Not in practice. Outeiro was rebuilt for COP30 with a ~710 m pier at R$ 233 m and *can* take large ships — but **CPH says low tide at typical cruise arrival/departure times makes Outeiro manoeuvres difficult, "tornando Icoaraci a opção mais viável,"** and schedule trackers report Outeiro underutilised with most ships still anchoring in Guajará Bay. ([Diário do Pará, 23 Oct 2025](https://diariodopara.com.br/belem/terminal-portuario-de-outeiro-quase-pronto-para-receber-transatlanticos/); [Portal World Cruises](https://portalworldcruises.com/escalas/belem/))

11. **"Yellow fever vaccination is required to enter Brazil."** It is **recommended for Pará, not required for entry to Brazil**. It **is required for French Guiana** — so passengers on this itinerary need it regardless. Do not state the Brazilian requirement as mandatory. ([Ministério da Saúde](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/campanhas-da-saude/2026/febre-amarela))

12. **Live sourcing hazard:** **`agenciapara.com.br`, the Pará state government news agency, is suspended until the end of the 2026 elections under Brazilian electoral law** (verified 30 July 2026 — it serves only an electoral notice). Several official Pará facts here are therefore quoted via search indexing and mirror outlets rather than fetched from the primary site. `cdp.com.br` returned HTTP 503, and `belemtur.belem.pa.gov.br` and `seurb.belem.pa.gov.br` returned 503 on the same day. **Re-verify from primary sources once these come back up.**
