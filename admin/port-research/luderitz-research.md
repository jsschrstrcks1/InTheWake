## 1. The berth — and a number that decides the day

**Lüderitz is a tender call for a ship the size of Volendam, and the arithmetic
is unusually clean.**

### What the port actually has

Namport's own description of the Port of Lüderitz:

- **One main berth, 500 m long, with a depth of 8.75 m**
- 25 hectares of land, at **Robert Harbour**
- **"It is considered a shallow port, and it is founded on bedrock which is not
  financially viable to dredge."**
- Handles mostly dry bulk from southern Namibia and South Africa's Northern
  Cape, and serves the fishing industry

([Namport, Port of Lüderitz](https://www.namport.com.na/ports/welcome-to-the-port-of-luderitz/523/))

### Why that means tender

**Volendam draws 8.1 m** (237.0 m LOA, 32.3 m beam, 60,906 GT, built 1999,
Rotterdam/R-class — [MS Volendam, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_Volendam)).

Against a berth dredged to 8.75 m on bedrock, that is **0.65 m of under-keel
clearance** before you account for tide, swell or squat. Most port authorities
want at least 10% of draft, which would be 0.81 m. The number does not work
with any margin.

**The published 2026 Lüderitz season corroborates it.** Namport scheduled
**13 calls at Lüderitz** and 20 at Walvis Bay for the 2026 passenger liner
season — 33 in total. The Lüderitz list:

| Date (2026) | Ship | Draft |
|---|---|---|
| 29 Jan | AIDAstella | 7.3 m |
| 10 Feb | Europa | ~6.3 m |
| 20 Feb | Azamara Journey | ~6.0 m |
| 4 Mar | AIDAdiva | 7.3 m |
| 13 Mar | *Hamburg* | ~5.0 m |
| 15 Mar | AIDAstella | 7.3 m |
| 28 Mar | Le Bellot | ~4.7 m |
| 29 Mar | SH Diana | ~4.8 m |
| 2 Apr | Sirena | ~6.0 m |
| 6 May | Silver Dawn | ~6.3 m |
| 13 May | Azamara Onward | ~6.0 m |
| 2 Jun | Regatta | ~6.0 m |
| 13 Jul | Zhao Shang Yi Dun | — |

([Aitsa, "Thirty-three passenger vessels scheduled to visit Namibian Ports"](https://www.aitsafrika.com/tourimus/thirty-three-passenger-vessels-scheduled-to-visit-namibian-ports-nmh004889-3014-5081);
drafts from each ship's own record — [AIDAstella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDAstella),
[AIDAdiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDAdiva))

**Not one ship in the published season draws more than 7.3 m.** AIDAstella is
253 m long and 71,304 GT — *larger* than Volendam in every dimension except the
one that matters here — and she still draws 0.8 m less water.

That is the finding: it is not size that keeps Volendam off the quay, it is
draft, and the fleet that does call at Lüderitz is systematically shallower.

### Where the tender lands

Ships **anchor in Robert Harbour and tender to the Lüderitz Waterfront jetty**,
which is on the edge of the town centre — the little town is walkable from the
landing. ([Whatsinport, Lüderitz](https://www.whatsinport.com/Luderitz.htm);
corroborated by the v0.1.17 pack's own description, and consistent with the
draft analysis above.)

*Note: `cruisemapper.com` says of Lüderitz that the "harbour features a shallow
rock bottom, which makes it unusable for modern vessels" — the same physical
fact Namport states, phrased more bluntly.
([CruiseMapper, Lüderitz](https://www.cruisemapper.com/ports/luderitz-port-1056))*

### What could change by 2028

Namport has a **N$4-billion Lüderitz expansion** on the books, aimed at making
the port an oil-and-gas supply base for the Orange Basin. Phase 3 would remove
old jetties, reclaim 3 ha and add **370 m of berth** — but at quay-wall depths
of **6, 7 and 7 m**, which is *shallower*, not deeper. A separate **Angra Point**
scheme would give **14–16 m** and is the only proposal that would change the
cruise picture. Timeline as reported: EPC tender mid-2025, first section of new
quay wall commissioned mid-2027, final section end-2028 — and Namport has since
paused southern-harbour upgrades to "clarify scope" and cancelled a Lüderitz
supply-base tender days after launching it.
([Mining & Energy Namibia](https://miningandenergy.com.na/namport-plans-n4-billion-luderitz-port-expansion-to-create-oil-and-gas-supply-base/);
[World Oil, 24 Oct 2025](https://www.worldoil.com/news/2025/10/24/orange-basin-at-a-crossroads-namibia-faces-key-decisions-ahead-of-2026-fid/))

**Read for 2028: still a tender call.** Nothing scheduled adds depth at Robert
Harbour, and Angra Point is a proposal rather than a project with a date.

## 2. Kolmanskop — and the closing time nobody mentions

**This is the reason most passengers come, and it shuts at lunchtime.**

Kolmanskop is a diamond-boom town in the Namib, **10 km inland from Lüderitz**,
abandoned as the field played out and now being taken back by the dune field.
It sits **inside the Tsau ǁKhaeb (Sperrgebiet) National Park and the restricted
diamond area**, and entry requires a valid permit.
([Kolmanskop, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmanskop);
[Kolmanskuppe, Tours & Prices](https://kolmanskuppe.com/tours-prices/))

### Hours, tours and prices — from the operator, 2026/2027

- **Opening hours: Monday–Friday 08:00–15:00; Saturday, Sunday and public
  holidays 08:00–13:00**
- **Guided tours: Monday–Saturday at 09:30 and 11:00; Sunday and public holidays
  at 10:00.** Duration 45–60 minutes, in English and German
- **Only Ghost Town Tours guides may conduct guided tours through Kolmanskop**
- **Standard permit: N$230 adult, N$90 child (6–14)**
- **Photography permit: N$480 per person**, sunrise-to-sunset access
- A "day pass" giving access **06:00–19:00** is referenced but not separately
  priced on the operator's page

([Kolmanskuppe, Tours & Prices](https://kolmanskuppe.com/tours-prices/))

**This is the single most important practical fact on the page.** A cruise call
that tenders its first boat at 08:00 has to move immediately to make the 09:30
tour, and the 11:00 tour is the last one on any day of the week. On a Saturday
or Sunday the site itself closes at 13:00. Kolmanskop is a **morning
commitment**, not an afternoon option, and not the "half-day whenever you like"
that general descriptions imply.

*See CORRECTIONS: the v0.1.17 pack currently describes Kolmanskop as "a half-day
with a local operator," which is true about duration and misleading about
timing.*

## 3. Shark Island — read this before writing a word about it

Shark Island is a peninsula at the edge of the town, joined to it by a
causeway. Between **March 1905 and April 1907** it was a German concentration
camp during the **Herero and Nama genocide of 1904–08**. Estimates of the dead
there run from **1,032 to 3,000** Herero and Nama men, women and children.
([Shark Island concentration camp, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_Island_concentration_camp))

**Two things about the site as it stands are hard to write and should not be
softened.**

1. **It is a campsite.** The peninsula is operated as **Shark Island Campsite**
   by Namibia Wildlife Resorts, with pitches, ablutions and rates published in
   the ordinary way. People camp on it.
   ([NWR, Shark Island](https://www.nwrnamibia.com/shark-island.htm))
2. **The dominant memorial on the site commemorates the perpetrators' dead.**
   The cluster of monuments behind the gate is centred on a semicircular wall
   carrying white plaques that name members of the German **Schutztruppe** who
   died at Lüderitz. ([Dark-Tourism, Shark Island](https://www.dark-tourism.com/index.php/namibia/15-countries/individual-chapters/1475-sharkisland);
   [Atlas Obscura, Shark Island](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/shark-island))

### The reparations position, as of 2026

- **28 May 2021:** Germany formally recognised the killings as genocide after
  five years of negotiation, and agreed **€1.1 billion in ex gratia development
  aid** while **rejecting legal responsibility**.
- **The Joint Declaration was negotiated between the two governments only.**
  Ovaherero and Nama representatives were not party to it. The Ovaherero
  Traditional Authority and the Nama Traditional Leaders Association filed for
  judicial review in the Namibian High Court in early 2023, seeking to have the
  declaration held unlawful under Namibia's constitution.
- **As of October 2025, Amnesty International's position is that Germany "has
  failed to engage in meaningful consultations with these communities or provide
  reparations."**
- **Namibia now marks 28 May as Genocide Remembrance Day**, first observed in
  2025 — the date the camps were closed in 1907 after international criticism.

([Namibian genocide reparations, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibian_genocide_reparations);
[Amnesty International, October 2025](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/10/namibia-germany-must-deliver-reparatory-justice-for-its-brutal-colonial-past/);
[The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/germanys-genocide-in-namibia-deal-between-the-two-governments-falls-short-of-delivering-justice-246719);
[Human Rights Research Center](https://www.humanrightsresearch.org/post/germany-s-forgotten-genocide-namibia-marks-genocide-remembrance-day))

**Writing guidance.** This is not a "dark tourism curiosity" and it is not
settled history. It is an open grievance with active litigation and a
government-to-government agreement that the affected communities reject. The
page should state what happened, state plainly that the site is now a campsite
and that the memorial there names the German dead, and stop — without
editorialising and without smoothing.

## 4. The rest of the town

Everything below is **within walking distance of the tender jetty** unless
stated. Lüderitz is small: **population 16,125 (2023)**.
([Lüderitz, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCderitz))

### Felsenkirche (the Rock Church)
The 1911–12 Evangelical Lutheran church on Diamond Hill above the town, and the
building on every photograph of Lüderitz. **No entrance fee; donations
requested for upkeep.** ([Tripadvisor, Felsenkirche](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g316112-d3227668-Reviews-or60-Felsenkirche-Luderitz_Karas_Region.html))

### Goerke Haus
The 1910 diamond-boom villa of Lieutenant Hans Goerke, on Diamond Hill beside
the Felsenkirche; a national monument, later the magistrate's residence.
Published visiting hours are **14:00–16:00 Monday–Friday and 16:00–17:00
Saturday–Sunday**. ([Namibia Accommodation, Magistrate's Residence](https://www.namibia-accommodation.com/listing/magistrates-residence-luderitz-goerke-house);
[Trip.com](https://www.trip.com/travel-guide/attraction/luderitz/goerke-haus-58292586/))

**Note the collision:** Kolmanskop's last guided tour is at 11:00 and the site
closes at 13:00 or 15:00; Goerke Haus does not open until 14:00. On a single
port day you can comfortably do one or the other, and the natural order is
Kolmanskop first, town after.

### Diaz Point and the padrão
A replica stone cross on the headland at the mouth of the bay, marking where
**Bartolomeu Dias** raised a *padrão* — the **padrão de São Tiago**. Dias reached
the bay, which he named **Angra Pequena**, in **1487** on the outbound voyage,
and erected the pillar on the **return leg in July 1488**. **Sources disagree on
the exact day: 24 July and 25 July are both given**, the latter argued from St
James's Day. ([Artefacts.co.za, Bartolomeu Dias Cross replica](https://www.artefacts.co.za/main/Buildings/bldgframes.php?bldgid=11407);
["A History of the Padrão de São Tiago at Lüderitz"](https://www.academia.edu/105706961/A_HISTORY_OF_THE_PADR%C3%83O_DE_S%C3%83O_TIAGO_AT_L%C3%9CDERITZ))
There is a **Cape fur seal colony** at the point.

### Halifax Island and the penguins
An island off the bay holding **the third most important African penguin
breeding site in Namibia**. Boat trips run from the **Lüderitz Waterfront,
departing daily at 08:00 and returning by about 10:15**, via Shark Island and
Diaz Point. Two operators: **Zeepaard Boat Tours** and **Penguin Catamaran
Tours**. Dolphins, Cape fur seals and seabirds are usual; whales occasional.
([Expert Africa, Halifax Island boat trip](https://www.expertafrica.com/namibia/excursions/halifax-island-boat-trip);
[Penguin Catamaran Tours](https://penguincatamarantours.wordpress.com/))

**Second collision, and it is the real planning decision of the day:** the
penguin boat departs at 08:00 and the last Kolmanskop tour is at 11:00. Whether
they combine depends entirely on the ship's tender start, and the honest answer
is that they often will not.

### Agate Beach
A long beach north of town, named for the agates found along it; one of only two
places in Tsau ǁKhaeb that can be visited without a tour operator and special
permit, the other being Kolmanskop itself.
([SafariBookings, Tsau ǁKhaeb](https://www.safaribookings.com/tsau-khaeb))

### Bogenfels
A **55 m sea arch** down the coast inside the Sperrgebiet. **Guided day tour with
a licensed operator and a permit only.** It is a full day from Lüderitz and is
**not feasible on a port call.**

### The Lüderitz Speed Challenge
A purpose-cut canal near Diaz Point — **10 m wide, 800 m long including run-in
and run-out, with a WSSRC-approved 500 m course** — where the offshore wind is
funnelled between slopes and accelerated across a plain to the sea. Held
annually since 2007 and observed by the World Sailing Speed Record Council.
**Antoine Albeau's 53.49 knots** is the windsurfing world record; **Jenna
Gibson's 48.03 knots** the women's, both at the 2024 edition. The official course
runs **3–30 November**.
([Lüderitz Speed Challenge, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCderitz_Speed_Challenge);
[Severne, LSC wiki](https://www.severnesails.com/speed/);
[Surf Magazin](https://www.surf-magazin.de/en/windsurfing/scene-and-events/speed-surfing-how-the-canal-in-luederitz-is-prepared-for-the-speed-challenge/))

This is not a passenger activity — it is the most vivid available proof of what
the wind does here, which *is* a passenger concern.


### The first diamond — name the man who found it

On **14 April 1908**, at **Grasplatz** on the Aus–Lüderitz railway line, a worker
named **Zacharias Lewala** picked up several stones he suspected were diamonds.
He had worked the diamond fields at **Kimberley** and could recognise rough by
sight, which is exactly why he stopped to look. He handed one to his German
supervisor, **August Stauch**, who sent it to Swakopmund for testing and applied
for the prospecting licence. Stauch became rich on the rush that followed and
built Kolmanskop.
([Zacharias Lewala, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zacharias_Lewala);
[August Stauch, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Stauch);
[Grasplatz, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasplatz))

**Note the location:** the find was at Grasplatz, on the railway — *not* at
Kolmanskop, which the rush then built. Descriptions that place the discovery at
Kolmanskop have the sequence backwards.

## 5. The name

**The town is still officially Lüderitz.** In **August 2013** the *constituency*
was renamed **ǃNamiǂNûs**, President Hifikepunye Pohamba describing it as "the
original name of the area." Debate followed about whether the town itself was
covered; the settled reading is that **the constituency was renamed and the town
was not**. ([ǃNamiǂNûs Constituency, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C7%83Nami%C7%82N%C3%BBs_Constituency);
[CBC, As It Happens](https://www.cbc.ca/radio/asithappens/as-it-happens-friday-edition-1.2975615/namibian-town-to-change-name-from-l%C3%BCderitz-to-nami-n%C3%BCs-1.2975882);
[American Name Society](https://www.americannamesociety.org/luderitz-namibia-debates-name-change/))

Adolf Lüderitz, the Bremen merchant the town is named for, bought the bay in
1883 in the transaction that began German South West Africa. Given §3, the name
is not a neutral piece of trivia and the page should say what it is.

## 6. Practicalities

### Visa — changed on 1 April 2025
**Namibia ended visa-free entry for a long list of previously exempt
nationalities on 1 April 2025.** Affected travellers need a visa, obtainable on
arrival at designated ports of entry at **N$1,600** (roughly US$83–90), or in
advance as an **eVisa**, which the Ministry of Home Affairs, Immigration, Safety
and Security has made the default route through 2025–26 at roughly **US$70–95**
single entry. Children under 6 are exempt.
([Mission of Namibia, "New VISA Requirements 1 April 2025"](https://missionofnamibia.ch/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=31&Itemid=376);
[Inside Namibia, eVisa 2026 guide](https://inside-namibia.travel/insights/namibia-eta-visa-2026-guide);
[Africa Unpacked, Namibia visa 2026](https://www.africaunpacked.com/namibia/visa))

**Critical caveat, and it must be written as a caveat:** the published
visa-on-arrival ports of entry are **airports and land border posts — Hosea
Kutako, Walvis Bay International, Oshikango, Trans-Kalahari, Oranjemund,
Noordoewer, Katima Mulilo, Impalila Island, Ngoma and Mohembo.** **No seaport
appears on that list**, which is precisely the sort of gap that matters to a
cruise passenger arriving by tender. **UNVERIFIED:** whether cruise arrivals are
handled under a ship's-agent collective clearance, whether the eVisa is required
in advance for a cruise call, and whether US, Canadian and Australian passport
holders are on the affected list at all — the published lists have changed
repeatedly and none I could reach enumerates them unambiguously. **Put this to
the line in writing, and check
[eservices.mhaiss.gov.na](https://eservices.mhaiss.gov.na) before sailing.**

### Money
**Namibian dollar (NAD), pegged one-for-one to the South African rand, and the
rand is also legal tender in Namibia.** The reverse is not true — Namibian
dollars are not accepted in South Africa. Namibia is a straightforward card
economy in town.
([Namibian dollar, en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibian_dollar);
[MEFT, Currency and Getting Around](https://www.meft.gov.na/visit-namibia/currency-and-getting-around/101/))

### Language
English is Namibia's sole official language. German and Afrikaans are widely
spoken in Lüderitz specifically, and Kolmanskop's guided tours are offered in
**English and German**. ([Kolmanskuppe](https://kolmanskuppe.com/tours-prices/))

## 7. Climate — cold, dry, and extremely windy

Lüderitz is **BWk — cold desert** — under Köppen, which is an unusual thing to
find at 26° S and is entirely the doing of the **Benguela Current**, a cold
northward flow that chills the air over the water, suppresses rain and generates
persistent morning fog.

| Month | High °C | Low °C | Rain mm | Wind km/h | Cloud | Sea °C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | 23 | 16 | 0 | **24** | 9% | 17 |
| Feb | 24 | 16 | 0 | 22 | 8% | 17 |
| Mar | 24 | 15 | 3 | 20 | 8% | 16 |
| Apr | 23 | 14 | 3 | 17 | 13% | 16 |
| May | 23 | 13 | 3 | **16** | 16% | 14 |
| Jun | 22 | 12 | 3 | 16 | 14% | 14 |
| Jul | 21 | 12 | 3 | 17 | 13% | 13 |
| Aug | **20** | **11** | 3 | 18 | 14% | 13 |
| Sep | 20 | 12 | 3 | 20 | 18% | 13 |
| Oct | 21 | 12 | 0 | 22 | 18% | 13 |
| Nov | 22 | 14 | 0 | 23 | 13% | 14 |
| Dec | 23 | 14 | 0 | **24** | 9% | 16 |

([Weather Spark, Lüderitz](https://weatherspark.com/y/78283/Average-Weather-in-L%C3%BCderitz-Namibia-Year-Round))

**Annual rainfall is about 35 mm.** For comparison, Belém takes more rain in an
average *three days* than Lüderitz takes in a year.

**Temperature is remarkably flat and remarkably cool** — maxima between 20 and
24 °C every month of the year, minima 11–16 °C. Weather-Atlas gives the range as
20.6 °C in August to 26.3 °C in March for maxima, and 13.3 °C to 19.6 °C for
minima, which is the same picture from a different series.
([Weather-Atlas, Lüderitz](https://www.weather-atlas.com/en/namibia/luderitz-climate) —
*note: this site returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch on 31 July 2026; figures are
via search indexing*)

**The wind is the story.** Mean wind speed never drops below 16 km/h and peaks
at 24 km/h in December and January — and those are *daily means*, which flatten
the afternoon. Lüderitz records **average wind speeds of over 40 km/h on summer
afternoons**. This is a place where a purpose-built speed-sailing canal produces
world records.

**Sea temperature 13–17 °C all year.** This is not a swimming coast.

### What to expect on a world-cruise call
Leg 7 of the v0.1.17 Volendam itinerary places Lüderitz between Cape Town and
Walvis Bay at roughly **days 101–117** of a 129-day voyage departing early
January — so **late April into May 2028**. That is the calmest part of the
Lüderitz year: **wind at its annual minimum (16–17 km/h mean), maxima around
23 °C, minima 13–14 °C, cloud rising to 16%, sea 14–16 °C.** It is also the
tail of the low-rain months, though 3 mm is a rounding error in any case.

**The practical read:** dress for a cool, bright, windy day rather than for
Africa. A windproof layer matters more than sunscreen, though you want both —
the sun at 26° S over a cold sea burns without feeling hot. Morning fog off the
Benguela is normal and usually clears.

## 8. The honest read — one port day in Lüderitz

**The shape of the day.** Anchor in Robert Harbour, tender to the Waterfront
jetty on the edge of town. Unlike Belém, **there is no transfer to lose** — the
town starts at the jetty. Kolmanskop is 10 km inland, twenty minutes or so each
way, and operators meet the pier.

**The governing constraint is not distance, it is opening hours**, and there are
two hard ones:

1. **Kolmanskop's last guided tour is 11:00**, and the site closes at 13:00 on
   Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays, 15:00 on weekdays.
2. **The Halifax Island penguin boat leaves at 08:00** and is back about 10:15.

If the ship's first tender is early, the penguin boat and the 11:00 Kolmanskop
tour can just about both happen. If it is not, they cannot, and the choice has
to be made in advance rather than discovered ashore.

**The strong recommendation.** Kolmanskop first, on the earliest tour you can
reach; then walk the town — Felsenkirche, the Bahnhof, the Art Nouveau and
Jugendstil houses along Bismarck Street, the Waterfront — and finish at Goerke
Haus after 14:00 if the ship is still alongside its anchorage. Shark Island is a
short walk over the causeway and should be visited knowing what it is.

**What is not feasible:** Bogenfels and the deep Sperrgebiet (permit and full-day
tour), the Fish River Canyon (hundreds of km), Sossusvlei, or anything that
implies Namibia's interior. Lüderitz is a town-and-ghost-town day.

**The one-line verdict.** Lüderitz is the rarest kind of port on a world cruise:
a small place where everything worth seeing is either at the jetty or twenty
minutes from it, wrapped around a genocide site that the town has not resolved
and a ghost town that closes before lunch.

## CORRECTIONS

1. **"Kolmanskop is a half-day with a local operator."** True about duration,
   misleading about timing — and the v0.1.17 Volendam pack currently says this.
   **Guided tours run only at 09:30 and 11:00 Monday–Saturday, and 10:00 on
   Sundays and public holidays; the site closes at 13:00 at weekends and on
   public holidays, 15:00 on weekdays.** It is a morning commitment tied to a
   fixed tour time, not a flexible half-day.
   ([Kolmanskuppe](https://kolmanskuppe.com/tours-prices/))

2. **"Lüderitz is too small a port for a big ship."** Wrong mechanism. The berth
   is **500 m long** — ample for a 237 m ship — and **8.75 m deep** on
   undredgeable bedrock. It is **draft**, not length, that keeps Volendam
   (8.1 m) at anchor. AIDAstella at 253 m and 71,304 GT berths there and draws
   only 7.3 m. ([Namport](https://www.namport.com.na/ports/welcome-to-the-port-of-luderitz/523/))

3. **"The town has been renamed ǃNamiǂNûs."** No. The **constituency** was
   renamed in August 2013; **the town is still officially Lüderitz**.
   ([ǃNamiǂNûs Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C7%83Nami%C7%82N%C3%BBs_Constituency))

4. **"Dias raised the cross at Lüderitz in 1487."** He *reached* Angra Pequena
   in 1487 outbound; the **padrão de São Tiago went up on the return leg in July
   1488**, and sources give either 24 or 25 July.
   ([Artefacts.co.za](https://www.artefacts.co.za/main/Buildings/bldgframes.php?bldgid=11407))

5. **"Namibia is visa-free for most Western tourists."** **Stale — reversed on
   1 April 2025.** A visa is now required of a long list of previously exempt
   nationalities: N$1,600 on arrival at designated entry points, or an eVisa in
   advance. And **no seaport appears on the published visa-on-arrival list.**
   ([Mission of Namibia](https://missionofnamibia.ch/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=31&Itemid=376))

6. **"Germany has paid reparations for the genocide."** No. The 2021 Joint
   Declaration offered **€1.1 billion in ex gratia development aid over 30 years
   while expressly rejecting legal responsibility**, was negotiated between the
   two governments without Ovaherero and Nama representation, is under judicial
   review in Namibia's High Court, and as of October 2025 Amnesty International
   states no reparations have been provided.
   ([Amnesty International](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/10/namibia-germany-must-deliver-reparatory-justice-for-its-brutal-colonial-past/))

7. **"Namibia is warm."** Not on this coast. Lüderitz maxima run **20–24 °C
   every month of the year** with sea temperatures of 13–17 °C, because of the
   Benguela Current. A world-cruise call in April–May wants a windproof layer.

  indicative and the cruise-day claim as unverified.

- **Current status of the Shark Island campsite.** One booking aggregator shows
