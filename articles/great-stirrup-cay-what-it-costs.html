Great Stirrup Cay: What It Actually Costs

Free to land, free to sit, free to eat. Then a ten percent tax on every tour and rental, photographed off the island’s own board.

by Ken Baker | September 5, 2026

Answer first: getting onto Great Stirrup Cay costs nothing. Norwegian describes the island as including "expansive beaches, complimentary dining, a resort-style pool, a kids’ splash pad", with lounge chairs and umbrellas "based upon availability". The paid layer is cabanas, the Great Tides Waterpark day pass, and excursions. And there is one line almost nobody warns you about, photographed off the island’s own price board: "For all tours and rental on Great Stirrup Cay, there will be a 10% Bahamian sales tax added to the advertised prices." The posted number is not the number.

What is included

Norwegian’s own island page lists the complimentary layer: the beaches, "numerous complimentary dining options", and the Great Life Lagoon, which it describes as "a 28,000 square-foot pool with multiple swim-up bars, a kids’ splash pad, and private cabanas". Lounge chairs and umbrellas are complimentary "based upon availability" — a qualification that means exactly what it sounds like on a full ship day.

Towels, lockers and life jackets are described as complimentary in the waterpark context. Read that carefully: those specific provisions are stated on the page about the waterpark day pass, not as a general island promise, and I am not going to widen a sentence past what it says.

What costs money

Three things, broadly.

Cabanas. Norwegian sells several tiers, on the beach and at the lagoon and at the waterpark, and describes them as "available for purchase". It does not publish prices on the island page — they appear inside the booking system, vary by sailing, and I did not have a live booking open to read them, so there are no cabana figures here.

The Great Tides Waterpark. Norwegian calls it "an upgraded experience available for an additional cost" with "Price varies by sailing and availability". That is the whole of what the line publishes; anyone quoting you a fixed waterpark price is quoting a sailing, not a policy.

Excursions and rentals. This is where the photographed board comes in, and it is the only part of this page where I can give you real numbers.

The zipline board, as posted

Photographed on the island on 27 August 2026. These are the posted prices on the day, not a quoted policy, and they will move:

  • Osprey — three lines that go over the beach — $99 adults, $79 kids
  • Seahawk — three lines that go over the ocean plus climbing — $99 adults, $79 kids
  • Island Combo — both tours combined — $139 adults, $119 kids

The ten percent nobody mentions

At the foot of that board, in text you would walk past: "For all tours and rental on Great Stirrup Cay, there will be a 10% Bahamian sales tax added to the advertised prices."

Two things follow from the wording. First, the advertised price is not the price — the $139 combo is $152.90, and a family of four doing the combo is paying roughly fifty-six dollars in tax they had not budgeted. Second, the sentence says tours and rental. By its own wording it reaches beyond the ziplines to rented equipment as well, so whatever you were pricing at face value on this island, add ten percent before you decide.

This is not a criticism of Norwegian. It is a Bahamian tax, it is disclosed, and the sign is posted where you buy. It simply is not in any brochure, any app screen, or any pre-cruise planner I have seen, and it is the difference between a budget that holds and one that does not.

The board that decides whether you can go at all

Next to the price board is a second sign that matters more than pricing for a good number of readers, because it decides availability rather than cost. As posted:

  • Age: "Flyers must be a minimum of 8 years of age. Flyers under the age of 18 must be accompanied by an adult."
  • Weight: "Minimum 50 lb / 23 kg with a maximum weight of 250 lb / 113 kg."
  • Height: "Flyers must be a minimum of 4 feet tall / 122 cm."
  • Dress: pants or shorts and a shirt. Footwear "must consist of a closed toe shoe or sandal", with heel straps required — the sign names Crocs and Keens — and "No slip-ons, flip flops or bare feet."
  • Physical: flyers "should be in good physical condition and able to climb a 20-foot ladder unassisted."
  • Not recommended for people with "balance problems, epilepsy, heart, back, neck or shoulder conditions or any other condition that may impair your safety."
  • Long hair must be tied back or bunned under the helmet. No alcohol, drugs or medication that might affect balance, concentration, coordination or judgment. Participants must be able to understand instructions, and safety equipment must fit as determined by the operator.

The one to read twice is the ladder. "Able to climb a 20-foot ladder unassisted" is a harder filter than the weight range, and it appears in no marketing anywhere. If you are planning around a bad knee, a replaced hip, a heart condition or a shoulder that will not go overhead, that sentence is the answer to your question, and you would otherwise find it out standing in line having already paid.

The 250-pound ceiling deserves the same flag. It is a real limit, it is checked, and finding it out at the platform is a bad way to spend a port morning.

The part that costs nothing and still costs you

The island had a working pier on 27 August 2026 — a temporary one, while the permanent two-ship pier is finished. That is good news and it is covered separately in what is changing at Great Stirrup Cay.

What the pier does not solve is the distance. It is a long walk from the ship to the island facilities. For a strong walker that is a pleasant few minutes in the sun. For someone using a chair, a walker, or simply not moving well in heat, it is the defining fact of the day, and no price list mentions it. I did not see a lift deployed. Plan the day around the walk, not around the map.

What I cannot price

Honestly: most of it. There is no published price for cabanas, for the waterpark day pass, for equipment rental, for food beyond the complimentary options, or for the other excursions on the island. Norwegian says the waterpark price "varies by sailing and availability", and cabana pricing behaves the same way. The zipline board above is the exception, and the only reason I can give you those three numbers is that I stood in front of the sign and photographed it.

So take this page for what it is: one verified corner of the island’s pricing, one tax line that changes every other number on it, one eligibility board that decides whether the money is even relevant to you, and an honest admission about the rest. Your cruise planner and the island’s own excursion desk are authoritative for your sailing.
