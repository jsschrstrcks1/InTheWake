> Made for the solo cruiser who has booked this sailing with Tina's group, or is thinking about it, and wants one plain read on the twelve nights: the ship, the eight stops, the canal, and what to check before you go.

This pack is about one cruise: **[Norwegian Sun](/ships/norwegian/norwegian-sun.html)**, Norwegian Cruise Line, sailing 12 nights round trip from **Miami, Monday March 1 to Saturday March 13, 2027**. ## Section 1 — Your Ship: Norwegian Sun {#section-1}

### Built in 2001, refurbished in 2024

[Norwegian Sun](/ships/norwegian/norwegian-sun.html) was built at the Lloyd Werft shipyard in Bremerhaven, Germany. NCL's ship page says she was built in 2001 and lists her as refurbished in 2024. She's a Sun-class ship, and her one NCL sister was Norwegian Sky.

Gross tonnage, 78,309 for her, isn't a weight. The International Maritime Organization says it "is a function of the moulded volume of all enclosed spaces of the ship." Divide it by NCL's guest count and you get about 42 gross tons per guest. If you want to see how she compares with other ships, the [Ship Size Atlas](/tools/ship-size-atlas.html) lines them up.

### What's on board

These come from NCL's "What's On Board" page for Norwegian Sun, read October 2026. Line-ups can change, so check the app.

- **Included restaurants:** Four Seasons and Seven Seas, the main dining rooms (Deck 5), the [Garden Café](/restaurants/ncl/garden-cafe.html) buffet and [The Great Outdoors](/restaurants/ncl/the-great-outdoors.html) (Deck 11), and the Sports Bar on Deck 11, which NCL lists as a 24-hour eatery.
- **Specialty restaurants (extra):** [Cagney's Steakhouse](/restaurants/ncl/cagneys-steakhouse.html), [Teppanyaki](/restaurants/ncl/teppanyaki.html), [Le Bistro](/restaurants/ncl/le-bistro.html), [Los Lobos](/restaurants/ncl/los-lobos.html) and the Sushi Bar (Deck 12), [La Cucina](/restaurants/ncl/la-cucina.html) (Deck 5), and [Moderno Churrascaria](/restaurants/ncl/moderno-churrascaria.html) (Deck 11). NCL gives a cover charge of $40 to $60 a person, and its gratuity FAQ adds a 20% charge to specialty dining.
- **Bars:** among others, the [Atrium Bar](/restaurants/ncl/atrium-bar.html) (Deck 5), [Bliss Ultra Lounge](/restaurants/ncl/bliss-ultra-lounge.html) and Windjammer (Deck 6), Magnum's Champagne & Wine Bar (Deck 7), the Spinnaker Lounge and Champs Bar (Deck 12), and Topsiders (Deck 11).
- **Shows** are in the Stardust Theater, which NCL lists on Decks 6 and 7.
- **Pool, spa and fitness:** the Oasis Pool, the Mandara Spa and Salon, and the Pulse Fitness Center, all on Deck 11. A jogging and walking track is on Deck 6.
- **Quiet corners:** the East Indies Library (Deck 6) and the Chapel (Deck 12), which NCL says "also functions as a meeting room."

### What Sun doesn't have

- **No Studio cabins and no Studio Lounge.** NCL's deck plans for Sun list Solo Inside, Solo Oceanview and Solo Balcony cabins, and no Studios. NCL's solo page says Studio "availability and Studio Lounge accessibility varies by ship." Tina's group is your solo lounge on this one.
- **No self-service laundry.** NCL's packing page says "laundry is not self-service." Fill in the order form in your cabin and your steward collects it.

### Cabin choice: sailing solo

NCL's solo page says Solo Inside, Oceanview and Balcony cabins are "available fleetwide," and that "double occupancy rates don't apply" in them. Tina's flyer prices the group's solo cabins at **$3,039 inside, $3,419 ocean view and $6,109 balcony**, with a $125 deposit.

Prices move, though. When Tina shared the flyer she wrote: "The Ocean view room is currently cheaper than the inside. Extra $150 off as well using a coupon." So ask her what each cabin costs on the day you book. The [stateroom check](/stateroom-check.html) can tell you about a specific cabin number on Sun.

### Free at Sea

For bookings made from November 5, 2025, NCL's package is called **Free at Sea**. Per NCL's Free at Sea page, read October 2026, it includes:

- An **open bar** for guests 21 and over, on board and at Great Stirrup Cay
- **Specialty dining**: four meals on a cruise of nine nights or more, so four on this one
- **Wi-Fi**: 150 minutes
- A **$50 shore excursion credit** at each port, for the first guest in the cabin

The open bar isn't free of charges. NCL's own Free at Sea flyer (December 2025) says: "Gratuities are $28.50/per person/per day for all Guests 21+. Please note this is the pre-cruise price. The onboard price will vary." For twelve nights alone, that's **$342**, on top of the daily service charge. A group booking can bundle things differently, so **ask Tina what your fare includes.** The [drink calculator](/drink-calculator.html?line=ncl) uses the same $28.50.

### Connectivity

NCL's Wi-Fi runs on Starlink. Free at Sea includes 150 minutes. Beyond that, NCL's Free at Sea page lists the **Voyage Wi-Fi Pass at $29.99** and the **Streaming Voyage Wi-Fi Pass at $39.99**, each per cabin per day, plus $14.99 a day for each extra device (October 2026). The [internet at sea guide](/internet-at-sea.html) explains how ship Wi-Fi works.

## Section 2 — Day by Day {#section-2}

Each port day has the times from NCL's schedule, the all-aboard time, and a few things to know. The port pages on this site go deeper.

### Day 1 — Monday, March 1 — Miami (embarkation)

**Ship leaves:** 4:00 PM. **Be on board by 3:00 PM.**

- **The terminal.** PortMiami lists Terminal B, "The Pearl of Miami," as NCL's terminal, and Terminal C as one that "serves ships from several cruise lines." **Your eDocs name yours.**
- **Parking.** PortMiami lists Garage B, 1756 N. Cruise Blvd., at $35 a day (effective January 1, 2025). If your eDocs say Terminal C, Garage C is at 1648 N. Cruise Blvd., at $28 a day. Debit cards aren't accepted.
- **Arrival time.** NCL asks guests who've checked in online to arrive at the terminal no later than one hour before their chosen boarding time.
- **Carry-on.** Keep your passport, medications, a swimsuit and a change of clothes with you. NCL asks you to carry a small bag with what you need on the first day "while your luggage is delivered" to your cabin.
- **Sailaway with the group.** Tina will tell the group where to meet.

More on the [Miami port page](/ports/miami.html).

### Day 2 — Tuesday, March 2 — Great Stirrup Cay, Bahamas {#day-great-stirrup-cay}

**In port:** 7:00 AM to 4:00 PM. **All aboard 3:00 PM.**

This is NCL's own island in the Berry Islands of the Bahamas.

- **The pier.** NCL says a multi-ship pier debuted in December 2025, and when it opened NCL said all ships visiting the island will benefit from it, with "guests no longer being required to tender from ship to shore" (quoted by Cruise Industry News). In September 2026 Cruise Industry News reported that "the new pier opened in August 2026." So expect to walk ashore, and **check the app** the day before.
- **What's free:** the beaches, loungers and umbrellas, and the Splash Harbor splash pad. The Free at Sea open bar applies on the island.
- **What costs extra:** the **Great Tides Waterpark** was "from $89" on NCL's excursion page in October 2026. Also cabanas, the zipline, and snorkel and paddleboard rentals.
- **Money.** NCL's ships use US dollars, charged to your shipboard account. Bring your key card.

More on the [Great Stirrup Cay port page](/ports/great-stirrup-cay.html).

### Day 3 — Wednesday, March 3 — At sea

The first sea day. Book your specialty dinners and any shore excursions you still want, find the library and the quiet corners, and meet the group.

### Day 4 — Thursday, March 4 — Cabo Rojo, Dominican Republic {#day-cabo-rojo}

**In port:** 9:00 AM to 3:30 PM. **All aboard 2:30 PM.**

Cabo Rojo is a new cruise port in **Pedernales Province**, in the far southwest of the Dominican Republic. The Dominican Port Authority says its first cruise ship arrived on January 4, 2024.

- **What's around it.** NCL's own page says the region is "sparsely populated" and that "there aren't any recommended shopping areas." Plan this day around an NCL Shore Excursion, or a quiet day near the port.
- **Bahía de las Águilas.** NCL calls it "the country's most beautiful beach" and describes it as five miles long. It sits in **Jaragua National Park**, which NCL notes is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.
- **Getting there.** The Dominican tourism ministry says you reach it "by an all-terrain vehicle, or by boat from the shores of Cabo Rojo." It also says the beach has "no hotels, shops, or restaurants" and no shade, so bring water and plenty of sun protection.
- **Money.** The Dominican peso. Ask before you pay in dollars.
- **Emergency number:** 911. The UK government notes that 911 "does not have full coverage across the country."

This site doesn't have a Cabo Rojo page yet, so this pack doesn't say more than NCL and the sources above do.

### Day 5 — Friday, March 5 — Aruba (Oranjestad) {#day-aruba}

**In port:** 11:00 AM to 7:00 PM. **All aboard 6:00 PM.**

A late-morning arrival, so you have lunch and the afternoon.

- **The terminal.** Aruba's tourism office says Main Street is "a ten-minute walk from the cruise terminal" and Surfside Beach a 15-minute walk, and that "all of Aruba's beaches are public and free."
- **Arikok National Park.** Aruba's tourism office says "nearly 20% of Aruba is a designated National Park," and that visitors "need to purchase a ticket" to enter. Travel guides say the park closes in mid-afternoon, so if you go, go straight from the ship.
- **Money.** Aruba's tourism office says the florin is the currency but "U.S. dollars are widely accepted," and some businesses won't take US $50 or $100 bills.
- **Emergency numbers:** 911 for ambulance and fire, **100 for police** (UK government travel advice).
- **Arrival card.** The written sources don't agree. Aruba's tourism site calls its online ED card "a mandatory requirement for all individuals... entering Aruba," but its own page for cruise visitors doesn't mention it. When I called at Aruba by cruise ship in 2023, we were exempt. In my experience, when a port does require paperwork, the cruise line handles it or tells you when it can't. Watch the NCL app.

### Day 6 — Saturday, March 6 — Curaçao (Willemstad) {#day-curacao}

**In port:** 7:00 AM to 5:00 PM. **All aboard 4:00 PM.**

A long day in a walkable old town.

- **The Queen Emma Bridge.** Curaçao's tourism site says it was built in 1888 and swings open to let ships into the harbor. While it's open, a small ferry carries people across free.
- **The pier.** The Curaçao Ports Authority says ships dock at the Mega Pier or the Otrobanda wharves on St. Anna Bay.
- **Money.** Curaçao's currency is now the **Caribbean guilder (XCG)**, which took over on March 31, 2025 (Central Bank of Curaçao and Sint Maarten).
- **Emergency numbers:** 911 for police and fire, **912 for ambulance** (UK government travel advice).

### Day 7 — Sunday, March 7 — At sea

The second sea day, and it's a Sunday. A good day for a slow breakfast, the spa, or a show.

### Day 8 — Monday, March 8 — Cartagena, Colombia {#day-cartagena}

**In port:** 6:00 AM to 2:00 PM. **All aboard 1:00 PM.**

An early start and an early finish. Eat breakfast before you go.

- **The dock.** NCL's page says the ship docks, at "Muelle 2 (Cartagena)."
- **The old town.** NCL says "the old walled city of Cartagena reflects a turbulent history of conquistadors and pirates," and suggests you "climb 'las murallas' for an unforgettable view of the harbor." More on our [Cartagena port page](/ports/cartagena.html).
- **Money.** The Colombian peso (NCL). Ask before you pay in dollars.
- **Emergency numbers:** **123 for police and ambulance, 119 for fire** (UK government travel advice).
- **Read this before you go ashore.** The US State Department rates Colombia **Level 3, "Reconsider travel"** (March 2026), "due to crime, terrorism, civil unrest, kidnapping, and natural disasters." Its Do Not Travel list names Arauca, Cauca (excluding Popayán), Valle del Cauca (excluding Cali), Norte de Santander, and the area near the Venezuela border. Cartagena isn't on that list. The same advisory says crimes including "drugging" happen "in some regions," and the UK government names street crime on "the Caribbean coast." An NCL Shore Excursion, or a walk with the group, is a sensible way to see the walled city. Keep your drink in sight.

### Day 9 — Tuesday, March 9 — Panama Canal (Gatun Lake), then Colón {#day-panama-canal}

**Canal:** 5:00 AM to 3:00 PM. **Colón:** 5:00 PM to 8:00 PM. **All aboard in Colón 7:00 PM.**

This is the day the cruise is named for. Set an alarm.

- **What the ship does.** It's a partial transit: Sun sails in through the Caribbean entrance, up into Gatun Lake, and later back out to Colón. The Panama Canal Authority says the locks "raise the ships from sea level ... to the level of Gatun Lake (26 meters above sea level)." That's about 85 feet. The water that lifts you comes from Gatun Lake itself, "by gravity," the Authority says.
- **Which locks.** The Authority lists the original Gatún Locks on the Atlantic side and the newer Agua Clara Locks, opened June 26, 2016. NCL doesn't say which Sun will use, so watch from the deck.
- **Getting off at Gatun Lake.** NCL's page says "only booked shore excursion guests can disembark at Gatun Lake," by tender boat, and that they travel to Colón and rejoin the ship there. **If you don't book one, you stay aboard for the lake.** That's not a loss. The open decks are the show.
- **Colón in the evening.** NCL says the ship docks at the "Colón 2000 Cruise Terminal," and that "everyone is welcome to explore" there. It's a short evening stop. The UK government lists "the city of Colón" among places to take care against mugging, and the State Department rates Panama **Level 2, "exercise increased caution"** (August 2026), noting pickpocketing and purse snatching "in tourist areas." Stay near the terminal, or go with an NCL Shore Excursion.
- **Money.** NCL lists the Panamanian balboa and says "US Dollars are widely accepted."
- **Emergency numbers:** **911** for ambulance, fire and police (UK government travel advice).

More on our [Panama Canal](/ports/panama-canal.html), [Gatun Lake](/ports/gatun-lake.html) and [Colón](/ports/colon.html) pages.

### Day 10 — Wednesday, March 10 — Puerto Limón, Costa Rica {#day-puerto-limon}

**In port:** 6:45 AM to 1:30 PM. **All aboard 12:30 PM.**

The shortest port day of the trip. A morning, not a day.

- **The dock.** NCL's page says the ship docks, at "Limon Port." It adds that "many guests have reported seeing peaceful sloths lumbering in the trees right in port," so look up as you walk off.
- **Nearby.** Costa Rica's tourism board says Limón is a port with a "large influx of cruises almost year round," and points to **Cahuita National Park**, with "live coral reefs (35 species of coral)." Neither it nor NCL gives a travel time from the pier, so an NCL Shore Excursion keeps you on schedule.
- **Money.** The Costa Rican colón (Central Bank of Costa Rica).
- **Emergency number:** **911** for ambulance, fire and police (UK government travel advice).
- **Travel advisory.** The State Department rates Costa Rica **Level 2, "exercise increased caution"** (April 2026), and says "petty crime is common throughout Costa Rica."

More on our [Puerto Limón port page](/ports/puerto-limon.html).

### Days 11 and 12 — Thursday and Friday, March 11 and 12 — At sea

Two sea days to finish. Use one for rest and one for packing. Check your onboard account in the app and settle anything you want to dispute.

### Day 13 — Saturday, March 13 — Miami (debarkation)

The ship is due in at 7:00 AM. Keep your passport, medications and a change of clothes with you. Don't book an early-morning flight.

## Section 4 — Packing List {#section-4}

This list fits **early March in the southern Caribbean and Central America**: hot days, showers likely at Gatun Lake, Colón and Puerto Limón, eight port calls and four sea days. The [packing lists](/packing-lists.html) page has more.

### Documents and money

- **Passport** (or the birth certificate and photo ID NCL allows)
- A copy of your passport, kept somewhere else
- Your insurance policy and its emergency number, printed
- Two cards, kept in different places
- Some small US bills for taxis and tips ashore

### Carry-on

- All medications, in their original bottles
- Phone, charger, power bank
- Refillable water bottle
- Swimsuit and a change of clothes

### Daytime

- Swimsuits and a cover-up
- Light shirts and shorts
- A light sweater for the air conditioning
- **A light rain jacket or a small umbrella** for the canal day and Puerto Limón
- Sunglasses, hat, reef-safe sunscreen

### Evenings

NCL's restaurant dress code: "shirts and footwear required," and "swimwear should be saved for the pool." Pack whatever makes you comfortable at dinner within that.

### Footwear

- Walking shoes for Willemstad and Cartagena's old town
- Water shoes for the beaches
- Sandals

### Wine

NCL lets you carry on bottles of wine and champagne, but not box wine or other alcohol. Its corkage fee is $15 per 750 ml bottle, waived if you have a drink package.

## Section 7 — Weather and Seasickness {#section-7}

### Weather

Below are typical figures for March 1 to 13, worked out from five years of weather records (2020 to 2024) for each stop. They're averages, not a forecast. The companion app shows a real forecast as the dates get close.

| Stop | Typical high | Typical low | Days with rain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Miami | 80°F | 68°F | 29% |
| Great Stirrup Cay | 76°F | 73°F | 29% |
| Cabo Rojo | 87°F | 72°F | 37% |
| Aruba | 84°F | 77°F | 31% |
| Curaçao | 85°F | 77°F | 38% |
| Cartagena | 91°F | 76°F | 15% |
| Gatun Lake | 84°F | 77°F | 54% |
| Colón | 83°F | 77°F | 52% |
| Puerto Limón | 83°F | 72°F | 78% |

"Days with rain" counts any day with more than 1 mm (about 0.04 inch) of rain at that point, so a short shower counts the same as a wet day. Read it as how often you might see rain, not how often a day is spoiled. Cartagena has the hottest afternoons and the fewest showers; Puerto Limón saw rain on most of these days. Bring the rain jacket on Day 9 and Day 10.

These come from Open-Meteo's historical weather records, at the grid point the records use for each stop. For small islands that point can sit over the water, which keeps the night-time lows close to the highs.

**Hurricanes.** NOAA's National Hurricane Center says the Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. March is outside it.

### Seasickness

Nobody can promise how your body will feel at sea. If you're prone to seasickness:

- **Think about where your cabin is.** A ship pitches and rolls around its middle, so a cabin low and near the middle moves the least.
- **Ask your doctor or pharmacist** about meclizine, dimenhydrinate or a prescription scopolamine patch, which the CDC names, and try anything new at home first.
- **Ginger and acupressure bands:** the CDC says ginger has "only weak and contradictory supporting evidence," and that bands are "no more effective than placebo" in laboratory trials.
- **Fresh air and a view of the horizon:** the CDC says a visible horizon gives "some protection" on ships.
- **The ship's medical center** can treat seasickness. It bills you. Medicare's own guide says it "doesn't cover health care services you get when the ship is more than 6 hours away from a U.S. port," so check what your plan covers.

### Phones

- **At sea:** use the ship's Wi-Fi, and turn off cellular data so you don't pay for the ship's network.

### Money

- **Aboard:** US dollars, on your shipboard account (NCL).
- **Cabo Rojo:** the Dominican peso.
- **Aruba:** the Aruban florin; US dollars "widely accepted" (Aruba's tourism office).
- **Curaçao:** the Caribbean guilder, since March 31, 2025.
- **Cartagena:** the Colombian peso.
- **Panama:** the balboa; NCL says US dollars are "widely accepted."
- **Puerto Limón:** the Costa Rican colón.

### Travel advisories (US State Department)

Read October 2026:

- **The Bahamas:** Level 2, "exercise increased caution" (July 2026).
- **Dominican Republic:** Level 2, "exercise increased caution" (June 2025).
- **Aruba:** Level 1, "exercise normal precautions" (August 2026).
- **Curaçao:** Level 1, "exercise normal precautions" (September 2026).
- **Colombia:** Level 3, "reconsider travel" (March 2026). Cartagena isn't in its Do Not Travel areas. See [Day 8](#day-cartagena).
- **Panama:** Level 2, "exercise increased caution" (August 2026).
- **Costa Rica:** Level 2, "exercise increased caution" (April 2026).

These advisories describe the world as it stood in October 2026, not as it will stand the week you sail. They change in both directions: a level can rise or fall before March. Look again in the week before you go. The companion app's Alerts tab shows the current level for each.

## Section 9 — The Solo Cruise Rhythm {#section-9}

Twelve nights alone at sea has a rhythm, and you get to set it.

- **Pick one anchor a day.** A port walk, a show, a dinner with the group. The rest can stay loose.
- **Eat where it's easy.** The Garden Café buffet, and the Sports Bar, which NCL lists as a 24-hour eatery, let you eat on your own schedule.
- **Use the sea days.** Day 3, Day 7, Day 11 and Day 12 are yours: the spa, a book, a long lunch.
